# Interface

Every screen and what it does. Layout, motion and controls are in the [Style guide](style-guide.md); viewfinders and rolls are in [Camera](camera.md). Code: `r1_funcore::ui`, one file per screen in `ui/screen/` ([r1metal documents](../overview.md#funcore-r1-funcore)).

The interface has six themes (`Theme` in `gfx::theme`, each a `Layout` in `ui/layout/` whose `Look` holds its style values): Stage shows one large icon at a time on stages; Dial rolls every list through a band in the middle of the screen; Deck lays everything out as cards on a board; Terminal builds menus from text; Companion has the robot lead with suggestions; Rail keeps a level of icons at the left of every screen and shows beside it at once what the line it rests on holds. A new device uses Dial.

## Screen tree

All themes present the same screen tree (`TREE` in `ui/screen/tree.rs`), walked the same way in each (`Tree`): the theme shows each level as a stage of icons, as a menu, or in Rail a level whose lines each open something as its rail (`Shape`). Each node is shown always, only once unlocked, only while the R1 is linked to Signal, or never (`Presence`); what each level shows is reckoned in one place from both facts (`Ui::reveal`), which the unlock and the link's change go through, a line that stays standing on its node. A node marked extra (`extra`) is shown only in firmware built with `r1 build --enable-extras` (feature `extras`), and never otherwise.

| `SIGNAL` | no stage; opens Signal's conversations, or the link window while the R1 is not linked |

| `CAMERA` | `PHOTO`, `VIDEO`, `PICTURES`, `VIDEOS` |

**Stage.** In Stage the current icon sits in the middle of the screen, its name and subtitle under it. The clock (local time over weekday and day of month) is in the bottom-left corner, the battery in the bottom-right, and dots down the right edge mark the stage's places. On the top-level stage the `CHAT` place shows the robot over `HOLD TO TALK`. Stages wrap around, and a stage opens on its first icon. Clicking a branch shrinks its icon to the middle of the left edge and brings its stage in from below. Shrunk icons stack upward without overlapping: the newest sits at the middle of the left edge, with a small down-pointing arrowhead between each pair.

## Home and the menus

In the other five themes each stage of the tree is a menu, a list of lines (`ui/screen/home.rs`). Home is laid out per theme (`Look.home`, `Layout::home_grid`): a page holds as many lines as fit and the rest go to the next page; Dial rolls through all of them without pages. Deck's home has a top line (time, day, the network's mark while connected, battery) above the conversation card (the robot, its name, `HOLD TO TALK`, and as many lines of its last reply as fit) and a board of tiles, each an icon above its name. The card stands on every page with the robot on it; the pages turn under it, and a page's tiles fill the room below the card: one alone takes all of it, two or three stand one under another, more two across. Every list in Deck fills its room the same way ([Style guide](style-guide.md#8-grids-and-lists)). In Rail every level is one of two kinds. A level whose lines each open something, a screen or a level (home, `CAMERA`, `MIC`, `SETTINGS`, `FEATURES`), stands on the rail once entered: a column of icons at the left, the battery beside the network's mark over the bell, and the time, under them. A level whose lines set a value or tell (`ROBOT`, `SIGNAL`, `NOTES`, `NEWS`, `WEB`, `BELL` under `FEATURES`, `TOOLS`, `MOBILE DATA`, `USB DRIVE`) stands as lines in the pane. The pane always holds what the rail's line opens: while the rail turns, the line's icon large; once it rests, the screen (`CHAT`'s conversation, `SIGNAL`'s conversations or link window, `NEWS`, `WEB`, `BELL`'s list, `POKEDEX`, `WIFI`'s networks, `BRIGHTNESS`, `NOTES`, `PICTURES`) or the level's lines (`CAMERA`'s, `FEATURES`'), with the wheel still on the rail and nothing marked in the pane; the viewfinders open on a click alone. A click gives the wheel to the pane, and a level that stands on the rail takes the rail's place, the pane following its lines; back gives the wheel to the rail, and back on the rail puts its lines in the pane again, the level before it back on the rail. A branch in the pane's lines (`TOOLS`) opens in the pane. A hold beside the Pokédex, the browser or the bell's list in the pane records for it as the button held on it does, that screen taking the wheel (`Ui::talk_screen`); the conversation a hold or a photo for the robot opens over what shows stands the rail on `CHAT`, and back returns to what showed, the rail on the line it stood on. The robot sits in the bar, at home too ([Style guide](style-guide.md#12-themes)).

**Subtitles.** Every line shows its node's icon, its name, a subtitle and a chevron where it opens something; a setting's line, `FORGET` and `UNLINK` act in place and have none. Rail's pane shows a line's name and subtitle alone. One function writes the subtitle for all themes (`write_node_word`); Stage shows the same text under the middle icon.

**Suggestions (Companion).** Companion's home lists `PHOTO`, `PICTURES` and `NEWS` (`SUGGESTED`), then `EVERYTHING`, which opens the whole tree, as rows like every list of Companion: an icon in the mark colour before the name, on no card. A suggestion has no subtitle.

**Uses.** Every run of a menu action, in every theme, whether started by hand on the stages, a menu or a suggestion, or by the conversation (`Ui::run_action`), and in Rail a screen the pane opened once a click gives it the wheel (`Ui::enter_rail_screen`), is kept by its word (`Use::to_word`): `chat`, `signal`, `calls`, `photo`, `video`, `pictures`, `videos`, `notes`, `translator`, `news`, `rundowns`, `web`, `pokedex`, `wifi`, `brightness`, `volume`, `themes`, `colours`, `clock`, `bell`, `passphrase`, `pin`, `tunnel`, `cost`, `power`, `about`, `memory`, `days`, `forget`, `debug`, `robot`. The `settings` thread appends it with its UTC second to `.r1metal/analytics/uses`, whatever the logging ([Storage](../firmware/storage.md)).

Above the suggestions the robot speaks at most two lines: a greeting by hour (`Good morning.` from 05:00, `Good afternoon.` from 12:00, `Good evening.` from 18:00, `Good night.` from 22:00, `Hello.` while the time is unknown), then, where they apply, `I am low, at 12%. A cable soon?`, `Anna wrote.` or `3 people wrote.` while Signal messages wait unread, `Today's news is ready.`, `I keep 3 notes.` (`I keep a note.`), and `I am at 64%.` or `I am on the cable, at 64%.`. A sentence that does not fit is dropped; a full greeting reads `Good afternoon. Today's news is ready. I keep 3 notes. I am at 64%.`

**Menu bars.** Behind home, a menu's bar shows the name of the line that opened it. Under the name: the selected line's position (`3 of 9`); in Deck the selected tile's name; in Rail, home included, the name of the level whose lines show in the pane, or of the line the rail turns on or that opens no screen there, over that line's subtitle; in Companion a sentence: `All is set. WiFi: Upstairs.` or `All is set. No WiFi known.` on `SETTINGS`, `Everything I can do.` on `EVERYTHING`, on a menu of four lines or fewer as many of its names as fit (`Photo, video or more?`), otherwise the selected line's position. A left swipe returns to the parent menu with the same line selected.

## The passphrase

Everything the partition keeps of the owner is encrypted under one key, which the passphrase opens ([Storage](../firmware/storage.md#the-safe)). Where the device has a data partition, the passphrase window (`ui/screen/safe.rs`) rises from below over home as the interface starts, before the settings can be read: in the default look, Dial with palette C, the robot in the bar. Until the safe is open the window alone takes input (`Ui::handle_safe`): a finger on the keys types, the wheel turns through the keys' layers, a click ends the passphrase as the ✓ key does; swipes, the robot, holding the button and every other screen do nothing. It is left only forward: once the safe opens it falls away onto home, and a device without an OpenAI key gets the key window next.

The bar's name is `PASSPHRASE` until the partition tells of the safe, then `PASSPHRASE NOT SET` while one is chosen for a safe not made, or `TYPE PASSPHRASE` while the made safe's is typed. Under it the window says what it is at:

| Word | When |
|---|---|
| `STARTING`, `FORMATTING` | the partition is brought up; nothing to type |
| `6 TO 12 CHARACTERS` | a passphrase of 6 to 12 characters is chosen, any characters of the keys (`safe::MIN_LENGTH`, `MAX_LENGTH`) |
| `TYPE IT AGAIN` | the chosen one is typed again; a different one says `NOT THE SAME` and the choosing begins anew |
| nothing | the made safe's passphrase is typed |
| `OPENING` | the made safe's passphrase is tried |
| `ENCRYPTING` | the safe is made with the passphrase chosen and what `.r1metal` held encrypted into it; and, the safe open, while photos, videos, notes, editions and the conversation that an earlier firmware or a restore left plain are moved under its key, which takes as long as they are large (`Progress::Encrypting`): nothing to type, and the system does not suspend meanwhile |
| `WRONG PASSPHRASE`, `NOT OPENED`, `NOT ENCRYPTED` | the last one was wrong, or the safe could not be opened or made: the keys come back |
| `POWERING OFF` | the third wrong passphrase of the start: the device powers off (`safe::ATTEMPTS`) |
| `OPEN` | on its way out |

The window is laid out as the password's keyboard is ([WiFi](#wifi)): the passphrase under the bar in the large writing, a dot a character until the eye at the right end of its line shows it, and the keys every keyboard has at the foot. As on every keyboard, a finger on the keys is logged without its place. Too short, the ✓ key is dim and refuses; past 12 characters a key types nothing.

**Changing it.** `PASSPHRASE` in the settings raises the same window over the menu, on the battery as on a cable (`Ui::change_passphrase`): `TYPE PASSPHRASE` for the one in use, then `NEW PASSPHRASE` over `6 TO 12 CHARACTERS` and `TYPE IT AGAIN`. Until the new one is typed again, a swipe back or home above the keys lets the window fall onto the menu. Then the bar says `CHECKING` while the storage thread opens the safe's key with the one in use and seals it under the new one ([Storage](../firmware/storage.md#the-safe)). A wrong one in use says `WRONG PASSPHRASE`, a change that could not be made `NOT CHANGED`; the robot slumps and the window asks for the one in use again, and no wrong one here counts toward the three of a start. A change made says `PASSPHRASE CHANGED` as the window falls onto the menu, the robot nodding (`Ui::take_safe_change`). Nothing the safe holds is copied, and the app runs on.

## The OpenAI key

All paid services use the owner's OpenAI key ([OpenAI](openai.md#the-key)). While no key is stored, a window over home asks for one (`ui/screen/keying.rs`, the window that takes by a scanned code what the device keeps: the key here, a tunnel's config under [Mobile data](#mobile-data)). Like the passphrase window it takes the whole screen (`ui/view/window.rs`), laid out as a screen with its bar, and rises from below over the screen it stands over, which shows under it only while it rises or falls and takes no input. It rises as soon as the settings are loaded, no key is found and the passphrase window is gone, over home whatever was showing. Until a key is stored the window cannot be dismissed: a left swipe on its lines does nothing, a swipe down, from the window or from a screen it opened, brings it back, and what the conversation asks opens no screen over it (`Ui::is_key_held`). A button held while the window rises is let go: its release clicks nothing in the window.

The window's bar says `OPENAI KEY` over its state (`NONE YET`, `KEPT`, `SCANNING`, `CHECKING`, `KEY SET`) or the reason the last attempt failed. A failure stays until a click, a wheel turn on the lines, or the next code read. In Companion the robot says the state instead: `I need an OpenAI key.`, `My OpenAI key works.`, `Show me the code.`, `I ask OpenAI.`, `The key works.`. The robot sits at the right end of the bar and looks at the selected line or the viewfinder; touching it does nothing here. The lines are laid out as every list of the theme is.

| Line | Subtitle | Click |
|---|---|---|
| `SCAN QR CODE` | `MADE BY r1 key --qr` | opens the window's viewfinder |
| `WIFI` | the network chosen last, or `NONE KNOWN` | opens the network list ([WiFi](#wifi)); a left swipe there returns to the window, and while no key is stored so does a swipe down |

**Viewfinder** (`SCANNING`). Shows the photo viewfinder's picture in the largest square below the window's bar: 464x464, 382x382 in Rail's pane (`Ui::scan_side`). It starts on the camera the viewfinders last used (rear after boot); the wheel switches between rear and front. A click or a left swipe returns to the lines. Every frame goes to the QR scanner ([Camera](../firmware/camera.md#scanning)).

| Scanned code | Result |
|---|---|
| not a key (a key is `sk-` followed by letters, digits, `-` and `_`) | `NOT A KEY`; the robot shakes its head; the same code is ignored until a different one is read |
| a key | `CHECKING`: the camera stops, its last frame stays, and the robot thinks while OpenAI is asked over the device's network |

| Check result | Window |
|---|---|
| key works and is stored | `KEY SET`; the robot nods; one line, `DONE` over `THE KEY WORKS`; a click on it or a left swipe drops the window onto the screen under it |
| refused | `KEY REFUSED`; the robot slumps; back to the viewfinder; the same code is ignored until a different one is read |
| no network, no answer, or the key works but could not be stored | `NO NETWORK`, `NO ANSWER`, `NOT KEPT`; the robot slumps; back to the lines; the same code may be read again |

A code is ignored only in the window that read it: a window that rises anew reads every code, and a key stored leaves none behind (`Keying.last_scan`).

A key stored another way while the window is open and not checking (`r1 key`, [Console](../firmware/console.md#verbs)) switches the window to `KEY SET` at once. If OpenAI later refuses the stored key ([OpenAI](openai.md#the-key)), the window comes back over home from any screen, its bar showing `KEY REFUSED`, and the robot slumps.

**Replacing the key.** `SET OPENAI API KEY`, the last line of the cost screen ([Cost](#cost)), opens the same window over that screen; its bar shows `KEPT` while a key is stored. With a key stored, a left swipe on the lines drops the window back onto the cost screen, and a swipe down goes home.

## Light and sleep

Until the stored settings are loaded, the backlight stays as the bootloader set it and the robot is hidden. Sleep is in [Sleep and clock](../firmware/sleep.md).

**Screen darkened during a task.** Some tasks need nobody watching (`Ui::is_unwatched` in `ui/screen/sleep.rs`): reading aloud while not paused, recording or playing a note, a translator session requested or running, a conversation turn in progress in voice mode on the chat screen, and an audio call on its way or talked in ([Calls](#calls)). During such a task the screen turns off 15 s (`DARK_UNDER_TASK_NS`) after the last touch, wheel step or button press, and the task continues. Speech in a translator session is not input. Off means backlight and panel off (`FBIOBLANK`), as in sleep, so touch and wheel stop as well and only the button turns the screen back on. That press clicks nothing, so a recording or session continues; held 382 ms (`HOLD_TO_TALK_NS`) it talks as a hold on that screen does, recorded from the press on ([Chat](chat.md#a-turn)). Nothing is drawn while dark (`r1_funcore::domain::app::light::Light`). Coming back, the screen is redrawn in full and the backlight comes on once that frame is up; it goes dark again 15 s after the press if the task still runs. Plugging in a cable turns the screen on. On a cable the screen never goes dark (`Ui::darken`) and the device never sleeps on its own ([Sleep and clock](../firmware/sleep.md#sleep)). When the task ends, pauses or its screen is left, the screen stays dark; the device sleeps 1 min (`IDLE_TO_SLEEP_NS`) after the last thing that kept it awake, and waking turns the screen on. Recording or playing a video counts as watched, so its screen stays on.

## Brightness and volume

- **Brightness** (`brightness_6`): the wheel sets the backlight in 10 steps from 10 % to 100 % of its range, one step per wheel report, shown as a row of 10 blocks under the icon. A block that lights grows from its centre slightly past full size and settles; one that goes out shrinks to nothing. The backlight follows at once. A click closes the screen. Default: 40 %.
- **Volume** (`volume_up`): two rows, one under the other. `VOLUME` is the speaker level for all sound but the interface's own sounds, in 10 steps of 3 dB from silence to the loudest, one block per step above silence; default 70 %, 9 dB below the loudest. `SOUNDS` (`notification_sound`) is the level the interface's own sounds play at, on the same scale: the robot's searching ([Chat](chat.md#searching-sound)), the bell's ring ([The bell](#the-bell)), which a call that rings plays too ([Calls](#calls)), and the notice (below); at 0 none of them plays; default 40 %. The wheel sets the row at full strength, the other stepping back to the dim interface colour, and a click hands the wheel the other row, as a click on the clock hands it the next field (`choose_next_channel` in `ui/screen/levels.rs`); the screen opens with the wheel on `VOLUME`. Sound already playing follows at once.
- At either end of the range the setting stays and the row the wheel sets swings. ## Clock

`schedule`. On opening, the screen asks the network for the time ([Sleep and clock](../firmware/sleep.md#clock)). Meanwhile the bar shows `ASKING` and the robot listens. Then for 1 s the bar shows the result (`CLOCK SET`, `CLOCK IS RIGHT`, `NO NETWORK`, `NO TIME`; `domain::clock::TimeAnswer`) and the robot nods or slumps.

The screen shows a scale of 39 ticks, one per UTC offset any time zone uses (`base::time::offset::OFFSETS`, `-11:00` to `+14:00`), drawn faint, with UTC's tick brighter. A mark sits on the current offset; it moves to the next with a slight overshoot and swings at either end. The time and the full date (`friday 16 october 2026`) at that offset are shown with it: in Stage and Rail the scale is under the icon and the time and date under the scale, in Rail its ticks a thin line apart so that all 39 fit the pane; in Deck, Terminal and Companion the time is over the date over the scale.

Dial has no scale. Its band rolls through `OFFSET`, `HOUR`, `MINUTE`, `DAY`, `MONTH` and `YEAR` as lines of a list (`List::Settings`), each with what it is set to at twice the size over the time and the full date, the part saying `NO TIME` while the time is not known.

A click moves the wheel to the next field: offset, hour, minute, day, month, year, then offset again (`choose_next_part` in `ui/screen/clock.rs`). In Dial that field rolls into the band. In the other themes the field being set is drawn at full strength: while the hour or minute is set, the rest of the time drops to the dim date colour; a day, month or year being set stands out at full strength in the dim date. The bar shows `CLOCK` over the offset (`UTC+02:00`) or the field (`HOUR`, `MINUTE`, `DAY`, `MONTH`, `YEAR`); in Companion `My clock is at UTC+02:00.` or `Setting the hour.`. Each wheel step changes the clocks at once by an hour, a minute (seconds reset to zero), a day, a month or a year. The day of month is kept where the month has it, otherwise the month's last day is used. Years range from 2026 to 2099 (`SETTABLE_YEARS` in `base::time::setting`). A left swipe closes the screen and stores the offset. Default: UTC.

## WiFi

**Network list.** Each opening runs a new listening pass: the radio scans passively and turns off ([Network](../firmware/network.md#scanning)). Meanwhile the bar shows `LISTENING`, the list is empty and the robot listens. Then the lines come in from below: six per page in Stage; four in Deck, each a full board row. Each line shows a network's name, a subtitle, and its signal as four blocks at the right: one for any network heard, one more per 10 dB above -80 dBm, all four above -60 dBm. A network heard from several access points is one line at its strongest; hidden networks are left out.

The bar shows the selected network's position (`3 of 17`); on the first line the count (`17 NETWORKS`, `1 NETWORK`, `NO NETWORKS`); the radio's state (`LISTENING`, `JOINING`, `RADIO FAILED`); or why a join failed. In Companion: `I hear 17 networks.`, `I hear one network.` or `I hear no network.`.

| Lines, in order | Subtitle |
|---|---|
| `LISTEN AGAIN`, not counted as a network | how many networks the last pass heard (`17 HEARD`) |
| known networks heard, the one chosen last first | `DEFAULT HERE` for the first, `KNOWN` for the others |
| other networks heard, strongest first | `OPEN`, `PASSWORD`, or, dim, `CANNOT JOIN` for a login, WEP, pre-RSN WPA or a name that is not valid text |
| known networks not heard, dim, no signal | `KNOWN, NOT HEARD` |

After a pass, a successful join or a forget, the first network is selected if there is one, otherwise the first line. Clicking a known network, heard or not, opens its page. An open network is joined at once. A network with a password opens the keyboard, then is joined. One that cannot be joined swings. `LISTEN AGAIN` clears the list and runs a new pass. A left swipe returns to the stage or menu; any pass or join is abandoned and the radio turns off.

**Known networks.** A network becomes known only after a successful join, with its typed password if it has one. Known networks are stored with their passwords, ordered by when each was last chosen; of those heard, the one chosen last is the default. A known network is identified by its name and whether it has a password: a network of the same name with the other security is a different network.

**Trial join.** A network is joined before anything about it is stored (`Ui::attempt`). Meanwhile the list is frozen, the bar shows `JOINING` and the robot listens; a click on a line swings it. On success the network becomes known and chosen last, the list is rebuilt with it first, and the robot nods. On failure nothing is stored: the line swings, the robot slumps, and until the wheel moves or a click the bar shows why: `NOT HEARD` (no access point heard when the join began), `WRONG PASSWORD`, `NOT LET IN` (refused, or no answer), `NO ADDRESS`, `RADIO FAILED`. Closing the list abandons a join. Sleep interrupts a pass or join; it runs again on wake.

**Known network page.** The bar shows `NETWORK` over its name (Companion: `I know Upstairs, and hear it.`). After a forget or a successful join the list is rebuilt from its first line; a left swipe returns to the list as it was.

| Line | Subtitle | Click |
|---|---|---|
| `MAKE DEFAULT` (`check_circle`) | `THE NETWORK TO JOIN HERE`; dim `NOT HEARD HERE` if the last pass did not hear it | trial-joins it and, on success, makes it the one chosen last; swings if not heard |
| `FORGET` (`delete`) | `THIS NETWORK AND ITS PASSWORD`, or `THIS NETWORK` | forgets it at once; the robot shakes its head |
| `FORGET ALL` (`delete_sweep`) | `3 KNOWN` | forgets every known network |

**Keyboard** (`ui/keys/keyboard.rs`, `ui/keys/mod.rs`). It sits at the bottom of the screen: 10 keys across, 4 rows, each key 48x78. Three rows of characters, with shift and delete beside the third; then the layer key, the space bar and the done key. Four layers: lowercase, uppercase, digits with common signs (`- / : ; ( ) $ & @ "` and `. , ? ! ' _ #`), and other signs (`[ ] { } < > ^ * + =`, `` % \ | ~ ` ``, and the same third row). Among letters shift toggles case; among digits and signs it switches between those two layers and is labelled `#+=` or `123`. The layer key is labelled `123` or `abc`. The wheel cycles through the four layers: up shows the next, which rises from below; down shows the previous, which drops from above. The button acts as the done key. A key is selected while the finger is down and typed on lift, so sliding corrects a slip; lifting off the keys types nothing. The character under the finger is shown at twice its size in a frame grown from its key and follows the finger. Delete removes a character as soon as the finger lands; held, it removes another after 0.4 s and then one every 0.1 s (`HOLD_NS`, `REPEAT_NS`). The text appears under the bar, 26 characters per line, with a cursor, a dot a character until the eye at the right end of its first line shows it; a touch on the eye hides it again, and every password begins hidden. The bar shows `PASSWORD` over the network's name (COMPANION: `The password of Upstairs?`). In Rail the text stands in the pane, 21 characters per line. The done key is dim while the text is not valid (a password has 8 to 63 characters); done on invalid text swings the text. Valid text starts the trial join and the list shows again. A left swipe above the keys returns to the list and discards the text. The robot watches the key under the finger.

## Chat

At home in every theme, a click on the conversation's place or line, or a touch on the robot, opens the conversation (`ui/screen/chat.rs`) and the robot moves to the bar; in Rail it shows in the pane once the rail rests on `CHAT`, and a click or a hold gives it the wheel. In the photo viewfinder a touch on the robot opens it with a photo for the robot, going back returning to the viewfinder ([Chat](chat.md#photos)). The bar shows the robot's name in capitals (`ROBOT` while it has none) over the conversation's state: `HOLD TO TALK`, `MUTED`, `LISTENING 0:04`, `TRANSCRIBING`, `THINKING`, `SEARCHING`, `READING` while it reads its reply out, as reading aloud does, `WAKING UP` before its first words, or the failure (`NO STORAGE`, `STORAGE FULL`, `NOT RECORDED`, `NOT HEARD`, `NO NETWORK`, `NO OPENAI KEY`, `LIMIT REACHED`, `REFUSED`, `NOT WRITTEN`, `NO ANSWER`), which stays until the next turn.

In Dial, in voice mode, the microphone disc is at the bottom, lit while the button is held. In Companion, in voice mode, the robot is drawn large above the conversation with its name or state under it and `HOLD THE BUTTON TO TALK` at the bottom; muted, it sits in the bar.

| On the conversation | Effect |
|---|---|
| button held, in voice mode | records while held, the bar counting `LISTENING 0:04`; the robot stops speaking; on release the recording is sent |
| wheel | four lines per step: down goes back through the days, up toward the newest; at either end the rows swing. At the oldest day shown, the day before is requested and inserted above, the visible rows staying in place. While scrolled back, new rows arriving below do not move the view and the robot looks down at the bottom |

## Web

`public`, after `NEWS` (`ui/screen/web.rs`). The voice browser: a request said or typed brings a page, which shows under the bar as text alone, a line a block, a heading and a list's item as every text shows them, and behind the words of each link its number in brackets, in bold (`late on Friday [1]`). A link's number, `back` and an address are resolved on the device, which fetches the page and reads it out of its HTML; anything else, and any page the device cannot read, is one step of the language model. While no page shows the room tells what to ask (`START`): a site, a topic or a question, on a page a link's number or what it is about, `back`, a click to type, and a touch on the robot to hear the page. Nothing is written to the data partition: a request said is recorded into a file in memory, and the pages stay in memory, the one that shows and those before it while their text together is at most 4 MiB (`net::fetch::MAX_BYTES`), the oldest going first (`Browser::add`). The page that showed last shows again whenever the screen opens, until the app exits; a new page unfolds over the one before, from its top. While a request is recorded the microphone's level stands at the top of the room as a row of blocks, as over the Pokédex's result: one layout of a page under the bar serves both screens (`screen::voice::PageId`, the `paged_*` functions of `ui/view/rooms.rs`). Once a page shows and nothing is in progress, it shows where the page is from, its site in capitals (`EN.WIKIPEDIA.ORG`) or the title of a page the model wrote, and while the page is read aloud `CONNECTING`, `READING` or `PAUSED`, or the reading's failure, as over a text ([Notes and texts](#notes-and-texts)). **Visit** (`r1_funcore::domain::web`; the threads `web` and `visit`, `crates/impshell/src/web/`). The network is held from the button's press on, through the key, and the connection to the speech model is made in that stay while the request is still said, as for the Pokédex; a typed request holds it through the key too (`Job::IS_KEYED`), so without a key nothing is fetched. Only the newest request is visited; closing the screen or sleep ends a recording and drops the requests not begun. What was asked is read on the device first, a closing `.`, `!` or `?` left off (`web::parse`):

| Request | Leads to |
|---|---|
| `back` or `go back` | the page before, which shows again without a fetch; where there is none the page that shows stays (`Browser::go_back`) |
| a link's number, in digits or as `one` to `twenty` or `first` to `twentieth`, alone or the one word left among `the`, `link`, `number`, `open`, `go`, `to`, `one`, `please`, `result` (`one` is the number where it stands alone), where the page that shows has a link of that number | that link's address |
| one word that is an address, under `https://`, `http://` or no scheme: a host of letters, digits, dots and dashes that ends in a dot and two letters or more (`example.org/news`) | that address, under `https` whatever it was written under |
| anything else that holds a word | the language model |

A visit then goes step by step, funcore saying each (`visit::Visit`, `Step`), until it comes to a page, to the page before or to a failure:

- **Fetch** (`SEARCHING`; `web::fetch`). A `GET` on a connection of its own, closed behind it, under the firmware's own name with `Accept: text/html, application/xhtml+xml;q=0.9, */*;q=0.5` and `Accept-Encoding: identity` (`web::HEADERS`). An answer that sends the asker on to another `https` address is followed, 3 times at the most (`net::fetch::MAX_HOPS`). A page is read from an answer of status 200, without content encoding, whose `Content-Type` is `text/html` or `application/xhtml+xml` or names none (`web::check_head`), of 4 MiB at the most that came within 30 s at its address, the limits a feed is read under too (`net::fetch::check_read`).
- **The page** (`web::read_document`). Its words are those of one element (`scope_of`): the block that holds more than half of the document's paragraph words in paragraphs of its own or of its sections, and more than half of all its words; else its first `main`; else its `article` where it has one alone; else its whole body. Left out wherever they stand: `script` and `style`; `noscript`, `template`, `svg`, `iframe`, `form`, `button`, `select`, `nav`, `footer`, `aside` and `dialog`; an element of the role `navigation`, `contentinfo`, `complementary`, `search` or `dialog`; what the page does not show, by `hidden`, `aria-hidden="true"` or a style of `display:none`; and a footnote's mark, what is raised (`sup`) around a link to a place on the page. A block is a line, a table's row too, a heading under `#`, `##` or `###` and a list's item under `-`, and the signs Markdown reads as its own stand behind a backslash, so the page's words stay words. A link with words that leads to an `https` address gets its number behind them, the number it has already where another link led there before; a link to a place on the page, of another scheme or without words gets none, but one without words that an `aria-label` names, as a link laid over a card, is the link of the line that ends in that label. The page stands under its first `h1` where its text holds one, else under a heading made of the document's first `h1`, its `title` or the host's name. A document that holds no words, as a page a script builds, is no page.
- **The model's step** (`THINKING`; `write::write_page`). A background request of the browser's model and effort (`MODEL` and `REASONING` under `FEATURES`, `WEB`; `gpt-6.1-terra` at `high` on a new device, [OpenAI](openai.md#language-models)) under `crates/funcore/assets/web/browse.txt`, the web search among its tools, told where the device is where the place service said so. For a request it is given the request's words and the page that shows as the screen has it, its address where the device fetched it and its text with the links' numbers, or that no page shows (`web::to_input`). It answers in one of four forms (`web::read_reply`): `BACK`; `LINK n`, a link of the page that shows; `OPEN https://...`, an address it knows for certain; or a page it writes in Markdown of what it found, `**[n]**` behind the words of each page worth opening and, under a last line `LINKS`, the address of each number; a number past the count of the page's marks names no link, and its address is left out. The device fetches the link or the address it named. What the device cannot read itself, a fetch that failed, a document of another kind or without words, an asker sent on a fourth time, or an address it cannot parse, has the model write the page at that address (`web::to_page_input`), and nothing but a page will do then: a visit takes two steps of the model at the most. A link's number the page lacks, and an answer that is no page where one was asked for or holds no text, fail the visit as `NO ANSWER` (`visit::NO_LINK`, `NO_PAGE`). A page the model wrote has no address, and the bar names it by its title.

A step of the model's is booked under `WEB` as a page, at its tokens and a cent for each web search it made (`summary::search_micros`), a step that came to nothing too, and a request said adds its writing down there and counts none; a typed request that the device resolves, fetches and reads itself costs nothing ([Cost](#cost)). The log tells of a page read its bytes, the characters and links read of it and the time (`web: a page of 48211 bytes read as 9120 characters with 64 links in 412 ms`), or why the device did not read one (`web: the page is not read by the device: status 403`), and each step of the model's as all background work is logged, by its characters, its time and its tokens (`openai: a step of the browser's taken in 2140 characters in 8312 ms, asked after 3 times`, [OpenAI](openai.md#models)); a fetch's connection logs its host, as every connection does (`net::to_web_line`). The robot listens while a request is said, thinks while it is written down and while the model takes its step, searches while the device fetches, nods at a page and slumps at a failure. It looks at the page, or at the key under the finger. A recording or a visit in progress keeps the device awake, and so does a reading aloud that is not paused.

## Pokédex

`catching_pokemon` from the older Material Icons, after `BELL`, in firmware built with `r1 build --enable-extras` (`ui/screen/pokedex.rs`). A name said or typed is looked up on PokéAPI in the language set, its artwork where the entry names it, and the result is shown under the bar: its artwork centred at the top and its text below. The last result shows again whenever the screen opens, until the app exits; a new result unfolds over the one before. While a name is recorded the microphone's level stands at the top of the room as a row of blocks, the blocks of the robot's conversation ([Chat](#chat)): the result scrolls to its top and moves down under the row, and back up as the recording ends (`Ui::take_paged_phase`, `Ui::paged_meter_height`), as the browser's page does ([Web](#web)).

The entry's text, its headings and labels included, is in that language, decimals after a comma in all but English; what PokéAPI has not written in it, as the Pokédex text of the newest Pokémon, comes in English.

The bar shows `POKEDEX` over the lookup's state: `HOLD TO TALK`, `TYPE A NAME` with the keys up, `LISTENING 0:03`, `TRANSCRIBING`, `SEARCHING`, or the failure (`NOT RECORDED`, `NOT HEARD`, `NO NETWORK`, `NO OPENAI KEY`, `REFUSED`, `NO ANSWER`), which stays until the next lookup. Once there is a result and nothing is in progress, it shows the result's number and name (`#0025 PIKACHU`). Terminal's status line shows `VOICE` or `KEYS`.

| button held, in voice mode | records while held, the bar counting `LISTENING 0:03` and the microphone's level showing at the top of the room; on release the speech model transcribes the name, prompted that it is a Pokémon's name in the language set (`pokedex::prompt`, [OpenAI](openai.md#models)), and the name is looked up; a recording too short to transcribe is discarded, and the bar shows `NOT HEARD`; beside Rail's rail it records from the press once the button is held 382 ms (`HOLD_TO_TALK_NS`), the Pokédex taking the wheel |

**Lookup** (`r1_funcore::domain::pokedex`). The network is held from the button press on, through the key, and the connection to the speech model is made in that stay while the name is still said ([OpenAI](openai.md#account)), so a spoken name is transcribed and sent without a wait for either; a recording still going after 15 s lets it go until the recording is handed over (`voice::NETWORK_HOLD_NS`). A typed name holds the network alone, and needs no key; a hold it took is not kept for a name said, which holds the network anew through the key. PokéAPI's GraphQL service (`https://graphql.pokeapi.co/v1beta2`, a POST of JSON for each query, 100 an hour from one address at the most) gives every Pokémon's names in the language set and in English, asked at the first lookup and again once the language changes, and kept in memory while the app runs (`pokedex::names_body`, `names::Names`). A name is compared as lowercase plain letters and digits, an accented letter as the one it is written over and words joined by a dash (`names::to_key`); the Pokémon is the one whose name lies the smallest edit distance from the name said or typed, the lowest number on a tie (`Names::nearest`). A second query gives its entry in the language, English filling in what the language lacks (`pokedex::entry_body`, `entry::Entry`): the number, category and types, the Pokédex text of the newest game, height and weight, base stats, abilities, the evolution chain and the moves by their letters, written as text under headings in the language. Its official artwork, a PNG, is composited over the screen background in a square whose side is the width of the area under the bar, inset by a gap on each side (464 px, 382 px in Rail's pane), divided by the golden ratio: 287 px, 236 px in Rail (`Ui::artwork_side`, [Media](../firmware/media.md#png)). An entry without artwork shows its text alone. A lookup goes step by step, funcore saying each and making its log's lines (`search::Search`, `Step`; the shell's `Searcher::search` does one effect a step): the names where those held are not of the language, the entry, the artwork. An answer that gives no names or holds no entry fails it as `NO ANSWER`; an artwork that is not fetched or drawn fails nothing, and its entry shows without (`ArtworkFault`). Every request asks for an unencoded response (`Accept-Encoding: identity`) on a connection closed after the response. A response is used only with status 200 and without content encoding (`net::fetch::check_plain`), of 4 MiB at the most that came within 30 s, the limits a feed and a page are read under (`net::fetch::check_read`); artwork is drawn only up to 1024 px wide and tall (`ARTWORK_MAX_PIXELS`), which is checked before its image data is inflated (`pokedex::read_artwork`). Only the newest requested name is looked up; closing the screen or sleep ends a recording and drops pending requests. The robot listens while a name is spoken, thinks while it is transcribed, searches while it is looked up, nods at a result and slumps at a failure. It looks at the result, or at the key under the finger. A recording or a lookup in progress keeps the device awake.

## The bell

`notifications`, after `WEB`. Any number of alarms, reminders and timers, set and cancelled by a request said on the bell's list or by the conversation (`set_reminder`, `cancel_reminder`, [Chat](chat.md#functions)), and cancelled by hand on an entry's page. Each is one entry of the bell (`bell::Entry`): when it rings, and a text of what it is for, which may be empty. Code: `r1_funcore::domain::bell`, `ui/screen/bell.rs`, `crates/impshell/src/bell/`.

| Entry | Set by | Rings |
|---|---|---|
| alarm | a time of day, no text | the next time the clock on screen shows that time |
| reminder | a day and a time of day, or a time of day alone, with a text | when the clock on screen shows that day and time |
| timer | a length, with or without a text | that long after it was set |

- **By the clock.** An alarm and a reminder are kept as seconds of the clock on screen (`When::Clock`), so they ring when it shows their time wherever its offset from UTC stands by then, after a change of daylight saving or of place too; a timer is kept as seconds of UTC (`When::Span`). Each rings once and is then set no longer. A time that is past, and one already set with the same text, is refused (`Bell::apply`): the conversation is told so, and after a request on the list the robot shakes its head.
- **Kept.** The bell has a file of its own, `.r1metal/interface/bell` ([Storage](../firmware/storage.md#layout)), which the settings' thread writes whenever the bell changes (`Ui::take_changed_bell`, `Keeper::keep_bell`): a restart keeps every entry, and one that fell due while the app did not run rings as it starts. The alarm and the timer an earlier firmware kept among the settings are read from there once and kept in the bell's file from then on (`Bell::read`).
- **Beside the battery.** Wherever the battery is drawn, the bell (`notifications`) stands left of it and of the network's mark while an alarm or a reminder is set, as tall as they are and a gap from them; left of the bell stands the time the timer that runs out first has left in the large writing, `4:59`, `1:04:59` past an hour, told anew every second while it shows (`Ui::update_bell`), nothing of it asleep. In Rail the bell stands right of the battery under the network's mark, or in its place where it does not show, and the time left takes the place of the time under them, in the small writing. Terminal's status line writes `ALARM` and the time left after the battery's level.
- **The list** (`BELL` at home, `Screen::Bell`). The first line takes a request: `NEW` over `HOLD AND SAY WHEN`, with the recording mark the notes' `RECORD` line has. Under it a line for each entry, the soonest first: its text, or `ALARM` or `TIMER` where it has none, over the day and time it rings at on the clock on screen (`friday 20 november 2026, 09:00`). The bar shows `BELL` over the request's state, `LISTENING 0:03`, `TRANSCRIBING`, `THINKING` or the failure (`NOT HEARD`, `NO NETWORK`, `NO OPENAI KEY`, `LIMIT REACHED`, `REFUSED`, `NO ANSWER`), else the selected entry's place among what is set (`2 of 3`), or `NONE SET` (Companion: `3 rings are set.`, `Nothing is set.`). A click on an entry's line opens its page (`Screen::BellEntry`): the bar shows `ALARM`, `REMINDER` or `TIMER` over its text, the lines `RINGS` over its day and time, `TEXT` over its text where it has one, and last `CANCEL` over `THIS ALARM`, `THIS REMINDER` or `THIS TIMER`, which cancels it at once, the robot shaking its head, and returns to the list. A list or a page that shows follows the bell as a request or the conversation changes it or an entry rings.
- **A request** (`Ui::press_request`, `Ui::pick_bell`). A hold on the list for 382 ms (`HOLD_TO_TALK_NS`), wherever the selection stands, records from the press for the bell and not for the robot (`Ui::talk_screen`): the selection goes to the first line, which then reads `SET` over `WHAT YOU SAY` with the microphone's level on it, as a note's `STOP` line has it (`Aside::Meter`), and letting go sends what was said. A click on the first line records until the next click, which sends, as the notes' `RECORD` and `STOP` do. Leaving the list, by a swipe, the ring's window or sleep, drops a recording (`Ui::leave_request_list`, `Ui::let_go_request`). On an entry's page a hold talks to the robot, as on every screen where a hold does nothing else. In Rail a hold beside the list in the pane records for the bell and gives it the wheel.
- **What a request becomes** (`r1_funcore::domain::bell::state`; the threads `bell` and `bell request`, `crates/impshell/src/bell/`). The job is a job said as the browser's is ([Chat](chat.md#a-turn)), wanted while the list shows and the device is awake (`Ui::is_bell_wanted`); only the newest request is read. With each request the interface hands it the bell as it stands and the clock's offset (`Standing`, `State::set_standing`). The speech model writes the request down, prompted that it is a request to set or cancel an alarm, a reminder or a timer (`bell::state::PROMPT`, [OpenAI](openai.md#models)); then the language model reads it once, in the background, at the bell's model and effort (`MODEL` and `REASONING` under `FEATURES`, `BELL`; `gpt-6.1-terra` at `high` on a new device, [OpenAI](openai.md#language-models)). Its input is what the device's clock shows, the listing of what is set and the request (`Bell::to_input`); its task is `crates/funcore/assets/bell/request.txt` with the declarations of `set_reminder` and `cancel_reminder` in it, the two the robot is given (`bell::FUNCTIONS`). It answers a line a call, the function's name, a space and its arguments as JSON, or `NOTHING` (`bell::parse_reply`); `bell::parse_call` reads the arguments for these calls and the robot's alike. The calls go to the main loop in the job's report, and the interface makes each as it makes the robot's (`Ui::take_bell_report`, `Ui::change_bell`): the notice plays where something changed, the robot nods, or shakes its head where the bell stands as before, and the selection goes to what was set. A reply that asks nothing fails the request as `NO ANSWER`. The robot listens while a request is said and thinks while it is written down and read. A recording or a request in progress keeps the device awake. A request is booked under `BELL` ([Cost](#cost)), and the log tells its reading as all background work is logged, by its characters, its time and its tokens (`openai: a request to the bell read in 61 characters in 1840 ms, asked after 1 times`, [OpenAI](openai.md#models)), never its words.
- **The ring** (`Ui::ring`). The main loop sets the clock's alarm for the soonest entry, which wakes a sleeping device ([Sleep and clock](../firmware/sleep.md#the-alarms-list)). What is due rings: the screen lights, the conversation's voice stops and a press is let go, and the bell's window rises over whatever shows (`Screen::Ring`, `ui/view/window.rs`), a line for each entry that is due, as the list writes it, so the screen says what the ring is for; an entry that falls due while the window stands joins its lines. The window does not rise over the passphrase's window, the window that scans, the key's or the tunnel's, or the window of a call that rings, and gives way to the key's where that takes the screen and to a call's that rings (`Ui::lower_ring`): what rang shows once that window has gone (`Ui::show_ring`). The bell beside the battery rings (`notifications_active`) in red, the window's bar says `RINGING`, and Terminal's status line writes `RINGING`. The ring's sound (`crates/funcore/assets/bell/ring.m4a`, `bell::ring_sound`) plays round and round at the level of the interface's sounds ([Brightness and volume](#brightness-and-volume)), and at 0 the bell rings without a sound (`Ui::does_ring_sound`); it takes the speaker over a sound played aside, as the robot's searching, and waits for one that has it, as a reading aloud. A press of the button silences it and does nothing else, nor does its release; unanswered the sound stops after 5 min (`bell::RING_S`). The window stays until a click or a swipe from the left lets it fall back onto the screen it rose over, onto home where the theme changed meanwhile, or a swipe down goes home: a ring nobody heard still shows at the next wake. While the sound rings the device does not sleep.

## Rundowns

`list_alt`, `RUNDOWNS` under `SETTINGS`, `FEATURES`, `NEWS` (`ui/screen/rundowns.rs`). The rundowns the daily edition makes, as the owner keeps them: added, changed and deleted by a request said on the list or handed over by the robot (`change_rundowns`; `open_rundowns` opens the list, [Chat](chat.md#functions)), switched and thinned by hand on a rundown's page. What a rundown is, its file and what a request becomes are in [News](news.md#the-rundowns-kept).

- **The list** (`Screen::Rundowns`). The first line takes a request: `NEW` over `OR A CHANGE: HOLD AND SAY IT`, with the recording mark the notes' `RECORD` line has. Under it a line for each rundown kept, in their order: the icon it goes by (`newspaper`, `finance`, or the one a request's model picked for it, [News](news.md#the-rundowns-kept)) and its name in capitals over `OFF`, or over the count of its feeds (`21 FEEDS`, `1 FEED`) and, where some gave no story when they were last read, how many (`21 FEEDS, 2 GAVE NO STORY`). Last, for each of the two a new device has that is kept no longer, `ADD` over `NEWS, AS A NEW DEVICE HAS IT` (`add`): a click keeps it again with the firmware's feeds and instructions, the robot nods, and the list stands on it. The bar shows `RUNDOWNS` over the request's state, `LISTENING 0:03`, `TRANSCRIBING`, `THINKING`, `SEARCHING` while the device reads the feeds a change adds, or the failure (`NOT HEARD`, `NO NETWORK`, `NO OPENAI KEY`, `LIMIT REACHED`, `REFUSED`, `NO ANSWER`, `NO FEED FOUND`), else the selected rundown's place among those kept (`2 of 3`), or `NONE KEPT` (Companion: `2 rundowns are made.`, `One rundown is made.`, `No rundown is made.`).
- **A request** (`Ui::press_request`, `Ui::pick_rundown`) is said as on the bell's list ([The bell](#the-bell)); both lists are a `screen::voice::RequestList` and keep the one `screen::voice::RequestLine`, the voice of a list whose first line takes a request. A hold on the list for 382 ms (`HOLD_TO_TALK_NS`), wherever the selection stands, records from the press for the rundowns and not for the robot (`Ui::talk_screen`): the selection goes to the first line, which then reads `ADD OR CHANGE` over `WHAT YOU SAY` with the microphone's level on it (`Aside::Meter`), and letting go sends what was said. A click on the first line records until the next click, which sends. Leaving the list, by a swipe, the ring's window or sleep, drops a recording (`Ui::leave_request_list`, `Ui::let_go_request`). On a rundown's page a hold talks to the robot. The robot's `change_rundowns` opens the list as a hand reaches it and hands it the request in words (`Act::Rundowns`, `Ui::ask_rundowns`).
- **What a request comes to** (`Ui::take_rundowns_report`, [News](news.md#requests)). A rundown, new or changed, is kept at once: the notice plays, the robot nods, and the list stands on it. A deletion shows on the rundown's page for the owner's click, the first at once and the next as each is deleted or kept. A deletion asked and neither made nor refused goes as its page is left (`Ui::lay_rundown_list`), and what a request comes to once the rundowns' screens are left is let go with them. At a request that fails the robot slumps.
- **A rundown's page** (`Screen::Rundown`), opened by a click on its line. The bar shows the rundown's name in capitals over the selected line's place (`2 of 24`). `MADE` over `WITH EVERY EDITION` or `OFF`: a click switches it. `ABOUT` over what the owner said of it, where they said anything. A line for each feed (`rss_feed`): its source in capitals (`ARSTECHNICA.COM`), over `GAVE NO STORY` where it gave none when it was last read. A click on a feed arms its line, `CLICK AGAIN TO REMOVE`, and the next click on it takes the feed away, the robot shaking its head; a rundown's last feed stays, and its line swings. Last `DELETE` over `THE RUNDOWN`, armed the same way (`CLICK AGAIN TO DELETE`): the second click deletes the rundown, the robot shaking its head, and returns to the list. The wheel and a click on another line disarm.
- **The page of a deletion a request asks** is the rundown's under two lines, in the place of its last: `DELETE` over `THE RUNDOWN` (`delete`) and `KEEP` over `IT AS IT IS` (`close`). The bar says `CLICK TO DELETE`. A click on the first line deletes the rundown and the robot nods; one on the second keeps it and the robot shakes its head. The page of the next deletion asked follows, or the list. Every other line swings: nothing is switched or removed there.

## Battery

The battery icon sits in the bottom-right corner of every stage and of Dial's and Companion's home; in Deck it is at the right end of home's top line; in Rail under the rail's icons on every screen the rail shows, as tall as two lines of the small writing, over the time in the small writing (`CornerStyle::Rail`); Terminal's status line shows the level as text. The icon shows the level; its colour shows what is done with the cell. While the device is in a network, the network's mark stands left of the battery, WiFi's (`wifi`) or, online over the modem, mobile data's (`signal_cellular_alt`), as the radio tells the way its stay goes (`radio::Report.link`), as tall as the battery and a gap from it, in every theme that draws the battery (`Corner::draw`), in Rail right of it, as tall as the small writing, over the bell, the drawing of the upper one beginning where the battery's does and of the lower one ending where it ends, the battery's body and the two centred on the rail as one; Terminal's status line writes `WIFI` or `MOBILE` after the level off the cable. Left of those stands the bell ([The bell](#the-bell)).

| State | Icon | Colour |
|---|---|---|
| off the cable | one of eight, one bar per seventh of a full battery: none up to 7 %, one bar up to 21 %, two up to 35 %, three up to 50 %, four up to 64 %, five up to 78 %, six up to 92 %, full from 93 % | interface colour; red below one bar's worth (14 % and less, `hw::power::battery::is_low`), and the robot looks worried |
| on a cable, charging | one of seven with the charging mark, chosen by the level in its name: the 20 up to 25 %, the 30 up to 40 %, the 50 up to 55 %, the 60 up to 70 %, the 80 up to 85 %, the 90 up to 95 %, full from 96 % (`battery_charging_full`) | interface colour |
| on a cable, held | the same seven: the 80 at a computer port's ceiling, full on a charger | green |
| level not known yet (before the first full charge) | `battery_unknown` off the cable, `battery_status_good` on one | interface colour |

Each icon covers the levels closer to its own than to its neighbours' (`battery_icon` in `ui/corner.rs`), the emptier one on a tie. The charging drawings fill less of the battery than their names say (the 60 draws 51 %, the 80 68 %, the 90 81 %), so they are chosen by name. The touch area is the icon's corner square (`CornerStyle::area`): 66 px, the 50 px icon plus the margin on every side; in Deck, the height of home's top line at its right end; in Rail, the rail's foot under its icons. Two touches have an effect, both only on a computer port below full (`Charge::touch`): on the green battery held at the ceiling, a touch charges it to full, and the icon turns from green to the interface colour as charging starts; a second touch during that charge holds it at its current level, green again. A full battery, one on a charger, and one still charging up to the ceiling ignore touches. A touch wakes the battery thread, so the response does not wait for the next reading.
