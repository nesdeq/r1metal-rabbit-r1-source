# Signal

The R1 is a linked device of the owner's Signal account, as Signal Desktop is: it shows the account's conversations, one-to-one and in groups, sends messages typed or said to either, and in auto mode has the language model answer a contact's conversation for the owner. The phone stays the account's primary device; the R1 follows what the phone decides (blocks, accepted requests, safety numbers, settings) through sync messages and the storage service. Screens: [Interface](interface.md#signal).

| Fact | Value |
|---|---|
| Linking | a QR code the phone scans under Signal, Settings, Linked devices; the device is named `r1metal R1` (`link::DEVICE_NAME`) |
| History | none: messages from the link on |
| Conversations | one-to-one and groups ([Groups](#groups)); a message's reactions stand at its side ([Receiving](#receiving)); calls are kept in a log of their own and handed to the call layer ([Calls](#calls)); stories and typing are not shown, and the R1 gives no reaction |
| Attachments | photos (JPEG, PNG) and voice notes are fetched, kept, shown and played, and the owner sends photos and videos of the rolls, notes recorded and voice notes ([Attachments](#attachments)); the rest by kind only: `video`, `file`, `sticker`, a shared contact as `contact`, a poll by its question beside `poll`; a message that needs a later protocol version than Desktop's 8 shows nothing but `needs a newer Signal` (`content::PROTOCOL_VERSION`, Desktop's `isUnsupportedMessage`) |
| Text only | `TEXT ONLY` in Signal's settings: every conversation shows text alone, a photo as `photo` and a voice note as `voice note`, and nothing is fetched, decoded, drawn or played |

## Linking

Opened while the R1 is not linked, `SIGNAL` shows the link window. The `signal` thread opens the provisioning socket, `wss://chat.signal.org/v1/websocket/provisioning/`, and answers the service's `PUT /v1/address` with 200. The window shows the address as a QR code (`sgnl://linkdevice?uuid=…&pub_key=…&capabilities=nopni%2Cnopni2`, `link::address`), the key being a fresh X25519 key pair. A code stands 45 s (`CODE_LIFETIME`); after 6 codes (`CODES`, counted by `link::Codes`) the window says `CODE EXPIRED`. The socket is open only while the window shows and the device is awake.

The phone sends a `ProvisionEnvelope` in `PUT /v1/message`. `link::open_envelope` checks its MAC and decrypts it (HKDF-SHA256 with info `TextSecure Provisioning Message`, AES-256-CBC, HMAC-SHA256) into the account: ACI and PNI with their identity key pairs, the number, the provisioning code, the profile key, the master key and whether the account sends read receipts. `link::prepare` makes a password of 16 random bytes, 22 characters of base64 without padding (`link::generate_password`), registration IDs, a signed pre-key and a Kyber1024 last-resort pre-key per identity, and the device name sealed under the ACI identity key. The link request, `PUT /v1/devices/link` on the account's socket with basic authentication `ACI:password`, answers with the device ID. Then the account is written, its own identity keys held as verified, as Signal Desktop holds them once linked, and 100 one-time X25519 and 100 one-time Kyber pre-keys per identity are uploaded (`link::first_uploads`). The keys the link carried count as confirmed.

| Failure | Bar |
|---|---|
| no network | `NO NETWORK` |
| no code accepted in 6 codes | `CODE EXPIRED` |
| 409: the service takes the R1 for an outdated client | `TOO OLD` |
| 411: too many linked devices | `TOO MANY DEVICES` |
| the account cannot be written | `NO STORAGE` |
| anything else | `REFUSED` |

A failure stands in the window until it closes or the device sleeps; opened again, it begins anew. From the phone's account to the end of the first uploads the link holds off a suspend and the radio's rest (`State::is_off`), so that a sleep does not cut the link request.

## The service

Every connection goes to `chat.signal.org` or `storage.signal.org` over TLS 1.3, trusting Signal's two roots alone (`service::ROOTS`, `Purpose::Signal` in `r1_funcore::net::tls`, which `service::to_purpose` gives every host of Signal's, whoever connects): its RSA root, under which the storage service, the groups server and CDNs are, and its Ed25519 root, under which the chat service is. Requests carry `User-Agent: r1metal/` and the version. A request over HTTPS on a connection of its own, to the storage service, the groups server or a CDN, is a `service::Request`, which also says how much of its answer's body is taken.

The account's socket is `wss://chat.signal.org/v1/websocket/` with basic authentication `ACI.deviceID:password` and `x-signal-receive-stories: false`. Sealed requests go on a second socket of the stay without authentication, opened for the first of them and closed once none waits for its answer (`Action::Anonymous`); one that cannot be opened or breaks has what waited on it tried again later. Frames are `WebSocketMessage` protobufs (`service::Frame`), requests numbered by the R1 across both sockets and answered by id. A stay (`connection::Connection`):

- answers every `PUT /api/v1/message` with 200 once the delivery is taken, and takes `PUT /api/v1/queue/empty` as the end of what waited, the catch-up, or, while the device sleeps, 60 s after the stay began where none came (`ANSWER_TIMEOUT`);
- sends `PUT /v1/devices/capabilities` once (`attachmentBackfill`, `spqr`, `usernameChangeSyncMessage`, and `optionalPhoneNumber` where the account has no PNI);
- asks `GET /v1/keepalive` every 30 s (`KEEPALIVE_PERIOD`), and ends when that or any request goes unanswered for 30 or 60 s. These times, an errand put off and a link code's 45 s run on the clock that a setting of the wall clock does not move (`CLOCK_BOOTTIME`), so a clock set by hand or by the time service neither ends a stay nor stops its keepalives; messages are stamped, and the kept schedules counted, by the wall clock;
- asks once a stay for each sender certificate an errand may go sealed under where none is held, `GET /v1/certificate/delivery` and `GET /v1/certificate/delivery?includeE164=false` (`service::certificate_path`), as Signal Desktop fetches one; a certificate is used until an hour before it runs out (`CERTIFICATE_MARGIN`), one asked without the number must name none, and the errand waits while it is asked;
- keeps the pre-keys of both identities up, as Signal Desktop does ([Pre-keys](#pre-keys));
- reads the contacts, the groups and the account's settings from the storage service once a day (`STORAGE_PERIOD`), at once where no reading has listed the groups yet, and again whenever the phone says the storage service holds something new ([Storage service](#storage-service));
- fetches every group whose state is due from the groups server, under the day's credential it asks the socket for ([Groups](#groups));
- once the catch-up is done: asks each device whose message did not open to send it again, answers each device that asked the R1 to send one again, sends the delivery receipts and the profile keys owed, and, once after the link, asks the owner's other devices for the blocked list and the settings (`SyncMessage.Request` `BLOCKED` and `CONFIGURATION`, not urgent, as Signal Desktop's `sendSyncRequests` asks for them beside the contacts, which the R1 names from the storage service alone).

A socket the service refuses with 401 or 403 means the phone unlinked the R1 (`service::is_unlinked`): `SIGNAL` opens the link window at once, and the account is forgotten ([Unlink](#unlink)). A stay that could not begin or broke gives its fetch up until the next wake or alarm, and the screens tell it by its reason: `NO STORAGE` where Signal's files could not be written (`State::fail_storage`), else the word of its failure (`cause_word`), which for a stay is no network; where the list, a conversation or a message still wants the socket, and asleep while a turn of auto mode is at work for its reply, the next stay follows after 30 s (`RETRY_WAIT`, `State::retry_wait`) or the next change of the board (what the screens show or ask, or the turn's reply), whichever comes first. Asleep with nothing at work, nothing is tried again before the next wake or alarm.

### Pre-keys

Per identity (`keys`), as Signal Desktop's `AccountManager` keeps them: after the first catch-up, and every 2 days after a check that ended whole (`keys::CHECK_PERIOD`), the service's counts of one-time keys are asked. A kind of one-time key the service holds fewer than 10 or more than 200 of (`MIN_COUNT`, `MAX_COUNT`, Desktop's `PRE_KEY_MINIMUM` and `PRE_KEY_MAX_COUNT`) gets a new batch of 100; the signed and the last-resort Kyber key are renewed where the newest confirmed one is 36 h old (`RENEW_AGE`). The service's acceptance of an upload confirms the keys it carried, and only then are replaced keys let go: a signed or last-resort key once it is neither among the newest 5 nor younger than 30 days (`NEWEST_KEYS`, `QUEUE_TIME`), with the uses of a last-resort key; an unused one-time key once it is past the newest 200 and 90 days old (`UNUSED_AGE`). A check whose answer fails is tried again 5 minutes later (`CHECK_RETRY`). Where the newest confirmed signed or last-resort key of the ACI is 14 days old or none is confirmed (`MAX_CONFIRMED_AGE`, Desktop's `KEY_TOO_OLD_THRESHOLD`), the keys are checked at once, before the catch-up, and nothing is sent until they are renewed, as Desktop sends nothing then.

## Receiving

A delivery is an `Envelope` (`device::Device::deliver`); one seen before, by its server GUID among the last 1,000, is dropped. Sealed sender is opened with the ACI identity key, its sender certificate checked against Signal's trust roots, its revocation and its expiry at the server's time (`sealed::open`): version 1, in which a message to one device comes, a sender key's distribution among them (HKDF-SHA256 over X25519 with the ephemeral key and then with the sender's identity key, each part AES-256-CTR with 10 bytes of HMAC-SHA256 behind it, the identity key inside the certificate's), and version 2, in which a message to many comes. A PreKey message begins a session as PQXDH's recipient (`session::State::accept`); a message continues one with the Double Ratchet, its post-quantum part SPQR's triple ratchet (`spqr`), whose ML-KEM-768 braid sends its keys in erasure-coded chunks and drops the handshake's key at the first message received; a group's message may come under its sender's sender key instead ([Groups](#groups)).

Signal Desktop's rules decide what is taken (`MessageReceiver`, `handleDataMessage`):

- From the R1 itself, nothing. To the phone number's identity (PNI), nothing sealed and nothing but a PreKey message; from a PNI, nothing unsealed.
- A key of the account's own must be the one held, received or sent; any other account's key is trusted for receiving. A PreKey message whose base key names a session the R1 holds must carry that session's identity key, as libsignal refuses it otherwise. A sender key's certificate saves no identity key.
- From a blocked account nothing is taken; a message to a blocked group is dropped once the profile key it carries is taken.
- A DataMessage whose timestamp is not its envelope's, or that carries a flag Desktop does not know, is dropped.
- A group's message that names a later revision than the one held waits, kept in its conversation's file, until the group's state is fetched, then is taken by the rules that follow. It is dropped where the owner or its sender is no member of the group, or where only administrators may send and its sender is none; a sealed one from an accepted conversation is then owed a delivery receipt; one to a group that was ended is dropped after that.
- A contact's message clears the mark that it is not on Signal.

What a message says goes into its conversation (`content::parse_content`): a text, with whom it mentions and the message it replies to; the owner's message from another device (a sync transcript), with when its timer began there; an edit; a reaction, a contact's or the owner's from another device; a deletion for everyone, by its author or a group's administrator, and the owner's deletions for themself on another device; a receipt; reads on another device, which begin the messages' timers at the time they were read there; the owner's profile key from another device, which also has the conversation shared; the blocked list, the read-receipt setting and the answers to message requests the owner gave on another device; the account's keys and a new number from the phone; a contact's proof of its phone number; and a word that the storage service holds something new. A Verified sync is dropped, as Desktop drops it: safety numbers follow the phone through the storage service. A call message, the account's syncs of its calls and a group's call update go to the calls' gate and log ([Calls](#calls)).

**Mentions and replies.** A mention shows as `@` and the name of whom it names in the place of its mark among the words, counted in UTF-16 units as Signal counts them (`Device::resolve_mentions`), `@Unknown` for one without a name. A reply shows over its words `Reply to `, whose message it quotes (`You` for the owner's) and the first line of what it quotes.

**Reactions** (`DataMessage.reaction`, `content::Reacted`, `Device::react`), as Desktop takes them (`isMessageAMatchForReaction`, `handleReaction`): a reaction names a message by its author and timestamp and carries one emoji, or takes the one its giver gave back. It is taken to a message its author wrote in that conversation that is not deleted for everyone: in a group that is not ended, from a member, while the owner is one; in a contact's conversation, where the author is the contact or the owner. Each who reacts holds one reaction to a message, the newest taking the place of the one before; of the owner's from another device a later one held stays. An emoji the screen draws no picture of (`glyph::is_emoji`) is no reaction. They are kept with their message in its conversation's file, each with who gave it and when, go with a message deleted for everyone, and stand at the message's side, each emoji once in the order it first came, with how many gave it where more than one did (`👍2 🙏`, `Entry::reaction_counts`). A reaction tells itself by no notice and is not read aloud.

**Deletions for everyone**, as Desktop takes them (`canReceiveDeleteForEveryone`, `Device::erase`): by the message's author, or in a group by one of its administrators (`DataMessage.adminDelete`, the message named by its author and timestamp); never in the Note to Self or in a group that was ended, nor of a message deleted already; and only where the deletion came no more than a day apart from the message, a day more allowed for its way (`DELETE_WINDOW`, `DELETE_GRACE`, Desktop's `DEFAULT_DELETE_MAX_AGE_MS` and `MESSAGE_SEND_GRACE_PERIOD`). The message keeps its place without its words, says `This message was deleted.`, and an administrator's says `by an administrator` at its side; the send log's copy of the owner's message goes with it.

**Deletions for the owner alone** (`SyncMessage.DeleteForMe`, `Device::apply_delete_for_me`), as Desktop takes them (`deleteMessage`, `deleteConversation`, `deleteLocalOnlyConversation`): each message named goes; a conversation cleared loses every message up to the newest of those named that it holds, else of those named that do not disappear; one deleted whole lets go of its safety number notice too once it holds no message, as does a conversation named as holding nothing of anybody's. The send log's copies go with the owner's messages.

**What comes before its message.** A deletion, an edit, a reaction, a receipt or a deletion for the owner alone that names a message the R1 does not hold yet is held, kept in the file `seen`, and taken in as soon as the message comes, as Desktop holds them (`forMessage`): a message deleted for everyone before it came is taken in deleted and is not told as new; of a deletion for the owner alone only what still waits is held. What was held 30 days (`EARLY_AGE`, the service's message queue time) goes at the next message, and no more is held than deliveries are remembered, 1,000, the oldest going first (`MAX_EARLY`).

**The owner's messages** show how far they got, as Desktop shows the best any recipient told (`getHighestSuccessfulRecipientStatus`): `sending`, `not sent`, nothing once sent, `delivered` once a recipient's device took it, `read` once one read it, never back. A receipt counts only from a recipient of the message and once the service took it; a read receipt, a viewed one alike, only where the owner sends read receipts, as Desktop drops it otherwise (`shouldDropReceipt`).

**The account's keys** (`SyncMessage.Keys`): the master key, else the one the account entropy pool gives (HKDF-SHA256, info `20240801_SIGNAL_SVR_MASTER_KEY`, `account::derive_master_key`); another than the one held is kept, and the storage service is read again under it (Desktop's `onKeysSync`).

**A new number** (`SyncMessage.PniChangeNumber`, taken only from the account's own ACI and with the envelope's `updatedPni`, before anything else of its delivery): the new phone number identity, its key pair, registration and number, and the signed pre-key and last-resort Kyber key the phone gave the service (`Ring::from_keys`), whose one-time keys the next check makes at once; the old identity's key, the sender certificates and the day's credential for the groups server, which name the old number, are let go (Desktop's `handlePNIChangeNumber`, `setPni`).

**A contact's number** (`PniSignatureMessage`): where the identity key held for a phone number identity signed the contact's identity key, as libsignal signs an alternate identity (32 bytes of 0xFF, `Signal_PNI_Signature`, the key as written), that identity is the contact's from then on, and a conversation held with it goes into the contact's (`Device::take_pni_signature`, Desktop's `handlePniSignatureMessage`).

**Disappearing messages.** A contact's conversation takes the timer a message carries by its version (`Conversation::take_timer`, Desktop's `updateExpirationTimer`): never from version nought or from an earlier version than the one held, a contact's or the owner's from another device alike. A group's timer is its state's, as the groups server tells it; a message to a group never changes it. A contact's message's timer begins when the owner reads it, on the R1 or on another device, at the time it was read there; the owner's when it was sent ([Sending](#sending)). While a stay is under way a message disappears at its time, the stay waiting no longer than until the next one does (`Device::next_expiry`, `Connection::due_in`); no stay is made for it, so outside one it disappears at the next. The send log's copy goes with it.

**Safety numbers.** The R1 holds every account's identity key as Desktop holds it (`device::KnownIdentity`): the key, the state of its verification (default, verified, unverified), whether it was the first held, when it was saved and whether its change was approved. The first key of an account is held as such. A changed key replaces the one held, unverified where the one before was verified or unverified, else in the default state; the sessions with the account's other devices are set aside, and the conversation says `SAFETY NUMBER CHANGED` until it is read. The storage service's contact records carry the phone's key and verification state, taken at the first reading, where none is held or where they hold the key held, as approved. How a key stands decides sending ([Sending](#sending)).

**Requests to send again.** A message that does not open is asked again of the device that sent it, as Desktop asks (`requestResend`), except a message taken before, one from a blocked account, one to the phone number's identity, and one whose ratchet key cannot be read: once the catch-up is done, 1 to 6 s later at random (`RESEND_UNIT`, `RESEND_SPREAD`), or at once for one that failed during the catch-up, a DecryptionErrorMessage naming the message's ratchet key, timestamp and device goes as plaintext content to every device of the sender, under the message's own timestamp, not urgent, sealed with the group its sealing named where it may be sealed (`Order::resend_request`). The same message opening meanwhile lets the request go. A device's request to the R1, plaintext content sealed or not, is taken only where it carries a DecryptionErrorMessage, and answered once the catch-up is done (`Device::resend`, Desktop's `onRetryRequest`): where it names the R1's device and the ratchet key the R1 sends to that device under, that session is set aside; what was sent under its timestamp is sent again as it was, with its timestamp and urgency, where the send log holds it and it is no older than 14 days, with the R1's sender key distribution beside it where it went in a group the R1 has a sender key of; else, where it names a group the R1 has a sender key of, that distribution alone, not urgent; else a null message where the session was set aside. Nothing goes to a requester that is no member of the group, and nothing where the R1's own record of the distribution is gone. Requests made and answered count 5 a message of a device at the most (`RETRY_LIMIT`), a count let go 14 days after its first (`RESEND_AGE`), or as the one begun first where more than 1,000 are kept (`MAX_RETRY_COUNTS`).

**Receipts.** Opening a conversation marks its messages read, begins their timers, and sends the account's other devices a read sync, urgent as Desktop sends it. Where the account sends read receipts, as the link, the storage service's account record or the phone's settings sync set it, and the conversation is accepted, each who wrote a message read gets a read receipt, at most 100 timestamps a receipt (`MAX_RECEIPT_TIMESTAMPS`), not urgent, unless blocked or found not to be on Signal in the last 6 hours (`UNREGISTERED_AGE`). A contact's sealed message to an accepted conversation is owed a delivery receipt, whatever the setting, sent the same way once the catch-up is done. A receipt of a group's message goes sealed under the group's endorsements where the writer refused the R1's sealing ([Groups](#groups)). A conversation left open while the device sleeps reads nothing until it wakes.

**Accepted conversations** (`Device::is_accepted`, Desktop's `isConversationAccepted`): the owner's own is; a contact the owner removed is not; one whose request the owner accepted is, and so is a contact the address book names, one the owner shares the profile with or wrote to, and one with no message yet. A group is accepted where the owner accepted it, wrote to it, or, with no message yet, shares the profile with it. The storage service's records and the phone's syncs set what decides it: a block blocks and stops sharing, an acceptance unblocks and shares, a deletion stops sharing and lets the conversation's messages go.

**The owner's profile key.** A message from a trusted contact (the address book names it or the profile is shared with it) that came unsealed is answered, once the catch-up is done, with the owner's profile key alone, not urgent, so that the next comes sealed (Desktop's `sendProfileKey`).

## Sending

What the device has sent is an order (`device::Order`: the content, whom it goes to and how), and its sending an errand (`errand::Errand`): the pre-key bundles of the account's devices are fetched where there is no session (`GET /v2/keys/ACI/*`; the service writes their keys and signatures in base64 without its padding, read with or without it, `code::unbase64_unpadded`), sessions begun as PQXDH's initiator with Kyber1024 encapsulation, and the message sealed for every device and sent in one `PUT /v1/messages/ACI` (`service::write_submission`). A message's timestamp is its id, so two written in one millisecond are a millisecond apart. The time a message is taken in or written at, by which a conversation's messages and the list stand in order, rises the same way from a mark of its own, which the newest message the files hold sets at a start (`Device::entry_time`): it never lies at or before the one before it, whatever the clock does meanwhile. A message is padded as Signal's clients pad one: a 0x80 byte, then noughts to one short of a multiple of 80 bytes.

**Trust.** A key is trusted for sending as Desktop trusts it (`isTrustedForSending`): where none is held; where it is the key held, not left unverified by a change of a verified key, and changed at least 5 s before or approved (`TRUST_DELAY`). A bundle's key is checked before a session is begun, and the session's before every seal. A key changed a moment ago has the message to that account wait and go on its own 60 s later, its conversation told nothing more; an unverified one stops it until the phone verifies the key again. A session whose key is not trusted is set aside with all of the account's, and its pre-keys are asked anew once.

**Sealed sender**, as Desktop seals (`getSendOptions`): never to the account's own devices; to a contact whose sealing is not known yet, under its access key, the first 16 bytes of AES-256 of its profile key over the counter block of nought but a final 2 (zkgroup's `derive_access_key`), or under noughts where its profile key is not held; under noughts to one that takes sealed messages from anybody; to one whose sealing was refused, unsealed, unless the message is of a group whose endorsements authorize it, under the group's token ([Groups](#groups)). It goes only while a sender certificate is held: the one that names the account's number where the number is shared with everybody, as the storage service's account record says, else the one without it. The R1 seals in version 1 (`sealed::seal`) and sends on the unauthenticated socket with `unidentified-access-key` or `group-send-token`. A 401 or 403 there has the pre-keys asked, or the submission sealed anew, under the R1's name, and the contact's sealing refused from then on; a message taken sealed while its sealing was not known marks it as taking sealed messages under its access key, or from anybody where no profile key was held. A new profile key of a contact leaves its sealing unknown again. Syncs to the owner's devices go under the R1's name.

**Answers.** The service's 409 (devices missing or extra) and 410 (sessions stale) set the sessions of extra and stale devices aside, as Desktop archives them, the pre-keys of missing and stale devices are asked, and the errand is tried again, 3 times at the most (`MAX_TRIES`); a 404 to the pre-keys sets every session with the account aside and marks it not on Signal, as does a 404 to a submission. A 428 asks a challenge only the phone can answer, which ends the errand; a 429 or 5xx has it tried again 60 s later where something else holds the stay, at the next stay otherwise: an errand put off holds no stay (`Connection::status`). At the next stay a message put off goes again from its conversation, where it is still `Sending`, and a receipt, a read sync or a sync transcript from the orders put off, which are kept in memory alone (`Device`'s `deferred_orders`). A message goes into its conversation file as `Sending` as soon as a stay begins or is under way, whether or not the socket opens, and stays so until the service takes it; one that cannot be sent is `not sent`, and the log tells why without naming anyone: how it ended, the service's answers to the pre-keys and to the submission, how many devices the pre-keys named and how many had a session, and how often a refusal was put right (`Errand::failure_line`). Every file a delivery or a sealing changed is written before the service hears of it; where a file cannot be written, the stay ends before anything is answered or sent, the screens say `NO STORAGE`, and memory keeps what it took until a later write succeeds.

**Who it goes to** (`Device::recipients`): a contact unless blocked or found not to be on Signal in the last 6 hours; a group's members but the owner and those blocked, where the owner is a member, the group is neither blocked nor ended, and the owner may send there. The owner's message shares the profile in its conversation from then on and carries the profile key only where it is shared; a contact's message carries the timer's version, also where the timer is off, a group's never, as Desktop writes them.

**After it is sent.** Its timer begins, and a sync transcript goes to the account's other devices, not urgent: the same DataMessage, when its timer began (`expirationStartTimestamp`), and each recipient with whether it went sealed, so the phone shows the message as sent. A message to Note to Self goes as that transcript alone, its timer begun as it is written, and where the account has no other device nothing goes (Desktop's `sendSyncMessageOnly`).

**The send log.** What the R1 sends is kept a day (`SENT_AGE`, Desktop's send log) to be sent again where a device asks, in the file `sent`; not what it sends again, nor its requests to send again, nor a sender key's distribution to one account. A copy goes with the owner's message it carries when that is deleted or disappears.

## Attachments

A DataMessage's `AttachmentPointer`s are kept with their message in its conversation's file (`attachment::Attachment`); lines written before still read. A pointer without keys, digest, size or a place on a CDN is none. Their plaintexts are kept one to a file as `DIGEST.EXT` in `attachments/` of Signal's files (`kept_as`, the digest in hex, `jpg`, `png`, `m4a`, `mp4` or `bin`). Transfers run on a thread of their own, `attachments`, beside the `signal` thread, one at a time, each holding the network for itself (`transfers::carry`), and where the network is refused one, every transfer due waits for the next stay with it (`State::hold_transfers`); the `signal` thread, the `attachments` thread, auto mode's writer, the `pictures` thread and the `voices` thread share one state with the main loop (`domain::signal::state::State`), which decides what is carried. A transfer due or under way holds off a suspend and the radio's rest as a request on its way does (`State::is_off`); what a transfer came to waits for the next stay, as a message waits for a socket. Logs tell how a transfer ended and nothing of what it carried (`attachment::to_finished_line`).

**Fetching.** Only photos (`image/jpeg`, `image/png`) and voice notes of the messages the conversation that shows holds in its view, its last 200 (`list::MAX_MESSAGES`), the newest message's first (`Device::downloads_due`), only while the device is awake and `TEXT ONLY` is off, and only those not held yet: a sleep, another screen or `TEXT ONLY` lets go of those due, one under way running to its end. The request is `GET /attachments/KEY` (the key percent-encoded as `encodeURIComponent` does) or `/attachments/ID` on the CDN of the pointer's number, `cdn.signal.org`, `cdn2.signal.org` or `cdn3.signal.org` (`service::CDNS`, the first for a number not known), under Signal's roots ([The service](#the-service)). The body is refused once it grows past what the pointer's size takes when sealed (`seal::sealed_size`, `service::Request::check_size`). It is opened as Desktop opens one (`decryptAttachmentV2ToSink`, `seal::decrypt`): whole blocks, the HMAC-SHA256 under the second half of the 64 keys bytes and the SHA-256 digest of the pointer compared without stopping at the first difference, AES-256-CBC with PKCS #7, the plaintext cut to the pointer's size. A fetch that breaks or whose file cannot be kept is tried again at the next stay; one that is refused (a 4xx but 401, 403, 408 and 429, or a check that fails) is asked again at the next stay at the soonest; one its CDN holds no more (a 404, or the first CDN's 403) is asked for no more, its place on the CDN let go, as Desktop lets it go (`downloadAttachment`, `markAttachmentAsErrored`, `Finish::Gone`).

**Uploading.** The owner's message with files is `Sending` until each went up, then goes (`Device::compose`, `uploads_due`, `take_finished`); after a restart the messages still `Sending` resume. What goes up is a photo of the pictures roll (`image/jpeg`, with its size in pixels), a video of the videos roll (`video/mp4`), and a note recorded or a voice note said (`audio/aac`, flagged a voice note): `Enclosure::photo`, `video`, `note` and `said`. A file whose ciphertext would be more than 100 MiB, which is one of more than 102,224,512 bytes, is refused before it is sealed, as Signal's apps send none larger (`attachment::fits`, Desktop's `isAttachmentTooLarge` with its default limit); its message is `not sent`. A video that large is told `TOO LARGE TO SEND` on its roll at the touch of its `send` icon and waits for no conversation; at the recorder's 16 Mbit/s about 50 s of video go. Each file is sealed as Desktop seals one (`encryptAttachmentV2`): padded with noughts to the first power of 1.05 no smaller (Desktop's `logPadSize`, at least 541 bytes: `seal::padded`), a random IV, AES-256-CBC under a key of 64 bytes drawn for the file, the IV before and the HMAC-SHA256 behind, and the SHA-256 digest of all. The form is `GET /v4/attachments/form/upload?uploadLength=N` on the chat service under the account's authentication; its CDN number decides the way up (`transfer::Transfer`):

| CDN | Way | Resume |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | TUS 1.0.0: `POST` to the signed location with `Upload-Length`, `Upload-Metadata: filename` and the key in base64, `Tus-Resumable` and the body | `HEAD` for `Upload-Offset`, then `PATCH` from there; 3 tries (`TUS_TRIES`, Desktop's `DEFAULT_MAX_RETRIES`) |
| any other | resumable upload: `POST` to the signed location for a session in `Location`, then `PUT` with `Content-Range: bytes 0-*/N` | `PUT` with `Content-Range: bytes */N`, whose 308 names the last byte in `Range`; 10 tries (`PUT_TRIES`, Desktop's `MAX_RETRIES`) |

The form's headers go with every request to the CDN but the resumable upload's `PUT`s; a header the request sets takes the place of the form's of its name, whatever the case of its letters, as Desktop lays its own over the form's (`createFetchForAttachmentUpload`), so the form's `Upload-Metadata` goes once. The pointer (keys, digest, size, kind, flags, size in pixels, CDN number, key, when it went up) is written into the message, which goes once all it carries went up. An answer that is a client's fault (a 4xx but 401, 403, 408 and 429) ends the message `not sent`; any other failure is tried again at the next stay. A photo of the roll is kept as `TIMESTAMP-INDEX.jpg` beside the others once it went up, its picture following it there unchanged (`Report::moved`, told with the conversation that holds it there), so that the conversation shows it on without decoding it again; a note recorded is kept the same way, as `TIMESTAMP-INDEX.m4a`, so that it plays where its message stands whatever becomes of the note; a video stays the roll's file alone, which its message names (`attachment::sent_as`); a voice note said is kept before its message is written, as `HASH.m4a` by the SHA-256 of its sound.

**Pictures.** A conversation shows a photo whole under its message's words, centred. A JPEG is decoded on the `pictures` thread from the kept file to stand as wide as the room, never larger than it is, turned as its EXIF orientation 1 to 8 says (`Jpeg::orientation`, the TIFF tag 0x0112 of the first APP1 segment that holds EXIF; `jpeg::oriented`); a PNG up to 4096 pixels a side (`pictures::LARGEST_SIDE`, Signal-Android's `PushMediaConstraints` level 3) is shrunk to the room's width over the screen's ground. A photo has its place from the first layout on, as tall as the size its pointer tells makes it at the room's width (`pictures::standing_height`), the word `photo` in it until its picture is decoded, so that a picture that comes moves no row; one whose pointer tells no size takes its place as its picture comes. The pictures are decoded from the foot up, the draft's first, then the newest message's (`Ui::signal_photos`). A file that cannot be decoded (a progressive JPEG, a WebP, a GIF, a PNG larger than that) keeps the word `photo` at its message's side, its place given up. The pictures go when the conversation is left, and the photos not decoded yet are decoded no more.

**Voice notes.** A click with no draft plays the voice note whose message stands nearest the foot of the room, or stops the one that plays; a press stops one that plays, and `playing` stands at the message's side. It ends at its end, on leaving the conversation and at sleep. The `voices` thread plays it from its kept file (`sound::play_voice`). Where `SAID MESSAGES` is set to `AS VOICE NOTES` (`signal_said voice`, `is_said_voice`), a held button records a voice note in place of a message for the speech model: the recorder's file as it is, kept, waiting under the draft as `voice note N s`, and a click sends it as `audio/aac` flagged a voice note (`AttachmentPointer.Flags.VOICE_MESSAGE`); nothing goes to OpenAI then. A note of `AUDIO NOTE` goes the same way from `SEND` on its page, its sound as the recorder wrote it, under the draft as `voice note N s` by its length ([Interface](interface.md#notes-and-texts)).

**Text only.** With `TEXT ONLY` on, a photo is told as `photo` and a voice note as `voice note` in every conversation; nothing is fetched, decoded, drawn or played, a click with no draft only swings the room, and a draft carrying a photo or a voice note still goes. Turning it on or off has the conversations published anew.

**Files.** The files of `attachments/` that nothing holds are taken away at the thread's start and after a round in which messages went (`files::sweep`, `attachment::unheld`): a file is held by a message, by a message waiting to be sent, or by a transfer under way or done and not yet taken into its message.

## The robot's tool

While the Signal tool is on ([Settings](interface.md#settings)) the model has two functions, declared after `change_setting`, strict, and waited for (`tools::functions`):

- `read_signal` with a `conversation` name or null. With null it lists the conversations, the pinned first, then the newest first: each by its name with how many messages are unread and its newest message. With a name it gives that conversation's last 50 messages (`auto::CONTEXT_MESSAGES`), the oldest first, each as `[Friday 16 October 2026, 14:03] who: words`, the time the R1 took the message in by the clock on screen, who `the user`, `you in auto mode`, the group member's name or the contact's. A message's attachments read `[a photo]`, `[a voice note]`, `[a video]`, `[a file]`, `[a sticker]`, `[a shared contact]` or `[a poll]`, and a message that needs a newer Signal reads `[a message this device cannot show]`; one deleted for everyone reads `This message was deleted.`. Reading marks nothing read.
- `draft_signal` with a `conversation` name and the `text`. The conversation opens as a hand would open it, from Signal's list, with the text as a draft the owner sends with a click, once the model's reply is over and only while the R1 is linked (`Act::Draft`). The robot sends nothing. A text that says nothing is no call.

A name is looked for among the conversations and the contacts a message may go to: the one of that name whatever its letters' case, else the one alone whose name holds the words. None, or several, is answered so, naming the several. Where the R1 is not linked the answer says so. The answers come from the `signal` thread's device in memory, without the network, through the main loop, by the round trip the interface's readouts take (`Asked::Signal`, `Reading::Ask`, `Signal::ask_question`, `robot::answer`, `Report::reply`).

## Storage service

`GET /v1/storage/auth` on the socket gives the credentials; the manifest and the contact, group and account records it lists are read (`storage::StorageRead`), at most 2,500 a request, and decrypted: the manifest with the key derived from the master key (`storage::storage_key`), the records with keys from the manifest's record key, without which the manifest is not read. The log's line counts the records that do not open, and a reading in which some did not and none did counts as none, as one whose manifest does not open. They are merged as Desktop merges them (`mergeContactRecord`, `mergeGroupV2Record`, `mergeAccountRecord`):

- a contact: its nickname, else its address-book name, else its profile name, else its number, else its username, else `Unknown` (Desktop's `getTitleNoDefault`); its phone number identity; its profile key at the first reading or where none is held; whether the address book names it; its identity key and verification state ([Receiving](#receiving)); blocked, accepted (whitelisted) or neither, and removed; when it was found not to be on Signal; archived, marked unread, muted until when, and whether it tells calls while muted. The account's own record is passed over;
- a group: its master key, known from then on with its state due ([Groups](#groups)); blocked, accepted or neither; archived, marked unread, muted until when, and whether it tells mentions, replies and calls while muted, the field before today's saying no to mentions where it is set (`resolveLegacyNotifyForMentionsIfMuted`);
- the account: the owner's profile key, the read-receipt setting, whether the number is shared with everybody (`PhoneNumberSharingMode`), the Note to Self archived or marked unread, the conversations pinned in their order, whether muted conversations stay archived when the owner writes there (`keepMutedChatsArchived`) and count unread (`includeMutedChatsInBadge`), and whether a muted conversation tells mentions, replies and calls where it does not say itself, mentions and replies on and calls off until the record says (`DEFAULT_NOTIFY_IF_MUTED`).

## The list

How a conversation stands follows the storage service's records ([Storage service](#storage-service)), as Desktop lists it:

- **Pinned** conversations stand first in their order, then the rest, the newest first, which is the one whose message the R1 took in or wrote last ([Sending](#sending)); a pinned one is archived no more (`pinnedConversations`).
- **Archived** ones stand under a line of their own at the list's end ([Interface](interface.md#signal)); their unread messages count for nothing.
- **Marked unread**: its line says `NEW: ` and it counts one where it holds nothing unread (`getUnreadCountForBadge`).
- **Muted** until its time: a new message plays no notice, but a mention of the owner or a reply to the owner's message does where the conversation, else the account, has them told (Desktop's `shouldNotify`, `getNotifyWhileMuted`); its unread messages count only where the account has muted ones count.

The R1 writes no record back. A mark is taken from a record only where the record says another than it said before (`conversation::Mark`), so that what the R1 does stands until the phone sets the mark anew: opening a conversation clears its mark unread, and writing in an archived one takes it out of the archive, unless it is muted and the owner keeps muted ones archived (Desktop's `canConversationBeUnarchived`).

## Groups

A conversation is with a contact or in a group (`address::Peer`). A group is known by its master key, which its messages carry (`GroupContextV2`, `group::Context`) and the storage service's group records list; its title, members, administrators, timer, whether only administrators may send, whether it was ended and its revision come from Signal's groups server, as Signal's clients get them.

- **The group's keys.** zkgroup derives from the master key Signal's id of the group, which names its conversation, the key of its encrypted attributes, and the key pairs its members' ids and profile keys are encrypted under (`group::Keys`), all drawn by Sho, poksho's hash over HMAC-SHA256, as scalars and elements of Ristretto255 (RFC 9496); an id is written into an element by Lizard over SHA-256 (`signal::zk`).
- **The day's credential.** The groups server takes a zero-knowledge presentation of a credential the chat service issues. A stay that has a group to fetch asks `GET /v1/certificate/auth/group` on the socket for that day alone (`service::group_credentials_path`), once in the stay: a zkcredential credential over the account's ACI and the PNI the answer names, or for an account without a PNI over the one zkgroup makes of the account's credential salt (`zk::attributes::false_pni`). The credential's proof of issuance is checked under Signal's server public params as Signal Desktop ships them (`zk::credential::Issuer`), and the credential is held in memory alone for the stay.
- **The state.** A group's state is due where none is held, where a message named a later revision than the one held, or where its endorsements are to be asked anew. Each is fetched once a revision in a stay: `GET https://storage.signal.org/v2/groups`, authorized by the group's public params and a presentation of the credential for that group, both in hex (`group::GroupFetch`). Every presentation is a new proof from fresh random bytes, which proves that the R1 holds the credential over the ids the group's key encrypts in it without telling them. The answer's title and timer are opened with the group's key (AES-256-GCM-SIV, a 4-byte padding length before the attribute), each member's id is decrypted, as an ACI or a PNI, whichever the ciphertext holds, and the administrators are told by their role. A 403 or 404 from Signal's own servers (the answer carries `x-signal-timestamp`, which a refusal by what stands before them lacks) says the owner is no member or the group is no more: its members are let go until a message names a later revision. Any other answer changes nothing, and the next stay asks again.
- **Endorsements.** Beside a group's state the server issues an endorsement of each member, which a token for some members is made of that the chat service takes in place of their access keys (zkgroup's `GroupSendEndorsementsResponse`, `zk::endorsement`). They are taken where their proof holds under the endorsements' root key in Signal's server public params, they run out at the start of a day, and 2 hours to 7 days remain; they are used while more than 2 hours and less than 2 days remain and every member has one, as Desktop uses them, the token the first 16 bytes of the SHA-256 of the members' endorsements summed and decrypted with the group's key of their ids, as zkgroup builds it. Where they may not be used, a message that needs them has the group's state asked anew once, unless the group is ended or blocked.
- **Sender keys.** A member's devices send a group's messages under their sender keys, sealed (sender key messages, sealed sender's kind 7), or one to one as to a contact. A sender key reaches the R1 as a distribution message beside a one-to-one message's content (`sender_keys::Distribution`): a chain, the iteration and key it stands at, and the key its messages are signed with. The R1 keeps each member device's chains of each distribution, the newest 5, as libsignal does (`sender_keys::Record`); a distribution sent again of a chain held under the same signing key sets nothing back. A sender key message's XEdDSA signature is checked under its chain's key, its message key comes from the chain as a session's does (HMAC-SHA256 with 1 for the seed, 2 for the next chain key), and HKDF-SHA256 with info `WhisperGroup` stretches the seed into the IV and the AES-256-CBC key. A message up to 25,000 ahead of its chain opens, and 2,000 skipped keys are kept, as for a session (`session::ChainKey::take_seed`); one at a chain's last iteration is refused, as libsignal ends a sender chain there.
- **Listed.** A group is listed from its first message on, as a contact's conversation is: one a member sent, or one the owner sent from the R1 or another device. A group the storage service lists or a change names holds no message and is not listed. A group goes by its title, else by the names of its members but the owner.
- **Sending**, as Desktop sends (`sendToGroupViaSenderKey`, `Errand::group_step`). A message to a group whose state is due waits at the head of the errands until its group is fetched in that stay; it goes to the members as they are when it begins. First under the R1's sender key: one is made where none is held, its distribution a version 4 UUID, and anew 90 days after it was made (`SENDER_KEY_AGE`), or where a device that had it belongs to an account that is no member any more. The group's state is asked anew once where its endorsements may not be used. Members without a session have their pre-keys asked, under their access key, else the group's token for them, else the R1's name, as Desktop's `fetchKeysForServiceId`. The members the endorsements name, with a session, a trusted key and found on Signal, at least 2 of them (`MIN_SENDER_KEY_MEMBERS`), take it: the R1's sender key distribution goes one to one to each device that has not had it, sealed with the group's id, then the message, encrypted under the sender key and sealed in version 2 to all of them at once (`sealed::seal_many`), their devices ordered by their accounts' ids as written, `PUT /v1/messages/multi_recipient` on the unauthenticated socket under `group-send-token`. The service's answer is taken as Desktop takes it: 200 sends it, those it names not on Signal marked so; 409 and 410 put devices right as for one account, a stale device losing the key; 401 or 404 sends everything one to one; 428 and 429 end the errand as above. That way begins anew after every change, 10 times at the most (`MAX_ROUNDS`, Desktop's `MAX_RECURSION`). Every member it did not reach goes one to one, as to a contact, with the group's master key and the newest revision the R1 knows. It counts as sent where one member took it, and its sync transcript names the members. A group the owner is no member of sends nothing, and its message is `not sent`.
- **Not in groups.** Auto mode does not answer in a group, and nothing of a group is read aloud.

## Calls

The R1 takes Signal's calls as Signal Desktop does up to the call itself: their messages pass a gate and go to the call layer, which rings, answers and carries the media ([Calls](calls.md)); the signals the call layer sends go out as messages do; and a log of calls is kept in step with the account's other devices. The call layer and the `signal` thread meet in the thread's state: `State::take_call_report` hands over what came, `command::Command` carries what the call layer asks, and `State::set_calling` holds the socket for it.

**What is taken** (`content::parse_content`, `call::read`, `call::read_sync`): a Content's `CallMessage`, a one-to-one call's offer, answer, ICE candidates, hang-up and busy, and a group call's opaque messages (a ring, its answer, a member's media key or leave); the account's syncs `CallEvent` and `CallLogEvent`; and a group's `DataMessage.groupCallUpdate`, held to the group's membership and end as a message is, but sent no receipt and dropped by no rule of who may send there. A call message goes into no conversation, is held for no later message, is owed no receipt and is kept in no file.

**The gate** (`Device::gate_call`), rule after rule in the order of Signal Desktop's `handleCallingMessage` and RingRTC:

1. From a blocked account nothing is taken, as of any message; from an account whose identity key is not held, nothing.
2. An offer from a conversation that is not accepted, one that holds no message counting as not accepted for a call (`ignoreEmptyConvo`): to the phone number's identity it is passed over, as an answer would tell the caller the account; else the caller's devices are sent a hang-up `NEED_PERMISSION` naming the device that called, not urgent, and the call is logged missed at the offer's timestamp. Nothing else of the message is taken.
3. A message for another device of the account (`destinationDeviceId`) is dropped.
4. An offer the service held more than 60 whole seconds (`MAX_AGE_S`, RingRTC's `MAX_MESSAGE_AGE`) is logged missed at the time it was made (`handleRejectedIncomingCallRequest`), its age the service's `X-Signal-Timestamp` of the delivery less the envelope's server timestamp (`calculateMessageAge`); one of a muted conversation that does not tell calls while muted, or from an identity left unverified, is logged missed now (`handleIncomingCall`). Neither rings.
5. A group's ring or its answer is taken only of a group known, not blocked and accepted, of which the owner and the ringer are members, that is not ended, and where only administrators send, from one (`handleGroupCallRingUpdate`). A ring cancelled goes on and is logged missed, whatever its age; any other older than 60 s is logged missed; one of a muted group that does not tell calls is logged as begun and does not ring. An answer counts only from the account's own devices, one that rings passed over: accepted is logged joined, any other missed (RingRTC's `handle_received_call_message`). A media key or a leave counts only from a member while the owner is one.

What passes goes to the call layer as it came (`device::Incoming`): the sender's account and device, the name the list gives the sender's conversation and that of the group a group's signal is of, the signals, how long the service held them, whether the conversation is accepted, the sender's identity key as held and the account's own ACI identity key, which a one-to-one call's keys are drawn under, and the number of this device, which a hang-up passed on for its own doing names.

**Sending** (`Command::Signal`, `Device::signal_orders`): a signal goes to a contact's devices, to a group's members as its messages go, under the R1's sender key where it may ([Groups](#groups)), to members of a group the call layer names, or to the account's other devices, and is written for one device alone where the call layer names one. It goes as Desktop's `sendCallingMessage` sends it: of no content hint, kept in no send log, urgent only for an offer and a group's signal to be handled at once, sealed where a message would be. Before an offer to a contact the profile is shared with the contact and the owner's profile key goes on its own (`startOutgoingDirectCall`). A signal is worth nothing late: one the service does not take now (a wait asked, keys too old to send, a stay that ends or never stands) goes no more, and the call layer is told so (`Event::Unsent`); one refused is told in the log as `a call's signal not sent` with the service's answers.

**Relays** (`Command::Relays`): `GET /v2/calling/relays` on the account's socket. The relays it hands out, each with its credentials, its URLs, its URLs by address and the host name these are reached under, are kept in memory for the least time any of them may be used, none kept where one names no time, and asked again after it, as Desktop's `#getIceServers` keeps them. An answer that gives none is told the call layer as none, and the log tells the service's status.

**A group call's token** (`Command::Token`): asked beside the groups' states under the day's credential ([Groups](#groups)), `GET https://storage.signal.org/v2/groups/token` under the group's public params and a presentation, the token the text of the answer's first field (`ExternalGroupCredential`, Desktop's `getExternalGroupCredential`). It is none where the group is not known, the credential cannot be had, the server refuses or the stay ends first. With the token the call layer is handed the group's members, the owner among them, as Signal's calling server names them in a call (`device::Membership`, `Device::membership`): each by the SHA-256, in hex, of its id's ciphertext under the group's key, the 65 bytes the group's state holds it in (RingRTC's `MemberMap`), with its id and the name the list gives it.

**The log** (`device::calls`, the file `calls`): each call by its conversation and id, its kind (audio, video, group), whether the owner began it, who rang, when, how it stands and whether the owner has seen it; the last 200 logged, the first logged going first (`MAX_CALLS`, as many as a conversation's view holds of its messages). It changes as Desktop's call disposition changes it (`transitionDirectCallStatus`, `transitionGroupCallStatus`, `transitionTimestamp`): by the gate, by what the call layer tells of a call (`Command::Course`: begun, ringing, accepted, declined, hung up here or by the far side, missed), by a group's call update (a call begun, by its era as RingRTC reads one, where the group is accepted and, where only administrators send, its sender is one; an earlier time taken where the call is held), and by the account's other devices. A call whose kind, direction or ringer disagrees with what comes stays as it is. A call that came in and was missed or rings unanswered, or a group's that rings, was seen begun or was missed, is new until seen, and once seen stays seen (`saveCallHistory`).

**In step with the other devices.** Where what the call layer tells changes a call to a status Desktop syncs, the account's other devices get a `CallEvent`, not urgent: accepted for one answered, joined or rung out, not accepted for one declined (`statusToProto`); a missed or pending call is not synced. A `CallEvent` from another device changes a call of a conversation the R1 knows. A `CallLogEvent` clears every call no later than the one it names or its time, deleted ones among them, each kept deleted so that nothing brings it back and shown nowhere (`clearCallHistory`), or marks seen the calls logged up to that call, in every conversation or in its own (`getUnreadCallMessagesAndMarkRead`); clearing one conversation's calls is passed over, as Desktop passes it. When the log shows (`Command::Seen`) the calls logged up to the newest count as seen and the other devices get a `CallLogEvent` read up to the newest (`markCallsTabViewed`).

**For the screens** (`CallReport`): the log, the newest first, each call by the name the list gives its conversation, with its kind, direction, status, time and whether it was seen, a deleted one left out (`list::CallRow`); and the contacts and groups a call may go to, the contacts a message may go to but the Note to Self and each named group whose messages go to anyone. Both are published when they change and emptied by an unlink.

## Auto mode

A finger held 3 s on the robot over a contact's conversation (`HOLD_NS` in `ui/screen/seat.rs`) switches auto mode on for that conversation, or off; the robot cheers as it comes on. Over a group's it does nothing. One conversation at a time has it, and a restart switches it off. While it is on, the bar says `AUTO` and nothing of that conversation is read aloud. Otherwise what comes in a conversation that shows in the voice is read aloud by OpenAI's voice model, the bar saying `READING`, unless `READ ALOUD` is off in Signal's settings ([Interface](interface.md)).

Auto mode opens no socket of its own: its writer holds the network only beside a stay made for other reasons, and it answers in those, the alarm's fetch among them ([Power](#power)), each held until its turn's reply is sent; a turn the pace puts later waits for the next connection. A contact's message starts a turn once 5 s pass without another (`auto::SETTLE_MS`) and the service has sent what waited, so a burst gets one answer. The turn's request goes to the model and reasoning `AUTO MODEL` and `AUTO REASONING` set, `gpt-6.1-terra` at `high` on a new device (`Task::Auto`, [OpenAI](openai.md#language-models)), with `crates/funcore/assets/signal/auto.txt` and the conversation's last 50 messages (`auto::CONTEXT_MESSAGES`), each marked as the contact's, the owner's or auto mode's; the contact's name goes with them. It has no functions, no web search and no memory of the robot's conversation. The reply goes as the owner's message, beginning with `🤖 ` (`auto::MARK`), and the R1 tells it `auto mode` at its side.

| Auto mode stops | |
|---|---|
| a finger held 3 s on the robot again | |
| the owner writes in that conversation, on the R1 or on another device | |
| as many replies since it came on as Signal's settings allow, 20 on a new device | `AUTO MODE STOPS`: 5, 10, 20, 50 or 100 |
| a turn that fails | no key, the spending limit reached, no network, or no answer |
| a sleep, where the alarm fetches nothing | `FETCH BY ITSELF` set to `OFF` |

Replies are at least 30 s apart and at most 30 an hour on a new device, so that two answering machines cannot run each other's bill up; Signal's settings set both (`AUTO MODE WAITS`: 10 s, 30 s, 1, 2 or 5 min; `AUTO MODE AN HOUR`: 10, 20, 30 or 60), and the thread takes them as they change (`auto::Limits`). Whether auto mode is on, for which conversation, and its count are held in memory alone, so a restart switches it off; each reply it wrote keeps its mark in the conversation's file. Every turn is entered in the ledger as one of Signal's auto replies ([OpenAI](openai.md#ledger)).

## Power

Asleep the R1 cannot be reached; the service keeps what waits for the next connection, up to 30 days. Signal's settings set how often the thread fetches it, awake or asleep: every 5, 10, 15 or 60 minutes after the last fetch or the end of the last stay, whatever it was for, or never (`signal_wake` in the settings, 60 by default). The `signal` thread sets its alarm for that time (`state::State::next_fetch_s`, [Sleep and clock](../firmware/sleep.md#the-alarms-list)), which goes off awake as asleep. When it goes off on a sleeping device, the main loop lets the radio off its rest, the thread fetches what waits, auto mode answers what came where it is on, and the device suspends again. A message such a fetch brings that tells itself ([The list](#the-list)) plays the notice then too, with the screen dark, whatever conversation the device slept on ([Interface](interface.md#brightness-and-volume)); the device suspends once the sound has ended, a sound asked for holding the suspend off from the asking on (`CardState::is_quiet`). A fetch that cannot reach the service is given up until the next wake or alarm, and asleep so is an answer auto mode owed then, unless its turn was already at work: that one keeps the radio off its rest and its reply is tried for again after 30 s (`RETRY_WAIT`). Auto mode is paced by the clock that goes on through a sleep and that a setting of the wall clock does not move (`CLOCK_BOOTTIME`).

Awake, the thread connects at each wake and at the alarm until the service has sent what waited and auto mode has answered it, and stays connected while the list or a conversation shows and while a message is on its way; the settings hold no socket. Auto mode leaves the device to sleep by its usual rules ([Sleep and clock](../firmware/sleep.md#sleep)); where the alarm fetches nothing, it answers only in connections made for other reasons, and a sleep switches it off. A request on its way over an open socket keeps the radio up and holds off a suspend until the service has answered it, and so does a transfer of an attachment due or under way ([Attachments](#attachments)); a photo or a voice note is fetched only while the device is awake. While the call layer holds the socket (`State::set_calling`), the thread stays connected and holds off a suspend and the radio's rest, asleep as awake, and so does what the call layer asks of the network (a signal, relays, a token) until a stay takes it; a stay that fails or never stands tells such a signal not sent and such relays or a token none had ([Calls](#calls)).

A `signal` thread that can wait no more, its `epoll_wait` failing, ends until the app starts again, as the radio's and the modem's do: from then on Signal holds off neither a sleep nor the radio's rest, no transfer is due, and the robot is told that Signal does not run (`State::end_thread`).

## Unlink

When the service refuses the account's socket with 401 or 403, the phone has unlinked the R1, and the `signal` thread forgets the account (`files::forget`), going on past whatever fails:

1. The account's file is written holding no account, so that the account cannot be read again and a start that finds Signal's files without an account finishes what this leaves (`device::LinkState::Leftovers`).
2. Every other file of Signal's goes, a half-written `.new` among them, and the folders `sessions`, `sender-keys`, `conversations` and `attachments` go with them, so that the names of their files, contacts' and groups' ids and the plaintexts of attachments, go too.
3. What else the partition keeps of the account goes: the uses of `SIGNAL` and of `CALLS` (`uses::SIGNAL_USE`, whose lines take the calls' with them, `uses::without_event`) from `.r1metal/analytics/uses` (`Keeper::uses`); the ledger's Signal kinds, its auto replies, messages said and readings aloud, each month keeping what it cost in all (`ledger::SIGNAL`, `Ledger::forget`, [OpenAI](openai.md#ledger)); and what tells of Signal in the logs ([Logs](../firmware/logs.md#unlink)).
4. The free blocks are handed back to the medium (`FITRIM`, `Data::trim`). fscrypt draws each file's key from the master key and a nonce kept with the file's inode, so a removed and trimmed file leaves nothing its key could be drawn from.
5. Where nothing failed, the account's file goes; where anything did, the log tells how many and the first error, the file stays, and the next start, which finds it, forgets again.

A start that finds Signal's files without an account that can be read, an unlink cut short or a link that ended part way, forgets them the same way before anything else. The screens let go of the conversations as the unlink is heard.

## Privacy

A linked R1 holds the account's identity keys and receives every one-to-one and group message, so whoever reads its data partition can read the owner's messages and send as the owner until the phone unlinks it: Signal, Settings, Linked devices. Once it has, nothing of the account stays on the partition ([Unlink](#unlink)). A backup holds Signal's files as the partition does, in an age file under the safe's passphrase, and whoever opens it holds the account the same way until the phone unlinks the R1; an unlink takes nothing out of a backup. An R1 a backup is restored to is the linked device the backup's R1 was, by its device ID, password and keys, and only one of the two may run with the account ([Storage](../firmware/storage.md#backup-and-restore)). OpenAI receives the text of a message said for a draft (not a voice note kept as one), the messages read aloud, and in auto mode the conversation's last 50 messages with the contact's name, each only while that runs; and where the Signal tool is on, what the model reads of the conversations with `read_signal`, which is other people's words. Messages to contacts and groups go sealed wherever Signal Desktop seals them, so the service learns who sent them only where the recipient refused the R1's sealing and no group's endorsement stands for it.

## Proof

Against outside sources, funcore's tests hold the R1's own ML-KEM-768 key generation and its encapsulation made in two halves to NIST's ACVP vectors (vector set 42, test group 2, case 26 of `ML-KEM-keyGen-FIPS203` and `ML-KEM-encapDecap-FIPS203`), Keccak to FIPS 202, the curve arithmetic to RFC 8032 and RFC 7748, a chain's message keys and next key to libsignal's `test_chain_key_derivation`, and an access key to zkgroup's `access_key_kat`; impshell's hold X25519 and an XEdDSA signature to libsignal's `test_agreement` and `test_signature` under AWS-LC, every bit of the signature changed refused, XEdDSA signatures to AWS-LC's Ed25519 under the converted key, AES-256-GCM-SIV to RFC 8452, and have AWS-LC decapsulate ML-KEM-768's split encapsulation and Kyber1024's. The keys and the access key are reckoned under a library of the tests whose SHA-512, SHA-256, SHA-1, HMAC-SHA256, HKDF-SHA256 and AES-256 in CTR mode are the standards' own (`signal::tests::Exact`), each held to the standard's vectors: FIPS 180-4, RFC 4231, RFC 5869, FIPS 197 and NIST SP 800-38A. Under it Signal's own server certificate checks against its trust root, and a signature another key made is refused wherever one is checked: a server certificate no trust root signed, a sender certificate the server's key did not sign, a pre-key bundle another identity signed, and a sender key message another key signed.

One funcore test (`signal/sealed.rs`) runs a session between two devices over a made-up library (`signal::tests::Standin`): X25519 by funcore's curve arithmetic, Keccak in place of the hashes and ciphers, ML-KEM-768 by funcore's own encapsulation, and a toy Kyber1024. For 100 rounds it begins the session by PQXDH with a one-time pre-key, sends PreKey messages until the first answer, turns the Double Ratchet every round with messages that come out of order or never, takes the post-quantum ratchet through two epochs, seals every message with a sender certificate by the R1's own sealing, two rounds in version 1 and two in version 2 by turns, and reads both sessions back from their records after every round. It then refuses a message changed on its way and one that comes twice. Another opens a sender key message sealed in version 1 and refuses it changed on its way, opened with another key, sealed under an identity key its certificate does not name, or of a version not known. The made-up SHA-512 is all noughts, so every XEdDSA signature verifies: these tests check no signature, trust root or revocation, which the exact library's do, and the formats they exchange are the R1's own on both sides.

The attachments' tests hold, each rule on its own: the padding to Desktop's `logPadSize` for sizes from 0 to 100,000,000 bytes, the sealing's round trip and each check that refuses (a MAC, a digest, a size past what was held, a body that is no whole block), the form's answer, an upload by TUS step by step with its resume and the offset that ends it, a header the form names that the request sets going once, a resumable upload step by step with its query and the range it resumes from, each end (refused, broken, tried the most times), a download kept, not kept, not checked and refused, the pointer's round trip and its place on each CDN, the largest file Signal's apps send and one a byte larger refused, a message `Sending` until each file went up and failed when one is refused, a video that goes up from the roll's file and a note kept beside the other attachments, the files nothing holds, the pictures' sizes with the orientations, the place a photo is given before its picture, the list's NEW MESSAGE and the contacts, a photo sent from the roll and the move into its message's own file, the voice notes' playing, stopping and ending, and the robot's questions and answers.

zkgroup's arithmetic is tested against the vectors its sources publish under the exact library: Ristretto255 against RFC 9496's appendix, Elligator's map and Lizard against curve25519-dalek's tests, Sho and a poksho proof against poksho's, an id's encryption and the group's generator points against zkgroup's, and a whole presentation of the auth credential, issued by a server the test makes as zkgroup makes its own, byte for byte against zkgroup's `AUTH_CREDENTIAL_PRESENTATION_V4_RESULT`. Group send endorsements are issued by such a server and received with their proof, and the token they make is the one the server computes. Each rule taken from Desktop is tested on its own as Desktop's sources have it: Note to Self, timers and transcripts, a message's disappearing at its time, read syncs and receipts, the owner's messages delivered and read, deletions for everyone and by an administrator, deletions for the owner alone, reactions given, changed and taken back, what comes before its message, mentions and replies, the list's pins, archive, marks and mutes, its order through a clock set back, a millisecond shared and a start, the account's keys, a new number and a contact's proof of its own, requests to send again both ways and the send log, identity trust and safety numbers, blocks, message requests, groups' rules, sealed sender and its fail-over, sender keys and the message sealed to many with the service's answers, the endorsements' use, the pre-keys' upkeep, the calls' gate, log, syncs, signals, relays and tokens, and the unlink, half-written files, a failure on the way and a start that finishes it included.
