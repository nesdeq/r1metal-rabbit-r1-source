# Chat

The robot holds one continuous conversation with the device's owner. It answers with the model and reasoning its settings set (`gpt-6.1-terra` at `high` on a new device, [OpenAI](openai.md#language-models)), uses the tools the owner switched on ([Tools](#tools)), keeps a memory of the owner, and speaks its replies. It needs only a working OpenAI key.

| Code | Does |
|---|---|
| `r1_funcore::domain::chat` | instructions, memory, parsing, state shared by the threads |
| `r1_funcore::openai::chat` | request body and response stream |
| `r1_funcore::ui::screen::chat` | the screen ([Interface](interface.md#chat)) |
| `crates/impshell/src/chat/` | the threads |
| `crates/impshell/src/openai/chat.rs` | OpenAI requests ([OpenAI](openai.md#the-conversations-turns)) |

## A turn

1. **Recording.** Holding the button records into a file in memory (`sound::Memo`, [Media](../firmware/media.md#sound)), as the browser, the Pokédex, the bell's request, the rundowns' and Signal's dictation do (`r1_funcore::domain::voice`). From then on the `turns` thread holds the network through the key, and in that stay the connection the recording's words go over is made ahead, as for every job said ([OpenAI](openai.md#account)); the conversation also opens its WebSocket ahead ([OpenAI](openai.md#the-conversations-turns)). A recording still going after 15 s (`NETWORK_HOLD_NS`) lets the network go, and it is taken again once the recording is handed over. Away from the chat screen, recording starts when the button goes down, but the network is held only once the press lasts `HOLD_TO_TALK_NS` ([Style guide](style-guide.md#10-controls)) (`Bid::Overhear`, then `Bid::Listen`); a click discards the audio (`Bid::Drop`). That holds at home, on the stages and menus and on every screen where a hold does nothing else (`Ui::holds_to_talk`): not in the video viewfinder, the translator, a keyboard, the window that scans, the safe's or the bell's window, the browser, the Pokédex or a Signal conversation, nor on the notes while one records or plays. On the bell's list the press records the same way, but for the bell: the hold sets or cancels a ring and opens no conversation ([Interface](interface.md#the-bell)); on a ring's page it talks to the robot. On the rundowns' list it records for the rundowns the same way, and on a rundown's page it talks to the robot ([Interface](interface.md#rundowns)). In Rail a press beside the Pokédex, the browser or the bell's list in the pane records for that screen the same way, and the hold gives it the wheel (`Ui::talk_screen`). The press that wakes the device counts too (`Ui::press_waking`); one that woke the browser, a Pokédex or a Signal conversation leaves it first, a conversation's draft kept, but for a Pokédex or the browser beside Rail's rail, over which the conversation opens. The press that lights a screen darkened during a task is the same press ([Interface](interface.md#light-and-sleep)): it clicks nothing, and held it records from the press on for the voice a hold on the screen that shows records for, the conversation's on the chat screen, the browser's, the Pokédex's and a Signal conversation's on theirs, and none where a hold does not talk (`Ui::talk_screen`). The conversation opens as the hold reaches its length, and going back returns to the screen the hold was on, in Rail the rail to the line it stood on, `CHAT`'s meanwhile (`Ui::return_from_chat`). On the chat screen a press stops the reply's voice, the press that woke the device or lit the screen once it is held. When muted, nothing is recorded: messages are typed on the keyboard, up to 500 characters (`MAX_TEXT_CHARS`), and a click sends them.
2. **Transcription.** Releasing the button ends the recording. `gpt-transcribe` receives it with `stream` true and, as `prompt`, the user's and the robot's names so both are spelled correctly. Words appear as they arrive (`transcript.text.delta`, `transcript.text.done`), and the bar shows `TRANSCRIBING` from the moment the recording is handed over. An empty transcript sends no message. A recording with under 0.1 s of audio after the encoder delay goes unsent and the bar shows `NOT HEARD` (`recording_seconds` in `r1_funcore::openai::transcript`): the service reportedly rejects shorter audio (LangChain issue #11449).
3. **Message.** Each message starts with local time, the city from the place service, and how it was entered: `[Friday 16 October 2026, 14:02, Vienna, spoken]`. If the user cut the last reply short (button, swipe away, mute, sleep, voice failure), the message adds the last sentence they heard, or that they heard none (`to_message` in `r1_funcore::domain::chat::message`); a message that comes to no words, an empty transcript or one that could not be written down, leaves that for the next (`Turns::give_back`).
4. **Request.** Over the WebSocket (`response_create` in `r1_funcore::openai::chat::request`), unless the month's cost is at the owner's limit ([OpenAI](openai.md#spending-limit)). It carries: the robot's model and effort (`Task::Chat`), verbosity and instructions (below); as `input`, the items new since the socket's last response; the tools; `include` of `reasoning.encrypted_content` and `web_search_call.action.sources`; `context_management` with compaction at 200,000 tokens; `prompt_cache_options` with mode `implicit` and ttl `12h`, so OpenAI keeps the prompt cached for at least 12 hours; `store` false. The tools are web search while the web tool is on (`search_context_size` `high`, `return_token_budget` `unlimited`, `external_web_access`, and an approximate `user_location` when the place service has one) and the functions of the tools that are on (`tools::functions`), always in the same order so the prompt cache holds. Each round of a turn takes the tools as they are when it begins.
5. **Response.** Text shows as it streams in; a search shows `SEARCHING`. The marks the model cites pages by (U+E200 `cite` U+E202 `turn0search4` U+E201), which no font draws, are taken out of what shows, what the day file keeps and what is said, each with the blanks before it; one still coming shows nothing until it closes (`strip_citations` in `r1_funcore::openai::output`). The reply is split into passages for the voice: the first sentence first (ended by `.`, `!`, `?` or `…` after a word of at least two letters and before a space), then line by line, without citations, URLs or Markdown marks (`r1_funcore::domain::chat::passage`, `r1_funcore::domain::paper::markdown`).
6. **Storage.** Every output item goes into the thread, encrypted reasoning included. The day file gets the searches, the reply and the cited pages; the ledger gets the cost. Every function call gets an output: what a call has the turn do, and its answer and act where they need no reading, is `calls::to_step`. Another request follows if the model waits for a call's output (`search_memory`, `read_device`, `forget`, `set_reminder`, `cancel_reminder`, `read_signal`, `draft_signal`, an unknown function or one of a tool that is off), or if a response with function calls has no text (`continues_turn`).
7. **End.** What `use_device`, `change_setting`, `change_rundowns`, `forget` and `draft_signal` asked runs after the turn (`Ui::do_act`); what `set_reminder` and `cancel_reminder` asked is done as they are answered. The bar shows `HOLD TO TALK` again, or why the turn failed.

A message that arrives during a turn waits for it. A response in progress is not cancelled, since it is billed anyway and its usage arrives only at the end; its voice stops and its text is kept. A turn fails after 120 s without data from the service (`models::TIMEOUT`).

## Instructions

In order: `robot.txt`, the user's tone, the reasoning modules ([OpenAI](openai.md#models)), the onboarding plan while onboarding runs (`onboarding.txt`), the memory, and the recollections of the last seven days before today that have one, however far back (`RECALL_DAYS`, `recall_days`). `robot.txt` covers the robot's identity and name (or that it has none); talk by default, full agent only on a real task; the tools, where `{TOOLS}` stands for the section of `tools.txt` of each tool that is on, then the tools that are off by name with where the settings switch them on, then what the device lacks (updates over WiFi, Bluetooth) (`to_tools_text`); when to save, search and forget memory; the message format; reply style (spoken: for the ear, no lists, tables, links, code, headings or emoji; typed: as brief, bold for names and figures; about 38 characters per screen line, no emoji); and the voice: Cormac McCarthy's economy, no preamble, no closing menu.

The instructions are rebuilt only when the prompt cache has expired (12 hours after the last request, `CACHE_DURATION`), after compaction, after a past day is settled into the memory, after something is forgotten, when a tool is switched (`warm_text` holds the tools they were told with), and when the robot is named, gets a tone or finishes onboarding. A rebuild asked while a turn holds the instructions comes as the turn gives them back (`Turns::mark_stale`). Otherwise each request repeats the previous text, so the instructions and the thread up to the new message are served from cache; a fact saved meanwhile is in the thread as its call.

## The thread

The service stores nothing. The device keeps the whole thread in `.r1metal/chat/thread`, one item per line, exactly as the service sent it, user messages and function outputs included. A request carries only the items new since the socket's last response, all of them where the socket holds none ([OpenAI](openai.md#the-conversations-turns)). Above 200,000 tokens (`COMPACT_THRESHOLD`, below the 272,000 where the model's price rises) the service compacts earlier items into one compaction item; the thread becomes that item plus what follows, and the file is rewritten. A line that is not a complete item, such as one a power cut leaves behind, is skipped on reading and the file is rewritten without it.

## Memory

Two Markdown files, included in every request:

| File | Content |
|---|---|
| `chat/core.md` | `## You`: the onboarding profile, one labelled line per area (`**Movies:** Heat; Alien; Ran`, or `rather not say`); `## The robot`: its name, who gave it and when, and its tone; `## Always`: facts that always hold for the user |
| `chat/knowledge.md` | facts learned, one line each with its date: `- 2026-10-16: The balcony faces west.` |

`save_memory` adds a fact to either file and deletes the one line that contains the `replaces` text. If several lines contain it, nothing is saved or deleted and the output says so. `search_memory` ranks the facts that always hold and those learned (not the profile's lines nor the robot's, which every request carries), every message said or typed and every reply of every day file (not its searches, its sources or a photo's label) and, while the notes tool is on, every note's summary and words and every translation's summary (`recall::read_papers`) with Okapi BM25 (`k1` 1.2, `b` 0.75, the standard values from Robertson and Zaragoza) and returns the best ten (`HIT_COUNT`), with dates; an empty query returns the facts learned last (`r1_funcore::domain::chat::recall`).

**Forgetting.** `forget` names what goes: `thread`, a day as `YYYYMMDD` whose words are kept, or text that one fact alone holds, whatever its case (`calls::to_forget_answer`); anything else is answered why not. It deletes nothing: once the reply is over the line a click forgets it on opens, as a hand reaches it (`Act::Forget`): the fact's or the day's page at `FORGET`, or `FORGET` under `ROBOT` ([Interface](interface.md#the-robots-memory)). A click there forgets it: a fact leaves its file, a day's `chat/talk/yyyymmdd.md` and `chat/days/yyyymmdd.md` go, or the thread is emptied; the free blocks are handed back to the medium (`Data::trim`) and the instructions rebuilt. A day forgotten while it settles keeps nothing of the settling, and a fact forgotten meanwhile stays forgotten (`Memory::apply_recollection`). The log names no fact (`to_forgotten_line`).

**Recollection.** When the chat screen opens, every past day without a recollection is sent in the background to the recollection's model and reasoning (`RECOLLECTION MODEL`, `RECOLLECTION REASONING`; `gpt-6.1-terra` at `high` on a new device) with the memory (`recollect.txt`). The response has three sections: `## Day` (what the day held), `## Always` (what always holds from now on) and `## Knowledge` (what is worth knowing later), all under 4,000 words. The last two replace the memory, with facts saved in the meantime added back; `## Day` goes into `chat/days/yyyymmdd.md`. A response without the three sections is stored as the day's recollection and the memory stays unchanged. Days are processed one after another under one hold, which is released before the last day's result is stored. Sleep stops a new day from starting; a day in progress finishes.

## Onboarding

A conversation never held starts with the robot: the screen opens, the robot wakes (`WAKING UP`) and speaks first. Its plan comes from 16 random bytes in `.r1metal/chat/state`, drawn as the conversation's files are first read where they keep no state, so it survives restarts, differs between devices, and is the same for every turn, a message from the console before the screen ever opened among them (`r1_funcore::domain::chat::onboarding`). The plan picks one of eight openers (`openers.txt`), an order for the nine areas after the name (age, place, languages, work, interests, movies, games, music, other media), and three of sixteen playful devices (`games.txt`).

The robot asks one thing at a time and responds to each answer with something of its own. After the name it offers to be named or to choose a name, then asks how it should sound, offering some of ten tones (`tones.txt`: witty, warm, dry, playful, calm, babbly, blunt, cheeky, gentle, nerdy); several may be chosen. `profile` records each area, or that the user declined, and its output lists what is known and what is still open. Onboarding is complete with the name, the naming, the tone and six of the nine areas (`ENOUGH_AREAS`); a list area counts once it has three picks (`ENOUGH_PICKS`), and a declined area counts too. The robot then wraps up and jumps for joy. The user can stop at any time; `end_onboarding` ends onboarding for good.

## Tools

What the robot may use beyond its memory, each switched on or off under `FEATURES`, `ROBOT`, `TOOLS` ([Interface](interface.md#settings)), all but Signal on for a new device (`Tools::default`). Kept as `tool WORD on|off` ([Storage](../firmware/storage.md#layout)). A tool that is off declares nothing, a call of its function is answered as unknown (`parse_tool_call`), and the instructions name it as off.

| Tool | Word | Gives |
|---|---|---|
| `WEB SEARCH` | `web` | the web search |
| `DEVICE` | `device` | `use_device`, `change_setting`, `set_reminder`, `cancel_reminder`, `change_rundowns`; `read_device` of the battery, the bell, the cost, the about screen and the settings |
| `NOTES` | `notes` | `read_device` of the notes and translations; `search_memory` over them |
| `NEWS` | `news` | `read_device` of the editions |
| `CAMERA` | `camera` | the photos the owner shows it ([Photos](#photos)) |
| `SIGNAL` | `signal` | `read_signal`, `draft_signal` ([Signal](signal.md#the-robots-tool)) |

## Functions

Every function is `strict`. The memory's come first, then `read_device` while a tool it reads is on, then `use_device`, `change_setting`, `set_reminder`, `cancel_reminder` and `change_rundowns` while the device tool is on, then Signal's.

| Function | Does | Model waits for output |
|---|---|---|
| `save_memory` | saves a fact, core or not, replacing the one it corrects | no |
| `search_memory` | searches the memory, every day and, while the notes tool is on, the notes and translations | yes |
| `profile` | records an onboarding area, or that it was declined | no |
| `name_robot` | sets the robot's name, or none; letters, digits, spaces, `-` and `'`, at most 21 characters (`NAME_MAX_CHARS`: 342 px of the bar beside the robot at 16 px per character) | no |
| `set_tone` | sets how the robot and its voice sound, one tone or several | no |
| `end_onboarding` | ends onboarding | no |
| `forget` | opens a fact, a day or the thread where a click forgets it ([Memory](#memory)) | yes |
| `read_device` | of what the tools that are on give: lists notes or translations, or returns one day's in full with summaries; returns a day's news edition, the latest by default, each rundown it holds under its name (`read::to_edition`); returns what OpenAI's services were paid, read from the book and told as the cost screen's lines, each page's lines under the line that opens it; returns what the device runs and is as the about screen tells it, but for what names this one device, the IMEI and the hardware addresses, which go to nobody but the screen (`Fact::is_identity`); returns every setting by the name `change_setting` knows it by, brightness, volume, sounds, theme and colors first (`Settings::to_readout`); returns the battery state as the power screen shows it, or every alarm, reminder and timer that is set with its day and time on the device's clock, which the interface reads out through the main loop (`Readout`, `crates/impshell/src/chat/read.rs`) | yes |
| `use_device` | runs one of 52 actions. It opens each of the 28 screens the tree opens (`Destination`): the photo or video camera, pictures, videos, notes, translations, news, the rundowns' list (`open_rundowns`, [Interface](interface.md#rundowns)), the web browser (`open_web`, [Interface](interface.md#web)), Signal, Signal's calls (`open_calls`, [Interface](interface.md#calls)), the Pokédex where the device has it, wifi, brightness, volume, themes, colors, clock, the bell's list (`open_reminders`), the window that changes the passphrase (`open_passphrase`), the keys of the SIM's PIN (`open_sim_pin`), the window that scans a tunnel's config (`open_tunnel`, [Interface](interface.md#mobile-data)), cost, power, about, the debug screen once it is unlocked (`open_debug`), what it keeps of the user (`open_memory`) or the days of the conversation (`open_days`). It records a note; starts the translator; sets brightness, volume or the interface's own sounds in percent, the theme by name (`stage`, `dial`, `deck`, `terminal`, `companion`, `rail`), the colors by letter `A` to `P` or the clock's offset from UTC as `+HH:MM` or `-HH:MM`, one a time zone uses (`set_clock_offset`, `offset::parse_text`); goes home; sleeps. Nothing that deletes, forgets or powers off, and nothing that is typed or scanned: a passphrase, a PIN, a password, a key and a tunnel's config are the owner's to give on the screen it opens | no |
| `set_reminder` | has the bell ring ([Interface](interface.md#the-bell)): at a time of day `at` as `HH:MM` of the device's clock, on a day `on` as `YYYY-MM-DD` or, with `on` null, the next time the clock shows it; or `in_seconds` from now, with `at` and `on` null; `text` says what the ring is for, or is null. Any number may be set. The interface, which holds the bell and the clock, makes the change at once and answers through the main loop (`Asked::Bell`, `Bell::apply`): the moment that is set (`Set for friday 20 november 2026, 09:00, by the device's clock.`), so a day the model worked out wrongly shows in the same turn, or why nothing is set: the time is past, the same is set already, or the device does not know the time | yes |
| `cancel_reminder` | cancels what `which` names: text one entry alone holds, its time as `HH:MM` or `YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM`, the word `alarm`, `reminder` or `timer` where one alone is of that kind, or `all`. The answer says what was cancelled; where `which` names none or more than one, nothing is cancelled and it lists what is set | yes |
| `change_rundowns` | has the rundowns the daily news is made of changed: `request` is what the user wants in full and in plain words, a new rundown's subject, what to stress, the language, the length and the sources they name, or a rundown the device keeps changed, switched on or off, or deleted. | `change_setting` | sets one of the 37 settings set where their lines stand, the browser's `web_model` and `web_reasoning`, the bell's `bell_model` and `bell_reasoning`, the rundowns' request's `rundowns_model` and `rundowns_reasoning`, the drive's `usb_drive` and the router's `usb_modem` and `vpn` among them, by its name (`Setting::to_name`) and a value of its steps (`Setting::parse_value`); a value that is none of them is no call, and a rundown is no setting: `change_rundowns` switches one. A change is made once the reply is over, whatever it costs: a dearer model, more effort, a higher limit or none, the daily news, mobile data. One alone is staged on its line for the owner's click ([Interface](interface.md#settings)), the answer saying so (`calls::to_step`): a change that leaves the device less safe or less private (`Settings::is_eased`), which is a tool switched on, Signal's messages read aloud or its said messages written down, both by OpenAI, the SIM's PIN kept, the USB drive shown to a computer, the modem's connection lent to a computer, and the tunnel switched off, which sends that computer's packets in the open. The declaration's description, which tells the model the words of each value and the changes that wait, is the settings' own (`settings::to_change_description`), and a test holds what it lists as waiting to `Settings::is_eased` | no |
| `read_signal`, `draft_signal` | read Signal's conversations; open one with a draft a click sends ([Signal](signal.md#the-robots-tool)) | yes |

`use_device` reaches a screen or a menu as a hand would, from home through the stages or the theme's menus (`Ui::do_act`), so back and home behave as after manual navigation, and a screen's use is counted. With these, `change_setting`, `change_rundowns` and the bell's two functions the robot reaches every line of the tree but its own on/off switch, which no theme shows, and sets everything the settings set; what it does not do is the debug screen's lines, which a click works, the clock's hour and date, which the network sets, and its own muting, which a touch on it does. A new theme or palette plays the robot's admire animation (`Reaction::Admire`); a new theme re-lays everything behind the conversation, which stays open, and any other screen that shows gives way to home in it, but a screen of a setting; while the key window may not be left, no theme is set. A brightness, volume or level of the interface's sounds the conversation sets shows at once on its line of the settings; setting or cancelling an alarm, a reminder or a timer gives the notice ([Interface](interface.md#brightness-and-volume)). Opening a screen does not stop the voice; swiping away from the conversation does.

## Photos

While the camera tool is on, a touch on the robot in the photo viewfinder takes a photo for the robot alone (`Beckon::Look`, `Action::Look`): developed and made 1024x1024 as the pixel cam's copy is, it is saved nowhere, sent to no pixel cam and told by no note (`camera::Shot`, [Camera](camera.md#viewfinders)). The viewfinder stays until it comes, stopped on the frame it showed (`Ui::shut_finder`), its stream at rest while the photo is developed ([Camera](../firmware/camera.md#photo)); then the conversation opens over it, going back returning to the viewfinder, and the photo goes to the `chat` thread among the screen's bids (`Ui::take_shot`, `Chore::Show`). Until the photo comes the robot takes no other touch there; a photo that failed, leaving the viewfinder or a sleep ends the wait, and a photo that comes after is dropped. With the camera tool off the robot neither glows nor takes a touch there. A hold in the photo viewfinder talks as everywhere.

The photo waits for the next message, said or typed: `photo attached` stands at the foot of the conversation (`Report.has_photo`). The message takes it as it is handed over (`Turns::take_photo`, as a cut-short reply's number is), so a recording too short to write down leaves it waiting, and a message that comes to no words gives it back where no newer photo waits (`Turns::give_back`); leaving the conversation lets go of it (`Chore::Hush`), and so does the camera tool switched off meanwhile. A newer photo takes the place of one that waits. That turn's requests send the message with the photo after its words, as `input_image` with a `data:image/jpeg;base64,` URL and `detail` `high` (`chat::request::user_message`, `models::chat::DETAIL`), the words telling it (`[With this message, a photo the user took with the device's camera.]`, `message::to_shown_messages`). The thread keeps the message without the photo, saying the robot saw it in that turn, so no later request carries it; the turn's requests send it by its place in the thread (`Thread::add_shown`, `Thread::to_sent`), so two messages of the same words go each with its own photo. The day's file gets a `shown` entry, a label without text, and the conversation shows `photo shown` at the side.

## Voice

A spoken reply is one reading by `gpt-realtime-2.1-mini`; passages are added as the reply grows ([Audio](audio.md#reading-aloud)). The session opens when the turn starts, while the model thinks. Its instructions are `voice.txt` (say each passage word for word, as to someone you know, never answer it), with the user's tone affecting delivery only (`to_voice_instructions` in `r1_funcore::domain::chat::instructions`). When muted, replies are not spoken. The reader reports how many passages played to the end; a cut-short message reports those as heard.

## Searching sound

While the robot searches the web for a reply, a sound plays round and round at the level of the interface's own sounds (the setting `sounds`, 40 % on a new device, set on the volume screen; [Interface](interface.md#brightness-and-volume)).

- **When.** While the conversation shows `SEARCHING` in the voice, the device awake and the interface's sounds above silence (`Ui::does_search_sound`), from the sound's first sample each time. Muted, once the reply is read out (`READING`), or with the interface's sounds at 0, it is silent.
- **How loud.** At the level of the interface's sounds, whatever the volume.
- **The speaker.** It plays aside (`sound::play_aside`): a speaker in use is left to whoever has it, and the moment another player waits for the speaker, the reply's voice with its first sound among them, the card plays out what it holds (85 ms at the most) and the speaker goes to the other. The reader holds the speaker from a reply's first sound to its last passage, so a search in the middle of a spoken reply is silent.
- **The file.** `crates/funcore/assets/chat/searching.m4a` (`chat::search_sound`), carried in the firmware: one channel of our AAC at 48 kHz, in whole frames of 1024 samples so that its last sample runs into its first.

## Files and threads

| File | Content |
|---|---|
| `chat/talk/yyyymmdd.md` | a day's conversation as plain Markdown, under the safe's key as every file of the owner's ([Storage](../firmware/storage.md#the-safe)): a head, then every message, search, reply and source list in order, each under a comment line naming the author and time (`<!-- you spoken 14:02 -->`; also `you typed`, `shown`, `robot`, `searched`, `sources`) and a bold label; appended entry by entry (`Data::append`) |
| `chat/days/yyyymmdd.md` | the day's recollection |
| `.r1metal/chat/state` | the plan's seed in hex, `onboarded`, `muted` |
| `.r1metal/chat/thread` | the thread, one item per line |

The conversation's files are read once the data partition is available. If a file exists but cannot be read, the conversation stops, so nothing overwrites it. Nothing deletes these files but a click on `FORGET` ([Memory](#memory)).

| Thread | Does |
|---|---|
| `chat` | takes the screen's requests and records; keeps the photo for the robot until a message takes it (`Chore::Show`); lists the facts and the days for their screens (`Chore::List`) and forgets (`Chore::Forget`) |
| `turns` | transcribes, sends, stores and answers |
| `recall` | writes recollections |

All three run at the lowest priority (`sys::spawn_background`). The device does not sleep while a recording or a turn runs. In sleep a turn or a recollection in progress may still use the network until it finishes (`Chat::is_owing`), and nothing new starts. The log records which function the model called and, of a call that has the device act, the act with its value (a percent, a theme, a setting and its value, a day to forget: `to_act_line`), never the words of an argument: what is searched for, a fact, a name, a draft or a request; and how many characters were transcribed, never the conversation's words.

## Cost

Booked under `CHAT` as `TURNS` and `WEB SEARCHES` ([OpenAI](openai.md#ledger)). A turn's first response counts as one turn with its cost. The turn's later responses, its transcription and voice, and recollections add their cost to `TURNS` without counting a turn. Each web search counts once at its price; pages opened count nothing.
