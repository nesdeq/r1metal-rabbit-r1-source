## Camera session

The camera runs only while a viewfinder shows, or a call sends this side's picture or the console holds a call's camera ([A call's picture](#a-calls-picture)), and draws its picture at the size the interface lays out for it (`Viewfinder::size`). Leaving the viewfinder or going to sleep ends the session: every part powers down, a photo whose burst waits for a buffer is given up, and the session's spare burst buffers, the picture kept for the screen and a call's picture are let go of (`camera::State::end_run`), while whether a call wants pictures stands for the next run (`camera::State::want_call_pictures`); bursts already taken go on developing in the darkroom ([Photo](#photo)).

Power up, in order:

1. Open `/dev/camera-isp` first. A dying process closes descriptors in ascending order, so this one closes first and stops CAMSV0's DMA; every other camera descriptor is opened above its number.
2. Open `/dev/kd_camera_hw`, and `/dev/ion` above the camera's node (`Isp::ion`), so that every buffer of the stream lies above it.
3. Switch translation on for larb 3.
4. Turn the camera and wait for the turn to end; then power the sensor and write its init table. The motor's and the sensor rails' inrush at once pull a nearly empty cell under the PMIC's undervoltage lockout ([Power](../hardware/power.md#battery-in-the-kernel)).
5. Program the receiver, with the trim from `/proc/device-tree/chosen/atag,devinfo` (the reset trim if that cannot be read), and set preview, slim video or, for a call, high speed mode (`develop::VIEW_MODE`, `recording::MODE`, `Mode::HighSpeed`, by the viewfinder, `Viewfinder::mode`).
6. Allocate one ION buffer with three frame slots and a header page.
7. Start streaming, pass the receiver health check, start CAMSV0.

Steps 3 to 6 and the finder that draws the viewfinder's part of the frame (`Viewfinder::view`, `Viewfinder::frame_size`) are one sequence for both viewfinders (`Rig::bring_up`).

Power down: stock's CAMSV0 stop sequence (up to ten vsyncs for the block to come to rest, `camsv::Rest`), streaming suspend, receiver off, analog words back to reset, sensor off, nodes closed. The stream's slots (`frames::Frames`, as a burst's) belong to the run beside its rig (`run_session`) and are freed after it, so CAMSV0 has stopped before any of them goes, whatever ends the run. A burst's buffer comes from the same ION and goes to the darkroom once CAMSV0 has stopped after the capture; the darkroom keeps it for the session's next burst until the session ends, and lets go of one of an earlier session once its burst is developed (`Darkroom::give_back`). The swivel turns the camera into the case (90°) when no viewfinder is wanted or the session failed; switching viewfinder or camera leaves it out. After a failed session none begins until no viewfinder is wanted (`camera::State::serve`), so the viewfinder stays dark until it closes. A power up the battery's reserve refuses (`camera::Start::Refuse`) ends as a failed session does, before any part has power, and tells `BATTERY TOO LOW` ([Battery](battery.md#camera-reserve)).

- Click to viewfinder bit: the turn, then the sensor's power up and init. A turn that takes over four times the driver's own time (10 ms spin up plus 2.46 ms per degree) fails.
- Frames: all three slots are queued; each slot is queued again once drawn. When more than one slot is full, the older ones are queued again unseen (`camsv::BufferQueue`). One fixed header page receives every frame header.
- Receiver health check: once the sensor streams, the receiver is read with a pause of 1 ms between two reads until it has seen a whole frame, for at most four frame times in pauses (`stream::timeout`, `seninf::await_stream`); its status is cleared and it is read the same way again, and the frames it then reports must be clean and of the mode's size (`Health::sees`).
- The session fails when a frame does not complete within four frame times, or when `/proc/driver/cl_cam_status` reads `1` (overheat, checked every 30 frames: half a second, a second in a call, `stream::is_look_frame`, `hw::camera::is_too_warm`). A file that cannot be read stops no stream; the log tells it once, and again only after a read that worked (`Rig::check_warmth`).
- Suspend waits until the camera thread is off (session over, camera parked) and the darkroom has nothing to do and holds no encoder (`camera::State::is_off`). The viewfinder resumes after wake.
- The JPEG encoder takes one claimant at a time ([Codecs and buffers](../hardware/codecs.md#jpeg-encoder)), and it is the darkroom's alone: the darkroom claims it for the first burst it develops or copy it makes for a redraw ([Photo](#photo)), `/proc/mtk_jpeg` opened and `INIT` called, with the buffers a photo passes through from `/dev/ion` opened above the encoder's node (`PhotoBuffers`, `Jpeg::ion`); a video or scanning session never claims it. It runs `CONFIG`, `START`, `WAIT` any number of times under that one `INIT`, and is released once no burst, copy or art waits (`Work::Release`), before its buffers are freed. Each job takes and releases the codec lock through a `/dev/Vcodec` opened for that job above the encoder's node.

## 60 fps streaming

Both viewfinders stream at 60.0075 fps with frames of 1263 lines (`Pace::LIVE`): shorter than preview's 2526, longer than slim video's 842. A call's viewfinder alone streams at 30 ([A call's picture](#a-calls-picture)). As the driver never shortens a frame ([Camera](../hardware/camera.md#driver-calls)), after the mode call the firmware writes the frame length register 0x0006 directly (`SENSOR_FEATURE_SET_REGISTER`; `Rig::configure`, `Pace::overrides_driver`, registers in `r1_funcore::hw::camera::mode`). Until the next mode call, exposure is written to register 0x0074 the same way (`Sensor::expose`, by the sensor's own record of a length set past the driver), and gain goes through the driver's call, which leaves the frame length alone.

60 fps limits the picture size. The sensor reads a fixed number of lines per second ([Camera](../hardware/camera.md#sensor)), and with the device upright a picture's width runs along sensor lines. So 2448 px across allows 30 fps, 1224 px across allows 60 fps, and 1280 px across at 60 fps is impossible.

| Aspect | Implementation |
|---|---|
| Cores | the camera thread and seven `Crew` threads are each pinned to a core, the first four on the little cores (`hw::CORES`). A viewfinder picture is drawn in four bands of whole lines (`hw::FRAME_PARTS`, `gfx::bands`), an encoder picture in eight parts of whole line pairs (`hw::PARTS`, `base::planes::parts`). The darkroom's crew of seven is pinned the same way and runs each stage of a burst in parts (`burst::Burst::parts`): the frames' unpacking, the merge and its fine samples cut by lines into eight parts or more, the pyramids a part a frame, the alignment a part a frame but the reference, the noise one part |
| Scheduling | the camera's eight threads are `SCHED_FIFO`, one priority below the drawing thread, with a minimum utilisation of 262/1024 (`MIN_LOAD`). That keeps their cores at 745 MHz or more; schedutil alone leaves a little core at 400 MHz. The darkroom's eight are ordinary at nice 19 (`sys::spawn_background`), so a viewfinder frame takes its core from them at once and keeps its 60 fps while a burst develops |
| Sync with the panel | 1263 lines last 16.665 ms; the panel refreshes about every 16.670 ms. `Genlock` adjusts the frame length, by at most 16 lines, so that each picture is ready 6 ms after a flip (`READY_AFTER_FLIP_NS`), reckoning with the frames a change of length takes to show ([Camera](../hardware/camera.md#driver-calls)). Pictures are shown once the stream is in sync, or after 24 frames of searching (0.4 s, 0.8 s in a call) |
| Drawing | the main loop draws when a picture arrives ([Display](display.md#timing)) |
| Allocation | `Crew` runs a frame's parts without boxing; a viewfinder frame allocates no memory |

The photo viewfinder shows the middle 1224x1224 of the preview frame at 480x480. It draws with lookup tables, each part covering a range of output lines (for the rotated picture, a range of sensor columns, which keeps a part's writes in the L1 cache).

With the device upright, the rear camera's picture (swivel 180°) needs a quarter turn clockwise and the front camera's (swivel 0°) three quarter turns. The camera flips over a horizontal axis on its way between the two, so the rear and front cameras see a device tilt in opposite directions. The front view is mirrored on screen, in the viewfinder, in a photo's review (`camera::Sight`) and in a call's own picture; photos, recordings and a call's picture for the far side are never mirrored. A click is accepted once the mode's settling frames have passed.

## Image pipeline

| Step | Method | `r1_funcore::hw::camera` |
|---|---|---|
| Lens shading | each raw sample is multiplied by its colour's gain at its position: the 17x17 grid of `shading::REFERENCE` ([Camera](../hardware/camera.md#tuning-data)), interpolated linearly, clipped at the sensor's maximum. Viewfinders, a recording's pictures and a call's apply it first; photos apply it after demosaicing, between half and all of its lift by the merge's signal-to-noise ratio (below). At a photo's corners the gains are 2.5 (red), 2.3 (green), 2.2 (blue) | `shading`, `raw`, `video`, `develop` |
| Metering | each viewfinder frame is summed in 16x16 zones (red, green, blue, clipped quads); every fourth row feeds the histogram | `raw`, `measure` |
| Exposure | targets a mean green of 18 % of the range, up to twice that when the brightest 1 % lies below 85 %. Exposure time alone up to one flicker period of 50 Hz mains (10 ms, 758 lines), then whole periods plus gain in steps of 1/16. A viewfinder exposes at most one period, a call's three ([A call's picture](#a-calls-picture)); a photo three, up to six (60 ms) once gain would exceed 4x. Beyond the maximum gain of 16x the picture is amplified in software, up to 8x | `exposure` |
| White balance | every fourth frame, a search along the sensor's line of whites (horizon to shade) and from 4.7 % below to 9.4 % above it. Zones count if their mean green is at least 16 codes and at most one quad in 16 is clipped. The white that brings these zones, weighted by green, closest to grey wins; each zone's error is capped, a warm zone's at half. Outdoors (exposure up to 16000 lines x 64ths of gain) no white warmer than 5000 K is allowed, indoors (from 160000) any, interpolated in between. With fewer than 16 grey zones the balance is kept. A stream's first frames set the balance directly; later frames move it 1/8 of the way | `balance` |
| Flare (`veil`) | the light of a frame's darkest 0.5 % counts as flare: four fifths of it are subtracted from every colour, at most 1/32 of the range | `exposure`, `tone` |
| Colour matrix | interpolated between the two nearest of Rabbit's five matrices by the ratio of red gain to blue gain. A photo takes all of it where its merged mid-grey stands 40 times above its noise and none of it at 10 times, the two signal-to-noise ratios of ISO 12232's noise-based speeds, in between in proportion (`develop::strength`) | `colour`, `develop` |
| Tone curve | sRGB with a contrast curve that keeps black, white and 18 % grey fixed and is 25 % steeper at that grey | `tone` |
| Local tone map | a burst taken below its light is fused with itself made as bright as asked, by Mertens' exposure fusion on its grey at the quads' resolution (well-exposedness alone, OpenCV's Gaussian of deviation 0.2, pyramids of `pyrDown` and `pyrUp`), and each quad's gain from the fusion lifts its light before the matrix, between quads bilinearly; the map is made once a burst, each of its passes (the two exposures, their weights, each pyramid level down and up, the gains) in bands of rows, a part for each of the darkroom's eight threads (`GainMap::new`, run by `Developer::new`'s caller) | `fusion`, `develop` |
| Sharpening | IPOL's three unsharp masks of the luma, deviations 1, 2 and 4 pixels, amounts 1, 0.5 and 0.5, each leaving detail under 5, 10 and 15 codes alone, or under three deviations of the photo's noise where that is more (the median of the finest diagonal Haar coefficients over 0.6745, Donoho and Johnstone) | `sharpen` |

Photos and viewfinders apply the matrix in linear light; recordings and a call's pictures apply it to the tone curve's output, folded into their tables. The viewfinder draws one sensor colour quad per screen pixel. For every photo the log records the white balance, flare, exposure, matrix and zones.

## Scanning

The viewfinder of the window that scans, for the OpenAI key ([Interface](../app/interface.md#the-openai-key)) or for a tunnel's config ([Interface](../app/interface.md#mobile-data)), is the photo viewfinder's stream drawn at 464x464 (`Ui::scan_side`). While the scanner waits for a picture, the camera thread copies each picture it draws into the scanner's slot and continues without waiting. Once no viewfinder scans, the scanner lets go of its pictures and buffers (`camera::ScanStep::Rest`). The scanner (thread `scan`, lowest priority) computes each pixel's luma by BT.601 and searches for a QR code (`r1_funcore::codec::qr::scan`) the way ZXing does:

1. **Binarisation.** For 8x8 pixel blocks, each block's threshold is its mean. A block whose darkest and brightest pixels differ by less than 24 has no edge; its threshold is half its darkest value, or the previous blocks' threshold where that is lighter. A pixel is dark when it is no lighter than the mean of the 5x5 block thresholds around its block.
2. **Finder patterns.** Runs along each row in the ratio 1:1:3:1:1, each within half a module, cross-checked down the column through their centre and along the row again, the cross-check's runs within 40 % of the row's. A finder counts once two rows have found it.
3. **Finder triples.** Of the 15 finders seen on the most rows (`MOST_FINDERS`, five symbols' worth, so a busy picture costs at most 455 triples), three that form a right angle with two equal sides, within 0.5 in angle and module size (`SKEW`); the closest to that shape is tried first.
4. **Sampling.** The alignment pattern nearest the position the finders predict, within 16 modules (`ALIGNMENT_REACH`), or that position itself; the modules are sampled through the perspective transform of the four points, at the size the finder distance gives, then one version smaller and larger, then at the size the symbol's own version bits give.
5. **Decoding** (`r1_funcore::codec::qr::read`): version and format by nearest codeword, mask removed, each block corrected by Reed-Solomon (Berlekamp-Massey, Forney), segments decoded. Mirrored codes are decoded too.

The decoded content goes to the main loop, which hands it to the window (`Ui::take_scan`) and logs its length under the tag of what the window took it for, `key` or `router`, and never its bytes (`scene::Scan::to_log_line`). The accelerometer is not used: nothing in the window rotates. The block size, 5x5, 24, half a module, two rows and 40 % are ZXing's values (ZXing cross-checks down a column); the skew, reach and 15 are r1metal's. Ignored tests check every version at every level against the host's `qrencode` and `zbarimg` (`cargo test -p r1-funcore -- --ignored qrencode`, `zbar`).

## A call's picture

A video call's own picture ([Interface](../app/interface.md#calls)) is a photo viewfinder that calls (`Viewfinder.is_calling`, given by `Ui::call_camera`): the sensor's high speed mode (`Mode::HighSpeed`), 640x480 of the middle 2560x1920 of the sensor, 78 % of its width and height ([Camera](../hardware/camera.md#sensor)), its frames set 2526 lines long past the driver, 30.0037 a second (`Pace::CALL`, `Viewfinder::pace`), as many as the far side is sent pictures, with exposures of up to three flicker periods before gain and none that stretches a frame. A session at another pace than the last takes the light the automatic last aimed at in that pace's exposure and lift (`Auto::restart`). It draws the whole frame (`Viewfinder::view`), upright, as wide as the call's screen names and 4/3 as tall (`Viewfinder::frame_size`), one quad a pixel, and meters all of it; the front camera's is mirrored, as each call begins looking at the owner, the wheel turning it over. It runs while the call sends this side's picture, from its way out on, or while the console holds it with no call (`camera on`, [Console](console.md#verbs)), its pictures then shown nowhere; and only while the device is awake and no viewfinder shows on the screen. It takes no click.

Pictures for the far side are made while a call wants them, a want the main loop sets at each pass (`Camera::want_call_pictures`, `camera::State::want_call_pictures`) and a run's end leaves standing, and no more than 30 a second: the stream takes the want at each frame, after the owner's picture shows, and makes a picture of a frame that, stamped with boot time, comes no earlier than half a turn before its turn, the turns 1/30 s apart (`take_picture_wish`, in `show_until_click`; ITU-T I.371's virtual scheduling). It makes the picture of the raw frame it drew (`camera/call.rs`, `Sender`, made at a run's first wish):

1. The frame becomes the picture's planes at its own size, 640x480 (`camera::CALL_SIZE`), as the sensor has it, by `hw::camera::video::Planes` as a recording's pictures are made: lens shading over the mode's window first, luma for every pixel and chroma for every quad, in Rec. 601's video range (`Planes::with_rec_601`, the matrix a stream that names none is read by, VP8's), Y, U and V apart (`video::Chroma::Apart`), in parts of whole line pairs on the camera's crew (`base::planes::parts_apart`).
2. The picture (`camera::CallPicture`) is stamped with boot time, the quarter turns that stand it upright as the device is held (`swivel::capture_turn`, never mirrored) and whether the rear camera looks, and handed over by a swap (`camera::State::put_call_picture`, `take_call_picture`); the main loop moves it to the calls' part ([Calls](../app/calls.md#pictures)).

## Photo

A photo is a burst of frames merged into one, as Google's HDR+ merges them (Hasinoff and others, 2016), by the parameters of IPOL's implementation (Monod and others, 2021). The camera thread takes the burst and goes on; the darkroom develops it.

1. Stop CAMSV0; set capture mode (`develop::MODE`) at the capture mode's own frame rate (`Pace::STILL`, exposure up to 60 ms) with the burst's exposure (`Auto::still`, `exposure::Still`): the light the viewfinder metered, taken up to eight times lower where the brightest 1 % of its green would pass 85 % of the range at the photo's exposure (all eight where it lay at the limit), but no lower than a frame's gain beyond the sensor's least times that underexposure leaves at eight. The frames are that product rounded up, from two to eight (`MOST_FRAMES`), HDR+'s range; run the receiver health check.
2. Capture the burst, each frame into a slot of its own, the frame the driver says to pass over into the first (`stream::burst_slots`), three queued at once and each next one queued as one fills (`camsv::Capture`), into a buffer of eight slots and a header page from the camera's own ION (`frames::Frames`, which refuses a burst of more frames than its slots, `camera::check_burst`), then stop. At most as many bursts wait as fit the kernel's 336 MiB reserve for media buffers (`CmaTotal`, `camera::to_burst_count`): four; a fifth waits for one to be developed with the sensor resting, and is given up with `PHOTO FAILED` where the viewfinder is left meanwhile (`camera::State::supply`). The photo is named at the click (a name a waiting burst holds counts as taken, `Darkroom::names`).
3. Hand the burst to the darkroom and rest the sensor while the photo's review shows and no click waits (`camera::State::after_rest`; [Camera](../app/camera.md#viewfinders)): the review begins with the click, whose burst is taken first, and a photo for the robot asked during it is taken from the stream it wakes. The sensor rests as well, with no click waiting, while a burst taken for the robot waits or is developed (`Darkroom::holds_look`): the stream's load on the cell does not come on top of that development's, and the conversation opens as the photo comes. Return to preview mode when the viewfinder shows again.

The darkroom (thread `darkroom` and its crew, `camera/darkroom.rs`) develops one burst at a time, each stage in parts on its eight threads (`burst::Stage`):

| Stage | Method | `r1_funcore::hw::camera` |
|---|---|---|
| Planes | each frame's middle 2448x2448 as four planes of 1224x1224, one for each place of the colour quads, mirrored eight samples past every edge | `mosaic` |
| Pyramids | each frame's grey, the mean of its four planes, then three levels below it, blurred by SciPy's Gaussian of half the scale step and taken every 2, 4 and 4 samples; the sharpest of the first three frames is the reference | `align` |
| Alignment | coarse to fine, tiles of 8, 16, 16 and 16 samples overlapped by half, each looked for 4, 4, 4 and 1 samples about the coarser level's guess (the nearest coarse tile's and its two nearest neighbours', the one of least absolute distance), by squared distance but at the finest level, refined between samples by a paraboloid at the coarser three. A tile past the coarser level's last takes that last one's guess | `align` |
| Noise | the variance of a raw sample by its light, a line through the medians of the darker and the brighter half of the reference's tiles, each tile's variance from the difference to the first frame laid on it, no less than a code's rounding | `noise` |
| Merge | each plane in tiles of 16 overlapped by half, in the frequency domain (`base::fft`, two planes as one complex tile): each frame's tile is moved toward the reference's by `|D|² / (|D|² + c σ²)` (IPOL's temporal factor 75), the mean of all taken, then each frequency kept by its power over its power and its noise (spatial factor 0.1); the tiles windowed by a raised cosine and added, into samples of sixteenths of a code | `merge`, `burst` |
| Development | at the square's own size: Malvar-He-Cutler demosaic, lens shading, light in 14 bits, the local tone map's gain, colour matrix and tone curve, rotation by the pose at the click, NV12 output | `develop`, `fusion` |
| Sharpening | the luma, as the pipeline above has it | `sharpen` |

Then the darkroom encodes with the hardware JPEG encoder at the finest tables (0x3), writes the file under a temporary name, syncs it and renames it, shows the photo's review at the viewfinder's size, mirrored for the front camera, and shrinks the planes to 1024x1024 by the mean over each pixel's area (`base::planes::Shrink`) and encodes them for the pixel cam; a failure there does not affect the photo. A burst whose development fails tells `PHOTO FAILED`. A redraw a roll asks for ([Camera](../app/camera.md#rolls)) has the darkroom make that copy anew from the photo's file, decoded at 1024x1024 on its crew and encoded the same way (`Work::Copy`); a copy it cannot make tells `NO PIXELS`.

Files are 2448x2448, baseline, 4:2:0. Two log lines tell each burst as it is taken (frames, exposure, underexposure, gains, the stream's white and matrix) and one each photo as it is saved: the milliseconds until its development began, and those of the merge (with its reference frame and noise line), the development, the sharpening, the encoding, the saving and the handover to the pixel cam. A copy made for a redraw has a line of its own, with its handover (`camera::to_redraw_line`).

## Recording

| Part | Implementation |
|---|---|
| Encoder input | each raw line is read once, shading-corrected, converted to luma per pixel and chroma per quad, Rec. 709 video range, in bands (`camera::video`) |
| Encoder | the register sequence of [Codecs and buffers](../hardware/codecs.md#h264-encoder) through `/dev/Vcodec`; `VCODEC_LOCKHW` type 5 is held for the whole recording (so the kernel grants the top codec clock); `VCODEC_WAITISR`. The JPEG encoder shares the lock |
| Rate control | 16 Mbit/s (`hw::rate`). One quantiser for each six inter frames, between 18 and 44, starting at 30; key frames use 2 less and get 4 times an inter frame's bits. The quantiser follows the six frames' mean size against a target that repays the bit debt over 60 frames. It moves at most 2 steps and stays put within one step, since one step changes a noisy frame's size by 30 to 50 % |
| Parameter sets | SPS and PPS written in software; the stream is split into length-prefixed NAL units (`h264::write`) |
| File | `ftyp`, `mdat` with a 64-bit size, `moov` last; `avc1` with `avcC` and `colr`, rotation in the track matrix; AAC at 96 kbit/s from the main microphone as the second track from the first frame, or no sound track when the microphone is unavailable ([Media](media.md)) |
| Writer thread | video samples and sound packets go through a channel of 120 entries to a thread of the `recording` pool, reused for the next file, which writes the file and finishes it with index, size and a sync (`reel.rs`) |

- Per frame: draw the viewfinder picture (four parts), take the previous frame from the encoder, build this frame's encoder picture (eight parts), start the encoder. The encoder never delays a viewfinder frame. Buffers are cached ION, allocated per recording as the encoder is taken, from an ION node opened above the encoder's (`Venc::open`); the encoder flushes the picture before each frame and invalidates its stream after (`Venc::start`, `Venc::stream`). The encoder's picture is made of the raw frame the viewfinder drew, read once its slot was invalidated and before the slot is queued again (`DrawnFrame`).
- Stream: H.264 High 4.1, yuv420p, TV range, BT.709, chroma at top left, 1280x720, with the rotation for the pose at the start in the track matrix (upright: players show 720x1280; button edge up: a plain 1280x720). One key frame per second (60 frames). Timestamps in 1/480000 s at 480000/7999 fps (60.0075), the SPS's clock; synced to the panel, the actual rate is 59.99 fps. A frame's timestamp follows CAMSV0's count of frames sent, dropped frames included. Sound runs on the sound card's clock; players sync it to the video.
- Starting a recording needs 64 MiB free (`storage::has_room`, `storage::is_full`, [Storage](storage.md#the-data-partition)); free space that cannot be read counts as room. A recording stops on the button, on leaving the viewfinder, at sleep, when free space falls below 64 MiB (checked every half second), when the encoder is stuck (100 ms), on a failed write, or on overheat. The file is always complete.
