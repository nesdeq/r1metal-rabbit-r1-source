# System

`r1-impshell` is one static binary, `/init`: process 1 and the app it supervises. It is built for `aarch64-unknown-linux-musl` at `opt-level = 3` for `cortex-a53` (`opt-level = "s"` draws a screen about 45 % slower) and links `aws-lc-rs`, `rustls` and `flate2` ([Credits](../credits.md)).

## Process 1

`crates/impshell/src/main.rs`, `pid1.rs`:

1. Its standard file descriptors are `/dev/null` (kernel patch `0074`).
2. It reads `flashed` from the ramdisk root before logging anything: the flash's stamp, its logging level ([Logs](logs.md#the-logging-switch)) and the OpenAI key given with it ([OpenAI](../app/openai.md#the-key)). If the flash chose logging off, process 1 logs nothing from the start.
3. It mounts the kernel filesystems, sets `printk.silent` if the flash chose off, writes `on` to `/proc/sys/kernel/printk_devkmsg` and writes the kernel's TCP settings ([Network](network.md#connections-and-tls)).
4. It restarts the device where a cable switched it on and the bootloader booted it to charge: boot mode 8 or 9 in `/proc/device-tree/chosen/atag,boot` with `androidboot.bootreason=usb` (`r1_funcore::domain::pid1`). That boot's kernel powers off when the cable is pulled ([Power](../hardware/power.md#charger)); the restart's reason is `reboot`, and it boots in mode 0 with the cable attached.
5. It creates every device node listed under `/sys/dev` (`CONFIG_DEVTMPFS` is off).
6. It writes the thermal policy into the kernel ([Power](../hardware/power.md#thermal)).
7. It sets up the screen recording's FunctionFS function and keeps its `ep0` open for good, so an app restart leaves the gadget on the bus ([Console](console.md#screen-recording)).
8. It forks the app. When the app exits, process 1 logs the wait status and starts it again, no sooner than 1 s after the previous start (`r1_funcore::domain::pid1::RESTART_SPACING`), so an app that crashes at start does not spin a core. An app that exits does not wait for a host to read what it wrote to the USB console, which the console's driver would wait 15 s for (patch `0204`). If process 1 itself dies, the kernel reboots after 1 s (`CONFIG_PANIC_TIMEOUT=1`).

## The app

`crates/impshell/src/app/`: the main loop draws the screen, handles input, decides when to sleep, and collects results from every thread. It reads the kernel's uevents: a device that appears later gets its node, and a new input device is opened and watched. Before a reboot or power off it has the `battery` thread save the gauge, writes the settings, makes the radio leave its network and stops the modem, then flushes the logs, waiting at most 10 s for all of it (`power::SHUTDOWN_TIMEOUT`), and calls `sync`. The app itself never asks to be started again: process 1 starts it again only where it ended by a fault.

**Scheduling** (`crates/impshell/src/sys/sched.rs`). The main thread is real time (`sys::set_drawing_priority`), one priority above the camera's eight threads and the `calls` thread (`sys::set_worker_priority`), which have the lowest real-time priority: no ordinary thread, kernel threads included, preempts them, so a call's packets wait behind none, the encoder of its pictures among them. The camera's threads keep their cores' clock up ([Camera](camera.md#60-fps-streaming)); the `calls` thread leaves its core's to the governor. The `call pictures` thread and its crew are ordinary: while a picture is coded the thread asks a floor of `hw::CODING_LOAD` under its core's clock (`sys::set_clock_floor`, a minimum utilisation), which its crew's threads, started while it asked that load, keep and weigh with only while they run a picture's parts, and between pictures it asks none (`hw::GOVERNED`). That load is the most that schedutil clocks the large cores for at 1.75 GHz, their fastest step at 0.85 V: the steps above it climb to 1.2 V on the rail all eight cores share, a quarter more clock for twice the energy a cycle (`CPU_DVFS_FREQ8_L_C65X` in the kernel's table for the MT6765X; what the cell gives: [Power](../hardware/power.md#battery-in-the-kernel)). A call's `microphone` and `speaker` threads are real time one priority above the main thread (`sys::set_sound_priority`), each from its stream's start, as a block takes little time and is heard where it comes late. Threads started by main or by a sound thread are ordinary; the requests the `calls` thread starts set themselves back to ordinary as they start (`sys::reset_scheduling`), as a request waits on the network. A pool's thread is ordinary and on any core whatever started it and whatever its last job made of it: it is set back the same way before its first job and after every job (`sys::reset_scheduling`: every core, the ordinary policy at nice 0, no floor under its core's clock). A thread no frame waits for starts at nice 19, as do all threads it starts (`sys::spawn_background`): the "lowest" in the table. The battery rundown runs on the `rundown` pool and lowers its own thread (`sys::set_background_priority`). The camera's, the darkroom's and the gallery's crews of seven have a thread pinned to each core but the first little one (`hw::CORES`, `Crew::with_cores`); left to itself, the scheduler puts two of them on one core while others idle. `Crew::on_cores` places a thread with its crew: the calling thread pinned to the first of the cores, a crew thread to each other. By it the camera's and the gallery's threads and crews take every core, the `call pictures` thread and its crew of three the large cores (`hw::LARGE_CORES`), as coding a call's picture takes whole cores for as long as it runs, and the debug shell's `bench` both, as each of those takes them. The darkroom's thread is not pinned. The `far pictures` thread is pinned to the four small cores (`hw::SMALL_CORES`).

**Crews and pools** (`crates/impshell/src/crew.rs`, `pool.rs`). A `Crew` is a fixed set of threads started once that split one job into parts without allocating (`Crew::for_each`). A `ThreadPool` is threads that take whole jobs as they arrive and stay for the next; a new thread starts only while all of them are busy. Pools run: recording files, the microphone and speaker streams, the radio's power, the hardware clock and the rundown. No thread is started per job, except the news edition's feeds and rundowns once a day, the feeds a request for the rundowns adds, a call's requests of the calling server, the debug shell's `bench` threads and their crews, and the router's threads beside its forwarder, the relay's each time the forwarder begins to serve a computer without the tunnel, one for the translation of a stay in a network of IPv6 alone, and one for each lookup of the tunnel's peer: starting and ending threads maps memory under `mmap_sem`, which the main thread would then wait for.

**No allocation per frame.** The allocator is shared by all threads. Tests count allocations (`alloc_count.rs` in funcore, used by impshell's tests too): a job split across a crew by `Crew::for_each` allocates nothing (`for_each_takes_no_memory`). `Crew::work` and `Crew::run` take boxed parts and rows and allocate their lists.

**Fixed descriptor table.** Before starting any thread the app grows its descriptor table to its `RLIMIT_NOFILE` of 1024 (`sys::grow_descriptor_table`), 8 KiB of kernel memory. The kernel grows a table shared by threads only after an RCU grace period (`expand_fdtable`), which the opening thread waits out. The table starts with 64 slots, and the app's parts open more than that between them, so the descriptor that grows it would otherwise be opened by a thread a frame waits for, the camera's as a recording starts, and its frame would miss the refresh.

## Input

`crates/impshell/src/input/`: evdev devices, decoded into `r1_funcore::input` events. A node under `/dev/input` is accepted by its name (`evdev::is_event_node`). A directory that cannot be listed, a node that cannot be opened and a device that does not tell its name are each logged with the error (`input: opening /dev/input/event3: ...`), and the app runs with the devices that opened.

- The button reports `KEY_POWER` through two drivers; the decoder makes one press of them. Wheel keys produce steps on press only; autorepeat is ignored.
- A finger the panel reports without movement produces no event: an event is produced only when the finger has moved since the last one, and a finger coming down where the last one lifted is a new touch. A resting finger still counts as use (`Inputs::take_finger_rest`), so the sleep and dark timers and the robot's doze do not run under it.
- One finger is the touch. With two fingers, the panel lists contacts in an order that changes between frames. Each contact has a number (`ABS_MT_TRACKING_ID`, reported after the contact's position on one driver path and before it on the other) that stays while the finger stays down. The decoder follows the first finger by its number wherever it is listed. When that number disappears the touch ends with a lift; a finger still down starts no new touch, so a second finger never takes over a gesture. A finger with a number the previous frame lacked starts a touch, in the next frame if it arrived in the frame where the first finger lifted. A reused number on a new finger is a new touch. A frame without numbers is read from its first contact.
- A device whose queue overflowed says so (`SYN_DROPPED`). The decoder passes over what follows up to the end of the frame the drop cut short, and then has every open device asked which keys it holds (`EVIOCGKEY`, `Decoder::take_drop`, `evdev::Held`): a press that began or ended among the lost records is told, and a touch whose finger no device holds ends (`Decoder::resync`); a finger still down shows in its next frame.
- The input log records every event, a keyboard touch without its position, and nothing of a resting finger ([Logs](logs.md)).

## Accelerometer

`crates/impshell/src/input/tilt.rs`, `r1_funcore::input::pose`, `r1_funcore::input::lift`; each read's samples are processed together in `pose::Posture`. The accelerometer sits behind the sensor hub and needs no Android.

- Start: write `0,0,PERIOD_NS,0` to `/sys/class/sensor/m_acc_misc/accbatch` and `1` to `accactive`. `/dev/m_acc_misc` then returns 36-byte records (timestamp, record type byte, x, y, z in mm/s²), one every `PERIOD_NS` (20 ms). The hub supports periods of 5, 10, 20, 40 and 80 ms; 20 ms is the shortest that is not shorter than a refresh. The kernel's queue keeps records across off and on, so it is emptied before switching on.
- Axes: x points to the edge with the button, y to the top edge, each reading about 9807 mm/s² with its edge up (`pose::G`). Readings move in steps of about 2.4 mm/s².
- It runs only while a viewfinder or the video roll shows, and while the robot is at home on a lit screen; never while asleep.
- The main loop opens the queue on the first request and reads it; the `accelerometer` thread writes the switches, so no frame waits for the hub. A queue that fails a read is closed and reopened at the next request.
- **Pose**: an edge counts as up once gravity lies within 30° of it in every reading for 240 ms (`STEADY_NS`), at any sample rate. Below 4000 mm/s² in the screen's plane the device counts as flat and the pose stays. `pose::LowPass` (100 ms) keeps a fifth of a hand's 8 to 12 Hz tremor. The console's `input pose NAME` overrides the sensor until the sensor reports a different pose.
- **Lift**: two seconds of readings (`STILL_NS`) within 28 mm/s² of the first one in the screen's plane (twice the largest difference between two still readings), then one reading more than 981 mm/s² off (a tilt of 6° or a jolt). Only the screen's plane counts, because a finger presses along the third axis. A lift is reported once; the device must lie still again before the next. The log records `accelerometer: picked up after lying still`. A lift does not count as use of the device.
