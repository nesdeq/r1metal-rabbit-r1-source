# Sleep and clock

What puts the device to sleep, what runs and what may wake it while it sleeps, and how the clocks are kept.

## Sleep

| Trigger | Condition |
|---|---|
| `STANDBY` on the power screen | always; no button is held, so the system can suspend at once |
| The conversation's `use_device` with `sleep` | after the turn that asked ends |
| 1 min without touch, button or wheel on any screen (`IDLE_TO_SLEEP_NS`) | no cable attached, nothing recording, no video, audio note or Signal voice note playing, nothing read aloud, no conversation turn in progress, no visit of the browser's, no request to the bell or for the rundowns being said, written down or read, no Pokédex lookup, no message for Signal being written down, the bell not ringing, no call standing, battery rundown off (`Ui::is_in_use`) |

On a cable the device does not sleep by itself and the screen stays on. After the same 1 min without use, what is billed by the time it runs ends as a sleep would end it: the translator's live session (`Ui::tick`). Work billed by the request goes on.

Entering sleep, after which the interface wants nothing of the parts until it is told that the device woke (`Ui::note_wake`):

- turns the backlight off and blanks the panel (`FBIOBLANK`), which also stops touch and wheel input; a screen already darkened during a task stays dark ([Interface](../app/interface.md#light-and-sleep));
- stops the camera, the gallery and the accelerometer;
- releases the radio: a scan or a join is cancelled, the network stay ends with its connections, new network requests are refused, and the connectivity core is powered off; a pixel cam photo being sent counts as unsent and waits;
- ends the battery rundown and the frame pacing test;
- ends a conversation recording: one for a message is kept, and its turn runs once the device is awake again; one of a press at home not yet held long enough is dropped; the voice stops, and the conversation starts no turn while the device sleeps;
- drops the Pokédex's, the browser's, the bell's and the rundowns' recording and pending requests;
- drops a message being said for Signal, gives up the fetch the wake asked for, and switches auto mode off where the alarm fetches nothing ([Signal](../app/signal.md#power)).

Only these let the radio off its rest during sleep: the alarm's uses below, with auto mode's answer to what Signal's fetch brought; Signal while a request is on its way over its open socket, while an attachment is due to go up or under way, or while the link the phone sent the account for runs to the end of its first uploads; the reader, for a few seconds, waiting for the cost of a passage already sent; a conversation turn or a recollection already in progress, until it ends, since the service charges for it anyway; the router, while a computer's link on the cable is up and it is lent the modem's connection ([Network](network.md#the-routed-stay)). While the rest is lifted for one of them, the radio serves any holder, and a stay checks the clocks where that is due ([Clock](#clock)).

**Suspend.** On battery, the app suspends the system when all of these hold: it waits only for the button; the data partition's thread neither formats nor moves files under the safe's key (`Storage::is_off`, [Storage](storage.md#the-safe)); the camera, gallery, radio, modem, router, rundown, notes, translator, reader, conversation, Pokédex, browser, the bell's requests, the rundowns' requests, Signal, Signal's dictation, the calls and the sound card are idle (`Parts::is_quiet`); the news editor has no edition to make; and the kernel reports nothing keeping it awake. It first flushes the logs for up to 10 s (`power::SHUTDOWN_TIMEOUT`), and suspends only where the parts are idle still: one that became busy meanwhile keeps the system up, and the kernel is asked anew once nothing runs (`Doze::next_step`, `Doze::hand_back`). On a cable the system stays up with the screen dark and the idle timer stopped. A cable counts as attached while the battery status is `Charging`, `Full` or `Not charging` (`Supply::is_plugged`).

**wakeup_count.** A read of `/sys/power/wakeup_count` blocks until no driver holds a wakeup source, then returns a count. Writing the count back before suspending makes the kernel abort the suspend if a wakeup event occurred since. The `suspend` thread does the read (`sleep::Gate`), asked once a question: `Gate::take_answer` takes its word and has the next asked anew. On a cable the read never returns, because the USB-C port driver holds a wakeup source ([Power](../hardware/power.md#suspend)). `r1_funcore::hw::power::doze` also requires the button released and the last battery reading to show no cable and no voltage under 3.40 V (`CUTOFF_MV`, a single sample); after a suspend that is a reading taken 2 s or more after the resume, and before the first suspend, while no reading has come, the cable as the sleep began. A refused suspend is retried at once up to 3 times (`QUICK_RETRIES`), then once per battery reading. If the count cannot be read, it is read again after the next battery reading.

**Waking.** The interface holds the sleep (`Ui::doze_mut`): asleep it takes only the button, whose press wakes the device and whose release after the press that asked for the sleep does not. A waking press held as long as a hold to talk takes, and still held once the panel is on, talks to the robot from the screen the device woke on, recorded from the press ([Chat](../app/chat.md#a-turn)); one let go before clicks nothing, on the chat screen too. Plugging in a cable, charger or computer wakes it too: the kernel wakes, and the first battery reading that shows the cable ends the sleep as the button does (`Ui::set_reading`, log line `wake: a cable is put in`); on a cable the screen stays on. The bell's alarm wakes it too where what it rings for is due: the interface ends the sleep and rings in the bell's window, over the screen the device slept on (`Ui::ring`, log line `wake: the bell rings`; [Interface](../app/interface.md#the-bell)). The device returns to the screen it was on, the panel comes on as the first frame is drawn ([Display](display.md#pages)) and the backlight once that frame is up, and the log records how long it slept and in how many suspends. After any other wake the app waits for the first battery reading 2 s after resume (`LOOK_AFTER_NS`), logs it, reads `wakeup_count` again and suspends again. A reading under 3.40 V keeps the system up, as does one showing a cable that was attached when the sleep began.

**Dark look.** The app runs only while the system runs: it looks at the battery after every suspend that ran (`Doze::take_suspend`), whatever woke the system, and where nothing else does the SPM's wake timer brings that look ([Power](../hardware/power.md#suspend)). A reading under 3.40 V keeps the system up, so that the cutoff can act on its 10-sample mean ([Battery](battery.md#cutoff)); the next reading above it lets the system suspend again. Each check keeps the system up 2 to 3 s, at about 100 mA 0.06 to 0.08 mAh ([Power](../hardware/power.md#draw)), and logs a line:

```
sleep: dark look 2.3 s after the resume, 5401 s suspended before it and 10802 s in 2 suspends since the screen went dark: 4127 mV -35.2 mA 123.4 mAh 24 C Discharging
```

[Power](../hardware/power.md#suspend) shows how to compute the sleep drain from these lines.

## The alarm's list

During sleep only what the button needs stays powered. The clock alarm may wake a sleeping device only for the uses in `Reason` (`r1_funcore::hw::power::alarm`), the only uses `Alarm::new` (`crates/impshell/src/power/alarm.rs`) accepts; only an `Alarm`'s descriptor is watched so that it holds a suspend off (`Epoll::add_wakeup`). A test checks that this table matches that list.

| A sleeping device is woken for | When | What runs |
|---|---|---|
| The daily news | the hour of the on-screen clock the news' settings set, 08:00 on a new device, none without a daily edition (`Edition::next_alarm`), then 120 s after the request to the model (`FIRST_LOOK_S`) and every 30 s after that (`SLOW_POLL_SECONDS`) | the network for the feeds and the request, then the radio rests and the device may suspend; at each later wake, a short network stay until the rundowns are ready ([News](../app/news.md)) |
| Signal's messages | every 5, 10, 15 or 60 min after the last fetch or the end of the last stay, as Signal's settings say (60 by default, or never) | the account's socket until the service has sent what waited, or for 60 s where it does not say so, and auto mode has answered what came, then the radio rests and the device may suspend, after the notice of a message that tells itself has played; a fetch that cannot reach the service is given up until the next; the alarm goes off for it awake too ([Signal](../app/signal.md#power)) |
| The bell's rings | when the soonest alarm, reminder or timer that is set is due | the device wakes, lit, shows what is due in the bell's window and rings until a press of the button or for 5 min (`bell::RING_S`), then sleeps by its usual rules, the window still up at the next wake; the alarm goes off for it awake too ([Interface](../app/interface.md#the-bell)) |
| The modem's kept attach | when the attach the modem keeps after a stay runs out (`KEEP` in `r1_funcore::cell::modem`) | the modem's thread turns the radio off (`AT+CFUN=4`), then the device may suspend again; a stay meanwhile takes the attach up without attaching anew ([Modem](../hardware/modem.md#in-r1metal)) |

**The alarm** is a `timerfd` on `CLOCK_REALTIME_ALARM`. At suspend the kernel programs the PMIC's RTC for the earliest such alarm (`alarmtimer_suspend`); MediaTek's RTC driver adds a second, and a suspend less than 2 s before an alarm is refused. Where the kernel has no such clock, or the process may not set wake alarms, the alarm falls back to an ordinary timer and the log says so. It is armed with `TFD_TIMER_CANCEL_ON_SET`, so setting the wall clock makes its owner recompute it.

**No suspend between alarm and use.** The waiting thread uses `EPOLLWAKEUP`: from the moment the timer fires until the thread waits again, the kernel holds a wakeup source, so `wakeup_count` does not return. This needs `CAP_BLOCK_SUSPEND`, and the timer needs `CAP_WAKE_ALARM`; the app keeps both. The bell's alarm the main loop watches itself, and the ring it finds keeps the device awake (`Ui::is_in_use`). The news editor notifies the main loop and waits for its go-ahead within that hold. Signal goes back to its wait at once; the fetch it then owes holds the suspend off instead (`Signal::is_off`). The main loop lets the radio off its rest and suspends nothing until the use is done. The go-ahead (`Editor::grant_radio`, `Signal::grant_radio`) takes what only a lifted rest gives (`radio::RadioGrant`, from `Radio::set_resting`), and goes only to a part the look that lifted the rest found owing (`app::Debts::to_grantees`).

## Clock

The system clock and the RTC keep UTC; the kernel sets one from the other at boot. The on-screen clock shows UTC plus the offset setting, in minutes. The offset also applies to photo and recording file names, the ledger's months and the USB drive's timestamps. Certificates, a recording's internal time and filesystem timestamps use UTC. The device has no time zones.

| Set by | Sets | How |
|---|---|---|
| A hold of the network, once an hour | clocks and offset | queries a time service and a place service beside the hold's purpose |
| The clock screen | clocks and offset on opening; the offset or a part of the time with the wheel | each step moves the clocks by one step (`clock::move_by`), never outside 2026 to 2099 (`SETTABLE_YEARS`); the offset steps through 39 offsets, `UTC-11:00` to `UTC+14:00` (`OFFSETS`) |
| `time` console command | clocks and offset | the same queries |
| `r1 sh clock` | clocks and offset | the host's UTC and offset (`clock SECONDS MINUTES`) |

Setting the clocks updates the system clock at once. The RTC, whose driver waits on the PMIC, is written on a thread of its own (`rtc`), never the drawing thread, so a fast wheel turn causes few writes.

- **Time**: SNTP (RFC 5905, client rules of RFC 4330; `r1_funcore::net::sntp`), one 48-byte datagram each way in the clear, so it works before the clock is correct, which TLS would need. Each request carries 8 random bytes (`sntp::MARK_BYTES`) that the reply must echo. Only a server whose own clock is set counts, and only a time within `SETTABLE_YEARS`; half the round trip is added. `time.cloudflare.com` is queried at its four addresses, never by name, 250 ms apart (`dial::exchange`). The clocks are set when they differ by more than 1 s, and the log records by how much (`clock::to_set_by_line`).
- **Offset**: `ipwho.is` over HTTPS returns the UTC offset at the requesting address, daylight saving included (`timezone.offset`; `r1_funcore::domain::place::parse_offset`). The last answer is saved in `.r1metal/clock/place`; only a different answer changes the offset (`place::has_offset_changed`), so a manual setting stays until the location changes. The same answer names the place (`country_code`, `region`, `city`, `timezone.id`; `r1_funcore::openai::chat::request::Location`), saved in `.r1metal/clock/where` for the conversation's messages and web search and the web search of the browser's model, and never logged.
- **When**: beside every hold of the network that is not quiet (`Timing::hold_hook`, `clock::State::next_hold_step`). TLS needs the clocks, so the first time check since the start comes before a hold goes on: the first hold to come makes it on its own thread, and every other waits for it, or for the end of its own stay; one that waited through a check that failed makes none of its own (`base::handoff::Turns`). After that, a hold has the timekeeper's thread `time` ask beside its stay: the place alone after a first check the hold made, else the time and the place where a check is due, once an hour (`clock::CHECK_PERIOD_S`, `clock::next_check_s`). The thread does so under a share of the hold (`Hold::share`), so the stay stands until the check is done. A check whose time service did not answer cost its stay 10 s of silence (`race::TIMEOUT`), so the next is due only after a wait that doubles from those 10 s with every failure up to the hour (`clock::State::end_check`); the first hold after the wait has it made, and a check that answers begins the waits anew. The radio never turns on for this check alone. The clock screen's query and the console's `time` take their own quiet hold on the same thread (`Online::hold_quietly`); their check is the first where none was made or is being made. A quiet hold asks for nothing and counts as the check that was due. The reader, which holds the network passage by passage, checks the clocks in its first stay only; its later stays are quiet (`Account::hold_quietly`).
