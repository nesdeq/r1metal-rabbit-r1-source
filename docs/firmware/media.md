# Media

JPEG and PNG decoding, video playback, MP4, AAC, Opus, VP8 and VP9, and the sound card with a call's sound. Gallery screens: [Camera](../app/camera.md#rolls). Hardware: [Codecs and buffers](../hardware/codecs.md), [Sound](../hardware/sound.md).

## JPEG

`r1_funcore::codec::jpeg` decodes baseline and extended sequential JPEG: 8 bit, greyscale or colour, chroma at full or half resolution in either direction. It decodes directly to the smallest scale (1/8, 1/4, 1/2 or 1) that is not smaller than the displayed size. Arithmetic-coded, progressive, lossless and hierarchical files are rejected. Tested against libjpeg-turbo ([Credits](../credits.md)).

- **Threads.** The gallery thread runs at the lowest priority on the first little core. Its `Decoders` (`crates/impshell/src/gallery/mod.rs`) starts a `Crew` of seven threads once, one on each other core (three little, then the four big ones), and keeps its buffers while a roll shows. The decoding itself is `media::still::decode`, which runs on a crew's threads or on the caller's alone.
- **Signal's photos** are decoded by the same function on the `pictures` thread, on its own alone, from the kept file to stand as wide as the conversation's room and never larger than they are (`domain::signal::pictures::decoded_size`), and turned as the file's EXIF orientation says (`Jpeg::orientation`, `jpeg::oriented`) ([Signal](../app/signal.md#attachments)).
- **Open photos** are split at their restart markers and decoded on all eight cores. Camera photos have a restart marker per MCU row, as the encoder is configured (`jpeg::restart_interval`); pixel art has none and decodes on one core.
- **Thumbnails** are the whole picture at the smallest size that still covers the grid cell. Each decodes on one thread, a page's photos in parallel; a different cell size makes them again. The visible page is made first, then the next, then the previous (`r1_funcore::domain::gallery::roll`). For an open photo, its other size is made first, then the neighbour in the wheel's direction, then the other neighbour (`r1_funcore::domain::gallery::stills`).

## PNG

`r1_funcore::gfx::png` reads the Pokédex artwork ([Interface](../app/interface.md#pokédex)) and the PNG photos of Signal's conversations ([Signal](../app/signal.md#attachments)). It checks every chunk's CRC and reads 8 bits per sample in every colour type (greyscale, RGB, palette, greyscale or RGB with alpha) with transparency, without interlacing. Another bit depth, compression or filter method, or an unknown critical chunk, is `png::Error::Unsupported`; a picture with a side longer than its reader allows (`Png::read`) is `png::Error::TooLarge`, and none of it is inflated. impshell inflates the image data with flate2 in one place (`media::still::inflate`), reading up to one byte past the expected size (`Png::read_limit`) so that data of the wrong length is detected (`BadData`). funcore unfilters each row in place, composites every pixel over the screen background by its alpha, and scales the picture down to fit its square (`Surface::shrink`), never up. The Pokédex's size limits are in [Interface](../app/interface.md#pokédex). Tested against files written by the `png` crate.

## Videos

MP4 with an H.264 track, decoded by the hardware decoder one picture at a time.

| Part | Code | Does |
|---|---|---|
| Parameter sets, slice headers | `r1_funcore::codec::h264::read` | parses what the decoder needs, with the bit positions of the fields the hardware reads itself |
| Decoded picture buffer | `r1_funcore::codec::h264::dpb` | the sliding window, the initial P list, picture order count types 0 and 2, frame number gaps, output order |
| Decoder registers, stream | `r1_funcore::hw::vdec` | the sequence in [Codecs and buffers](../hardware/codecs.md#h264-decoder); a stream's parameter sets and frames and what each sample makes of them (`Stream`), the wait for a picture (`Watch`) |
| Device node, buffers | `crates/impshell/src/media/vdec.rs` | `/dev/Vcodec`, the codec block's lock per picture, ION buffers in the number and size `Stream::buffers` and `Layout` give; `begin` returns while the hardware works, `end` waits for it |
| MP4 | `r1_funcore::codec::mp4`, `crates/impshell/src/media/recording.rs` | reads the whole index wherever it lies, each box by its size (`mp4::seek_index`), trusted only as far as the file can contain it (`Movie::read`); the first sequence parameter set sizes the stream (`Movie::sequence`); reads samples one at a time, each only if the file contains it whole (`Sample::fits`) |
| Picture for the screen | `r1_funcore::hw::projector` | converts the decoder's blocks to pixels: scaled down in whole steps, fitted, rotated, in the stream's colours |
| Playback | `r1_funcore::domain::gallery::{feed, player, show, projection, stats}` | decoding state (`Feed`), click actions (`Player`), the video clock and the pictures queued for the screen (`Show`), size and rotation, the speaker's position in the sound, the log line (`PlayStats`) |
| Drawing | `crates/impshell/src/gallery/video.rs` | four bands of whole lines per picture (`hw::FRAME_PARTS`, `gfx::bands`), one per little core: the `gallery` thread and the three little-core threads of its `Decoders`, because work done 60 times a second costs least there |

- **Pipeline.** The decoder starts on the next sample; meanwhile the previous picture is drawn and the next sample read; then the decoder's result is collected. The codec block's lock is held only from start to end; between pictures the kernel powers the block down.
- **Pacing.** Up to four drawn pictures queue (`show::QUEUE_LENGTH`); playback starts when four are queued, and the screen takes one per refresh. The video clock advances one refresh in each refresh the screen asks in, counted from the panel's last flip, however often the main loop asks within it (`Show::take_frame`); while no picture waits it stands, so a late picture delays playback instead of skipping. A stream within 1 % of the screen rate (`REFRESH_TOLERANCE`; our recordings run at 60.0075 fps) shows one picture per refresh; any other keeps its own clock.
- **Buffers.** As many pictures as the stream may reference, one being decoded, and two lent out (`feed::PICTURES_OUT`): the last drawn, kept for rotation, and the one being drawn (`Stream::buffers`). The `Decoders` keep the device node and buffers while a roll shows, from one video's thumbnail or playback to the next video they fit (`Decoders::open_video`). A video with another layout or more reference frames gets new ones (`Stream::fits`).
- **Rotation and size.** Rotation is the track header's quarter turns plus the device pose. Size is the largest that fits the area under the bar for the current pose. A pose change creates a new projector.
- **Colour.** The stream's matrix (Rec. 709 for 1, Rec. 601 for 5 and 6), else by size (709 from 1280 wide or over 576 high); range from the stream's flag; fixed-point arithmetic. Scaling weights are computed once per video and pose (`picks` in `projector.rs`). Planes are processed one band at a time in the decoder's block order (16 byte block lines), and each output line is written front to back, because strided writes and reading block lines along picture lines are several times slower.
- **Damaged streams.** The decoder's interrupt is awaited 16 ms, then the last macroblock is checked every 4 ms and a picture that has stopped progressing is abandoned; a picture still progressing gets at most 200 ms (`vdec::Watch`). An abandoned picture is shown as far as it got. A sample is skipped and counted as flawed (`vdec::StartOutcome::Unsupported`) if its header or parameter set cannot be read, if it changes the layout or needs more frames, or if its slices exceed its picture. A file that ends before its index shows `UNREADABLE`.
- **Closing.** When the roll closes or the device sleeps, the video stops and the node and buffers are released before the main loop suspends. A decoder whose feed does not come to rest keeps the codec block and the buffers it reaches instead, since the kernel's release would wait for that feed without end: the `Decoders` ask it again at the next video and as the roll closes, and let go of them once its feed has rested (`vdec::StuckList`). Until then suspend is refused, and every later user of the block, a photo's encoding, a recording's encoder or the next video's decoder, waits for it in the kernel up to 30 s at each asking and fails ([Codecs and buffers](../hardware/codecs.md#codec-node)).

| Plays | Does not play |
|---|---|
| H.264 Baseline, Main and High, 4:2:0 at 8 bits, 4 to 128 macroblocks wide (64 to 2048 px), at least 4 high, at most 8160 macroblocks (1920x1088) | interlaced, other chroma formats and bit depths, lossless macroblocks, slice groups, B slices, switching slices, scaling lists, long-term references, memory management commands, order counts in cycles (type 1) |
| any frame rate, weighted prediction | a stream that changes size |
| MP4 with the index anywhere, any quarter turn in the track header | fragmented MP4; in the video track, samples shown out of decoding order (`ctts`) and chunk offsets of 32 bits (`stco`); `avc3` |

Sound plays where it is low complexity AAC of one channel or two at a rate of the standard's ([AAC](#aac)), brought to one channel at 48 kHz and synced to the video clock; any other plays silent. What does not play is refused by name with a `codec::Flaw`: `Unsupported` for what the player does not do, `Missing` for an index without a box it reads (a video track indexed with `stco` has no `co64`). A video refused in its index or parameter sets shows `UNREADABLE`, a refused sample is passed over, and a refused sound track plays silent, with its flaw in the log (`gallery: NAME: no sound: ...`). A file with no sound track is no flaw: `Soundtrack::read` gives none, the video plays silent and the log tells nothing; what is read for its sound, a note or a voice note, refuses such a file (`mp4::NO_SOUND_TRACK`). A video track's edit list and pixel aspect ratio are ignored; a sound track's edits are read as [MP4](#mp4) says.

## MP4

`r1_funcore::codec::mp4` writes and reads video, sound, or both. Video: [Camera](camera.md#recording).

- **Sound** is an `mp4a` sample entry with `esds` (AAC, the two config bytes, max and average bit rate), 1024 samples per packet, and an edit list that skips the decoder delay so sound starts where it was recorded. The edit list uses 64 bit times (version 1), because at our video timescale (480000 per second) 32 bits last only two and a half hours.
- **Layout.** A sound-only file has the brand `M4A `. A sound chunk holds at most 32 packets (`CHUNK_PACKETS`); with video, sound and video chunks alternate.
- **Start.** A video file starts with its first frame, including sound recorded before it; a sound-only file starts with its first packet (`Content::starts_file`).
- **Duration.** Sound lasts its packets minus the decoder delay, and no longer than its edit lets be heard, partial seconds rounded up (`aac::track_samples`). A piece cut from it keeps the delay only if it starts with the first packet (`aac::cut_seconds`, `Soundtrack::cut_from`). The container takes the samples a packet holds from the codec (`Soundtrack::read`), so `mp4` names nothing of `aac` outside its tests.
- **Reading sound** of other programs and phones (`Soundtrack::read`): an `mp4a` entry of version 0, of QuickTime's version 1 with its four fields more, or of version 2 with its channels in a field of 32 bits, the stream's descriptor behind the fields or in a `wave` box; descriptors whose sizes take one byte to four and that hold the fields their flags say follow; chunk places of 32 bits (`stco`) or 64 (`co64`), several runs of chunks; a `ctts` passed over, as sound is in order. An edit list of version 0 or 1: the first edit that plays sets where the sound starts, empty edits before it passed over, and where it is the only one and the movie header tells its clock, how many samples are heard (`Soundtrack::heard`). An entry of a version past 2 or an edit list of a version past 1 is `Unsupported`.

## AAC

`r1_funcore::codec::aac` encodes AAC LC for r1metal's own sound, mono at 48 kHz in long sine windows with no optional tools, and decodes that and what phones record, written from ISO/IEC 14496-3 without a library. Square roots, logarithms and sines come from `r1_funcore::base::math`. Its tables are made at compile time, their roots, sines, cube roots and Bessel's function by `math`'s const functions: the windows of both shapes, the turns of the cosine transforms, two to the power of each step of an octave, every quantized line's size dequantized, and the trees of the code books and the scalefactors. The tests hold each table to the one a loop makes at run time, bit for bit (`tables_bit_exact`, `powers_bit_exact`), the const roots, sines and cube roots to the forms `math::iterated` writes with iterators (`const_forms_bit_exact`), and a decoder made to allocate its transforms' FFT plans and its buffers alone (`tables_allocate_nothing`).

- **Encoder.** Each 1024-sample frame and the one before go through the MDCT (one 512-point FFT) into 1024 coefficients; the 42 bands below 17.25 kHz are coded (`CODED_BANDS`). A band's quantizer step grows by a third of its level's octave (`STEPS_PER_OCTAVE`), so louder bands sit further above their noise. Each band uses the codebook with the fewest bits, counted exactly, including the cost of codebook switches. A bit reservoir holds the rate: steps grow by one per two packets' share of debt (`DEBT_PER_STEP`), and underspending saves up to eight shares (`CREDIT`). No packet exceeds 768 bytes; silence takes four bytes. There are no short windows, so a sharp attack smears over its 21 ms frame.
- **Decoder.** Decodes low complexity AAC of one channel or a channel pair at any of the standard's 13 rates (`aac::Config::read`): long, start, eight short and stop windows of the sine and the Kaiser-Bessel shape (`transform.rs`), pulses, temporal noise shaping, noise substitution, mid/side and intensity stereo (`tools.rs`); a pair plays as the mean of its channels. It refuses by name (`codec::Flaw::Unsupported`) another object type, a rate without an index, a channel layout of the stream's own or more than two channels, frames of 960 samples, a core coder, an extended configuration, spectral band replication, prediction, gain control and channel coupling. An undecodable packet plays as silence. Output lags one frame, `DELAY` (1024 samples).
- **ADTS.** A file that begins with an ADTS header, or an ID3 tag before one, is a stream of packets, each behind a header of its own (`aac::adts`); every packet plays, its encoder's lead included.

`cargo test -p r1-funcore -- --ignored ffmpeg` checks the codec against ffmpeg: ffmpeg decodes our packets to within 2 LSB (16 bit) of our decoder, plays a file of our sound from its first sample to its last, and decodes what it encodes at every rate of the standard as ours does. Files of FFmpeg's AAC encoder, most with temporal noise shaping, noise substitution, intensity and mid/side stereo on, of one and two channels at 16, 22.05, 44.1 and 48 kHz in MP4, QuickTime and ADTS (`crates/funcore/assets/sound/`), decode within 2 LSB of FFmpeg's decoding of each, kept beside it, and together reach every window sequence, both window shapes and every tool (`fixtures_match_ffmpeg`, `fixtures_reach_every_tool`). For that comparison the noise of a pair's two channels is made for each on its own, as FFmpeg makes it; the device's decoder gives both the same noise where the pair's mask is set on a band of noise, as the standard has it, and differs from FFmpeg's in those frames alone (`masked_noise_shared`).

## Opus

`r1_funcore::codec::opus` decodes Opus (RFC 6716) into one channel at 48 kHz and encodes CELT frames of one channel at a constant rate, written from the RFC and its reference decoder without a library. A call's sound is its one use ([A call's sound](#a-calls-sound)).

- **Decoder** (`opus::Decoder`): packets of every code (RFC 6716, 3), up to 120 ms and 48 frames, of SILK frames (narrow, medium and wide band, 10 to 60 ms), CELT frames (narrow to full band, 2.5 to 20 ms) and hybrid ones, of one channel or two: a CELT pair plays as the mean of its channels, SILK's stereo as its mid channel. SILK decodes at its own rate, 8, 12 or 16 kHz, which the one `base::resample::Resampler` brings to 48 kHz, and every layer is laid as far behind as that filter holds SILK back, so that the sound comes 238 samples (5 ms) late in every mode and a change of mode stays seamless. Redundant CELT frames (4.5.1) and changes of mode without one (4.5.2) are spliced in as libopus's `opus_decode_frame` splices them. A frame of 1 byte or none, and a lost packet, are concealed (`Decoder::conceal`): CELT's by noise in the last frame's bands at its energies, 9 dB down and 3 dB more for each frame lost after (`celt_decode_lost`'s noise), SILK's as the reference decoder's `PLC.c` conceals, without its comfort noise. A lost packet whose next one holds a copy of it (in-band FEC, 4.2.5, `Decoder::copy_of`) is made from that copy, SILK's LBRR frames (`Decoder::recover`). A packet that does not parse changes nothing and is refused by name with a `codec::Flaw`. `Decoder::reset` forgets every packet decoded, as a new stream begins, and keeps its filters' coefficients, as libopus's `OPUS_RESET_STATE` does.
- **Encoder** (`opus::Encoder`): CELT frames of 20 ms from one channel at 48 kHz, 1 to 6 to a packet, each packet the size it is given, so that the rate is constant (code 0 where one frame fills the packet, else code 3 with padding), as libopus encodes in its restricted low-delay application at its default complexity; narrow, wide, super-wide or full band, medium coded as wide as libopus codes it; a 3 Hz high-pass takes out a constant level.

The official test vectors of RFC 8251 (`crates/funcore/assets/sound/opus/testvector*`: prefixes of at most 64 KB, vector 12 whole, and a run of vector 10 where CELT and hybrid frames take turns) end every packet in their encoder's final range, every change of mode among them (`vectors_end_in_final_range`); SILK's packets decode exactly as libopus's at the internal rate, LBRR frames included, CELT's within 2 of libopus's samples, and the whole decoder's sound agrees with what libopus 1.6.1's decoder of one channel at 48 kHz makes of three vectors (`sound_near_libopus`); a copy lies nearer the sound than a concealment (`lost_packets_made_up`); a decoder reset after other sound decodes a vector sample for sample as a new one (`reset_decodes_as_new`). Every packet the encoder writes decodes in its own final range, and a CRC32 of its bytes holds them the same on every machine (`encoded_voice_decodes`); every packet after the first is coded in the room the first made, with no memory taken (`encoding_takes_no_memory`).

## VP8

`r1_funcore::codec::vp8` decodes VP8 (RFC 6386) into Y, U and V planes and codes pictures into frames in real time, written from the RFC without a library. A call's pictures are its one use ([Calls](../app/calls.md#pictures)).

- **Decoder** (`vp8::Decoder`): every version (0 with the six-tap filter, 1 to 3 bilinear, 3 with chroma moved by whole pixels), segmentation, loop filter deltas, the simple and the normal filter, up to 8 token partitions, frames not shown, golden and altref references; pictures of at most 8192 macroblocks, 256 across or down, H.264 level 4.1's as the device records (`codec::picture::check_size`). It refuses by name a version past 3, the reserved colour space, a larger picture and what is cut short, and after an error waits for a key frame.
- **Encoder** (`vp8::Encoder`): one stream, its work cut into six steps: `begin`; `Encoder::code_steps`, which hands each step (`vp8::Step`) with its work (`base::crew::Work`: parts, or a wave of rows) to its caller, to run on several threads or one after another: the last frame's half-pixel planes (`Step::Interpolations`, 9 parts at 640x480, none in a key frame: each sample the decoder's six taps half a pixel across, down or both, the reference's edges repeated past its border as its prediction repeats them, the plane of both filtered down from the samples across); the decisions of bands of 8 macroblock rows (`Step::Bands`, a part each), each macroblock a band predicts from a frame reconstructed into the band's lines of the frame coded; `Step::Code`, a wave of macroblock rows, each coding its macroblocks in order, those predicted within the frame reconstructed there in place of the band's where they cost less, the contexts a macroblock's tokens leave taken from where each block's levels end (`tokens::contexts`), in the one order of blocks the tokens are read and written in (`tokens::blocks`); a row codes a macroblock once the row above has coded the one above it and, in a key frame, the one after it too, whose pixels B_PRED's right subblocks read (`KEY_LEAD`, `INTER_LEAD`), the row above handing it its last line of each plane, the tokens' contexts and, in an inter frame, its records; the tokens and the macroblocks' kinds, modes and vectors counted in bands of 8 rows (`Step::Counts`, a part each); the frame's probabilities and its token partitions settled from those counts (`Step::Probabilities`, one part); the ends (`Step::Ends`), a wave of the luma's loop filter, each row filtering a macroblock once the row above has handed over its last four lines there, which that row does once it has filtered the macroblock after (`filter::LUMA_LEAD`), beside parts that write the token partitions and filter each chroma plane; then `finish`, which puts back the lines above each row that the row's top edges changed. An inter macroblock is reconstructed by the one `reconstruct::inter` the decoder runs, an intra one by its `reconstruct::intra`. A request names whether the frame is a key frame and its quantizer, 0 to 127, which the caller's rate control picks (`ac_step`, `quantizer_of`); the first frame and the first of a new size are key frames whatever is asked. Modes are chosen by SATD and λ times their bits, λ 24 times the AC step, B_PRED in key frames alone, motion by a diamond search of 16 steps refined to half and quarter pixels; SPLITMV by quarters from the last frame, searched where a whole macroblock's prediction costs more than 16 AC steps and one of its quarters differs at its vector by more than half again the quarters' mean (`SPLIT_FROM_STEPS`, `UNEVEN_QUARTER`); a golden frame every 30 frames; the loop filter at a quarter of the quantizer; the probabilities kept for one frame alone, so that a lost frame leaves the next whole; token partitions by the frame's own tokens, counted in `Step::Counts` and weighed at the probabilities `Step::Probabilities` settles for them, their signs and extra bits left out: one for each 2048 of their bytes begun (`BYTES_PER_PARTITION`), brought down to a power of two of 1 to 8 and no more than the macroblock rows. It uses no segmentation, loop filter deltas, altref frames or temporal layers.

The search's sums of absolute differences, the SATD of the choice of modes and every filter's taps are kernels of `base::simd`, NEON on the R1 ([Architecture](../architecture.md#media-and-hardware)); the loop filter takes eight segments of an edge at once in steps the compiler makes vector instructions of (`codec::edge`), those along lines read and written as the columns of blocks of 8x8 (`simd::columns`, `put_columns`). The debug shell's `bench` times a call's encoding and decoding on the device ([Console](console.md#debug-shell)).

Four streams (`crates/funcore/assets/video/vp8/`: segments and eight partitions; altref frames not shown and golden ones; version 1 with the simple filter; versions 2 and 3) decode to ffmpeg's pictures (`streams_match_ffmpeg`); the encoder's frames decode to its own reconstruction at six sizes (`reconstruction_decodes_exactly`) and where macroblocks take the golden frame and SPLITMV from the last (`golden_and_split_decode_exactly`) or are coded within the frame in place of the bands' inter prediction (`intra_in_place_of_inter_decodes_exactly`), the loop filter's groups of eight segments filter as one segment at a time (`groups_filter_as_one`), its steps on threads, each part and each row of a wave on a thread of its own, give the bytes they give on one thread (`threaded_steps_match_whole`), and a damaged or refused frame leaves the decoder waiting for a key frame (`damaged_frames_return`, `frames_refused_by_name`).

## VP9

`r1_funcore::codec::vp9` decodes VP9 of profile 0, 8 bits and 4:2:0, into Y, U and V planes, written from the VP9 bitstream specification, and as libvpx decodes where the two differ, without a library. The pictures of a group call's members that send VP9 are its one use ([Calls](../app/calls.md#pictures)).

- **Decoder** (`vp9::Decoder`): `decode` takes one frame, a superframe with its index (the specification's annex B), or a picture's spatial layers one after the other without an index, lower first, as a receiver joins them, and gives the last picture shown. It decodes key, intra-only and inter frames, compound prediction, references of another size than the frame's, tiles, segmentation, lossless frames, the error resilient and frame parallel modes with their probabilities' adaptation or none, and a reference slot's frame shown again; pictures within VP8's bound (`codec::picture::check_size`). It refuses by name profiles 1 to 3, RGB, a larger picture, padding that is not nought and what is cut short, and after an error takes nothing but a key frame or an intra-only frame.
- **Shared with VP8**: the boolean coder (`codec::boolean`, VP8's decoder and encoder and VP9's decoder), decoded pictures, their frames inside borders and the size bound (`codec::picture`), the taps of a prediction (`base::simd::filter`, and `plain::tap` for a scaled reference's), and the loop filter's masks and narrow filter over several segments of an edge at once, a segment in each lane (`codec::edge`), a run of 8 samples together in VP9's.

Nine streams libvpx 1.17.0 encoded (`crates/funcore/assets/video/vp9/`: hidden altref frames in superframes, lossless, error resilient, a change of size without a key frame, pictures of 1x1, 2x2 and 33x17, three spatial layers of 80x44, 160x88 and 320x176 as a group call's member sends them (`vp9::SVC`, which the bench decodes too), tiles) decode to libvpx's pictures (`streams_match_libvpx`), the lower layers alone, back to back or under an index, to libvpx's pictures of each layer (`lower_layers_alone`), a slot's frame is shown again (`existing_frame_shown_again`), the loop filter's run of 8 samples filters as each sample by itself (`lanes_filter_as_one`), and a refused or damaged frame leaves the decoder waiting, a good key frame after it decoding exactly (`frames_refused_by_name`, `damaged_frames_return`).

## Sound

`crates/impshell/src/sound/` drives the sound card with ioctls (`pcm.rs`, `mixer.rs`), without a library; `r1_funcore::hw::sound::card` decides which controls are set, to what, and in what order. Microphone and speaker each have a thread (`microphone`, `speaker`) that stays for the next stream, one user at a time; a stream let go of while it waited for its part switches nothing on, and when neither is in use the card is off. A sound counts as waiting for the speaker from the call that asks for it, before its thread runs (`CardState::await_speaker` in `play_at`), and the main loop is told the card is off only once none waits (`sound::is_off`, `CardState::is_quiet`), so that a sound asked for on a sleeping device, Signal's notice, plays before the suspend; the speaker's end raises the card's flag, which has the main loop look again. Blocks are 1024 samples (21 ms). The kernel buffers 8 recorded blocks (171 ms) and 4 playback blocks (85 ms, `r1_funcore::hw::sound::card::periods`), which together fill the six 4096 byte blocks of the audio SRAM; a larger ring would go to DRAM and cost power while the microphone runs ([Power](../hardware/power.md#draw)). A call's speaker holds 3 playback blocks (64 ms, `card::CALL_PERIODS`, by `sound::play_heard` alone): of the 2 queued while a block is written, one outlasts what the kernel's softirqs may keep a real-time thread from its core, three of its ticks, the one bound the kernel's sources give, and the other is for what none bounds: hard and threaded interrupts, real-time threads above the speaker's, a core's exit from idle and a block's longest work. Samples the microphone thread missed are counted from the clock (`r1_funcore::hw::sound::Intake::take_lost`) and recorded as silence; an overrun that the read of the card's delay after a block meets counts as one the read met, the block read before it going into the gap. An underrun that the read of the card's delay before a played block meets counts as one the write would meet: the stream is made ready and the delay read anew, so that the block is stamped by a ring that holds nothing. A recording's side and the translator's log the samples lost (`sound: N samples of the microphone came and went unheard`, `hw::sound::to_lost_line`); a call counts its overruns in the card's line ([A call's sound](#a-calls-sound)).

| | On | Off |
|---|---|---|
| Speaker | `Lineout_PGAR_GAIN` `-2Db`, then `Speaker_Amp_Switch` `On` | `Speaker_Amp_Switch` `Off`, before the stream ends |
| Main microphone | `Audio_MIC1_Mode_Select` `DCCMODE`, `Audio_MicSource1_Setting` `ADC1`, `Audio_PGA1_Setting` `24Db`, `Audio_ADC_1_Switch` `On`, `Audio_Preamp1_Switch` `IN_ADC1` | `Audio_Preamp1_Switch` `OPEN`, `Audio_ADC_1_Switch` `Off` |

- **Recording** (`r1_funcore::hw::sound::record`): 48 kHz mono. The first 60 ms (`SETTLE_SAMPLES`, counted by `record::Settle` at the rate the microphone is read at, for a recording and for the translator's stream alike) are silence; then sound fades in over 20 ms. Each block is levelled (`r1_funcore::hw::sound::level`). Voice is a level above 56 of 32768 (four times the microphone noise at 24 dB gain) and above four times the recent minimum; a steady background is not voice. Voice is brought to 20 dB below full scale with at most 24 dB of gain: gain rises fast in the first words and slowly after, falls at once, holds through pauses, and never drives the signal above 1 dB below full scale. A file's sound starts at the time in its name: earlier samples are dropped, and a late microphone is padded with silence (`record::Lineup`). Packets are held until the microphone's first 80 ms have passed (`FADE_END`, `Recorder::is_faded_in`). A shorter recording contains no sound, so it creates no file, note or message, and a video keeps its frames without sound. Notes and conversation messages record at 64 kbit/s (`record::VOICE_BITS_PER_SECOND`), videos at 96 kbit/s (`camera::recording::SOUND_BITS_PER_SECOND`).
- **The microphone's level.** Only `sound::capture` switches the microphone on and reads it: the translator and a call's sound take its blocks as they come, `sound::record` encodes them into a file's soundtrack, a video's, and `sound::record_voice` into a file of sound alone, a note's on the partition or one in memory (`sound::Memo`) for what is said to the robot, the browser, the bell, the rundowns' list, the Pokédex or Signal. The level is the card's, not a recording's: every block's largest sample goes into the card's state (`CardState::hear`) where no other thread holds that state just then, as the microphone's thread never waits for it, and the state's level is nought again once the microphone is let go, and the card's flag tells the main loop when the steps it lights change (`sound::take_peak`, `Source::Microphone`). The interface shows it from there ([Interface](../app/interface.md#chat)).
- **Playback.** A note plays from its first packet (`play_file`, `r1_funcore::hw::sound::playback`); an unreadable packet plays as silence. A voice note plays from its bytes (`play_voice`, `MemoryFile::parse`): an MP4 file or a stream of ADTS, in whatever of the standard's rates and one channel or two a phone recorded it, no more than its edit lets be heard; a packet that does not decode plays as silence and is logged, and a file of another kind is refused (`playback::UNSUPPORTED`). Sound at another rate than the card's 48 kHz is brought to it by a polyphase filter, a sinc under a Kaiser window, 70 dB down past the lower rate's band (`r1_funcore::base::resample::Resampler`, which brings SILK's rates to 48 kHz too, [Opus](#opus)), and two channels play as their mean. A video's sound follows the video clock (`play_along`, `r1_funcore::hw::sound::pace`): it plays up to 0.2 % fast or slow (`MAX_PACE_CHANGE`) to stay on the due sample, and jumps when it is more than 60 ms off (`SEEK_SECONDS`) or after the clock stopped; a call's far sound follows its jitter buffer's plan the same way ([A call's sound](#a-calls-sound)). Live voices at 24 kHz are upsampled to 48 kHz by Catmull-Rom interpolation (`r1_funcore::hw::sound::resample::Doubler`), as the filter's 88 taps for the same rates would play each sample 43 of the model's later. The interface's own sounds play from files held whole in memory (`MemoryFile`) at their own level: the robot's searching aside (`play_aside`), taking the speaker only where it is free and no player waits for it (`r1_funcore::hw::sound::card::CardState::take_speaker_aside`) and giving it up as soon as one does (`CardState::may_keep_speaker`), once the card has played out what it holds, round and round (`Playback::fill_looped`); the bell's ring, round and round, and the notice, once, waiting for the speaker as a note does (`play_sound`).
- **End of stream.** A stream that plays to its end writes its last block and drains (`Pcm::drain`); the amplifier turns off afterwards. The speaker stream sets `silence_size` to the kernel's boundary (the ring size times the largest power of two that fits a 64 bit `long`: 2^62 for the ring of 4 blocks, 3 times 2^61 for a call's of 3) and `silence_threshold` to 0 (`r1_funcore::hw::sound::card::thresholds`, requested by `software_parameters` in `pcm.rs`). The kernel then clears only what the hardware pointer has passed, never a block a late writer has written ([Sound](../hardware/sound.md#end-of-a-stream)).
- **Volume** (`r1_funcore::hw::sound::volume`, `sound/mod.rs`): two settings, the volume for all speaker output but the interface's own sounds, and the level of those sounds ([Interface](../app/interface.md#brightness-and-volume)). `gain` maps either to a 15 bit fixed-point gain in 3 dB steps: 100 down to 0 give 32768, 23170, 16384, 11585, 8192, 5793, 4096, 2896, 2048, 1448 and 0, so 10 is 27 dB down; values between steps take the nearer step. `CardState` holds the gains (`set_volume`, called by the main loop at start and on change). `write_blocks`, the only writer of speaker blocks, scales each block after it is filled by its stream's gain, as the card's state holds it, or as it last read it where another thread holds that state just then, so that the speaker's thread never waits for it; it rounds half away from zero and does not scale at unity. The speaker stream opens only in `run_speaker`, which `play`, `play_heard`, `play_sound` and `play_aside` reach, so notes, video sound, a call's sound, the translator, reading aloud and the interface's sounds all pass through it. A change takes effect within one block plus the card's 85 ms, in a call within one block plus 64 ms, a block later where the speaker's thread finds the card's state held. The line output's gain stays as the speaker's switching sets it.

### A call's sound

`r1_funcore::hw::sound::call` is a call's sound on the microphone's thread, the speaker's and the calls' own ([Calls](../app/calls.md#the-parts)). Each sound thread works on a side of its own, `call::Microphone` or `call::Speaker`, and takes the lock of the one `call::Sound` it shares with the others only to move what passes between them: the blocks the speaker played, for the microphone's canceller; the microphone's packets, for the far side, and their buffers back; the far members' packets, for the speaker; whether the microphone is muted; and what the canceller and each member's sound tell. The lock lends its holder the priority of a sound thread that waits for it (`sys::PiMutex`), as the calls' thread runs under the drawing thread and the sound threads above it. Once their buffers have grown, the sound threads take no memory: the calls' thread hands the far side a copy of the microphone's packets, made outside the lock, and gives theirs back, whose buffers the microphone's side codes the next packets into (`Sound::take_packets`, `Sound::give_back`). The microphone's blocks come through `sound::capture` at 48 kHz (`call::RATE`), and the speaker's go out through `sound::play_heard`, which hands back each block as the card takes it, at its volume, and each underrun (`sound::Played`). Both run at once, each on its pool's thread, while the call is talked in, real time above the drawing thread ([System](system.md#the-app)).

- **Stamps.** Each sound thread reads its clock as it is handed its block, beside the card's delay: a recorded block is stamped by that clock less the block and the samples recorded after it that wait to be read (`Capture::Samples`), a played block by that clock and the samples the card plays before its first (`Speaker::exchange`). However late a thread comes to its block, its stamps stay where the card's samples are.
- **Out** (`Microphone::take`, `Microphone::record`, `Microphone::give`). The blocks the speaker played since the last are taken from the call's sound for the canceller; each recorded block is cleared of them (the canceller, below), levelled (`hw::sound::level`, whose `Leveller::level` also tells whether a block holds voice), and every 2880 samples, 60 ms, encoded into one Opus packet of 240 bytes, 32 kbit/s, three CELT frames of 20 ms at wideband, as the canceller lets nothing above wideband through ([Opus](#opus)); the packets wait in the call's sound for the calls' thread. A packet carries the time of its first sample, counted from the first recorded, its level in decibels under full scale (RFC 6464, 3: 0 to 127, 127 for silence) and whether it held voice. While the microphone is muted nothing is encoded, and the far side hears a pause; samples the microphone missed pass on the far side's clock the same way (`Microphone::lose`).
- **In** (`Sound::push`, `Speaker::exchange`, `Speaker::play`). The calls' thread leaves each far member's packets in the call's sound, where one that waits longer than a plan's most delay, 500 ms, goes (`jitter::is_stale`). The speaker's side takes them as it fills its next block, each into a jitter buffer of its member's own (below), made at its first packet; each buffer fills one block, levelled by a leveller of its own, and their sum, held to what a sample can be, plays (`jitter::mix`). The block as the card plays it goes back into the call's sound for the microphone's side (`Sound::hear`), no further behind the newest than the canceller reaches (`echo::RING`).
- **The log.** As the sound stops, the calls' thread logs the canceller's line, the card's and a line for each far member (`Sound::to_lines`): `echo: converged` or `learning`, then `lag X ms` or `no lag found`, `3 dB under the microphone in N % of hops of far sound`, `the echo N dB against the microphone` or `no echo measured`, the decibels the filter and the suppressor took off, the hops of far sound and the realignments; `sound: card: N underruns of the speaker, N overruns of the microphone losing N samples, the speaker's ring X ms at the least and Y ms on average` (or `no block asked for`), the ring as the speaker's side found it at each block (`Speaker::exchange`); and `sound: member N:` with the packets late and recovered, the samples made up and waited for, the restarts and the delay.

**The echo canceller** (`r1_funcore::hw::sound::echo::Canceller`) works at 48 kHz in hops of 256 samples, 5.3 ms, a quarter of the card's block, on blocks stamped by the card's sample clock, and gives each recorded block back a hop late:

- The filter is a multidelay block frequency-domain adaptive filter (Soo and Pang, 1990) of 6 partitions of a hop (`PARTITIONS`): the 16 ms an echo of the case and the hand about it is assumed to last (`CASE`), and twice a stamp's error either way, a tick of the kernel's scheduler at 250 Hz each (`STAMP_ERROR`, `SKEW`). The speaker's and the microphone's blocks are placed by their counts of samples while their stamps lie within that, else at the stamp, and the filter is realigned.
- The filter's reach stands at the echo's lag behind the stamps, whatever the card's paths add to them, which a search finds as AEC3's delay estimator finds one in outline (`echo::Lag`): both sides at 6 kHz, an eighth of the card's rate, the far side read a skew ahead of the stamps; each time half of the last 341 ms has come anew, 171 ms, and only where the far side's newer half sounds loud enough for its echo to stand over the microphone's noise, the microphone's newer half is correlated with the far side's whole by the phase transform (Knapp and Carter, 1976), both frames tapered over a hop at each end by a raised cosine, and weighed a third into a running mean. The mean's peak is a search's lag where it stands over four times the mean's size and no lag more than a hop from it stands over that and over half the peak, as a far side whose period is longer than the skew gives a second peak; no lag within a hop of either end of the range is read, where the frames' ends make peaks of their own, so that a lag lies from a hop to 165 ms behind the far side as the search reads it (`MOST_LAG`). A lag is taken once five searches in a row put theirs within a hop of each other, the middle of them; where it lies more than a skew from where the reach stands, the reach moves to it and both models start anew. The far side the canceller keeps (`echo::RING`) reaches over what a call's speaker holds and a block, what the microphone holds, a skew, the farthest lag and the filter's reach, to the next power of two. Of the hops of far sound, those whose error stands 3 dB under a microphone of 50 of 32768 a sample or more count as converged (AEC3's `kConvergenceThreshold`), and the echo's estimate is weighed against the microphone over them, for the log.
- Two models of the echo's path (Ochiai, Araseki and Ogihara, 1977): one learns, the other's estimate is taken out, and each takes the other's weights where its error shows worse over about 85 ms (`COMPARING`), by Speex's test. A bin steps a quarter of the residual echo's share of its error (`STEP_SHARE`), the leak estimated as Valin (2007) does, at most a half (`MAX_STEP`); the leak's averages follow the far side's speech within 2 s, as Speex's do, so that near speech while both talk does not move them.
- A suppressor of the echo left, a gain for each bin over frames of two hops under a root of a Hann window: the echo left taken 9 dB over its estimate (`OVER`), falling by no more than 3 dB a hop (`HOLD`), the share of near sound smoothed as Ephraim and Malah (1984) smooth it (`SMOOTHING`), the gain never under 40 dB down, as Speex's (`FLOOR`). Bins from 94 Hz to 7.97 kHz pass; the rest are zeroed.
- The card's own figures are constants measured on it: the microphone's noise at 24 dB of preamp, -67.4 dBFS (`NOISE`), the echo's share of the played power, -15 dB at 12 dB of preamp (`COUPLING`), and the speaker's harmonics, 50 dB down (`MIN_LEAK`).

**The jitter buffer** (`r1_funcore::hw::sound::jitter::Stream`) holds one far member's Opus packets, decodes them and keeps them to the speaker's clock, its delay reckoned as libwebrtc's NetEq reckons it in outline:

- A packet's transit is the samples between its arrival on the calls' clock and its timestamp. The latest of each 500 ms goes into a histogram of a hundred buckets of 20 ms, measured from the soonest transit of the 2 s before or after it, whichever is later, so that a route that changes is no delay; each span keeps 0.983 of what was. The delay aimed at is the 95 % quantile, or the latest of the last 2 s where that is later, and never more than 500 ms over the soonest (RingRTC's `max_target_delay_ms`). The plan moves toward it at half the pace's range, 0.1 % (`PLAN_RATE`), and `pace::Follower` plays the far side's clock up to 0.2 % fast or slow, leaping past 60 ms off.
- A stream holds 50 packets at the most (RingRTC's `max_packets`), none over 7650 bytes, 120 ms at Opus's highest rate. A packet whose sound has played counts late and goes; packets that fit no plan, more than 64 behind the next or their transit more than 500 ms off the last, restart the stream once they keep coming for half a second. A restart begins the stream anew but keeps its buffers and its decoder, reset (`opus::Decoder::reset`).
- A lost packet is made from the copy the packet after it carries (in-band FEC, `opus::Decoder::recover`) where it carries one, else concealed by the decoder, 10 ms at a time; while room is left the far clock stands, so that a lost packet that comes late still plays. A far side that is silent (packets of 2 bytes or less) is filled by the decoder's concealment, and its silence cut where the delay is to shrink. A stream nothing came of for the 2 s its arrivals are kept rests (`Rooms::is_resting`): its samples are nought and its decoder is left alone until a packet comes, so that a member who left, or whose silence the calling server drops, costs the speaker's thread no concealment and adds no noise to the sum.
- Where the delay is to shrink, sound is accelerated too, as NetEq accelerates it (`jitter::accelerate`, `accelerate.cc`, `time_stretch.cc`): while the plan's delay beyond the soonest packet's stands at or over its limit, read at each try as it stands, the pull's as the block's cuts and waits have moved it since, the latest transit of the last 2 s with the spread and 20 ms over it (`Timing::limit`, NetEq's high limit), a pitch period is taken out of the sound that plays next, the period before it faded into the one after, never more than the plan holds over its aim. The period is the lag of the strongest correlation of the next 30 ms with itself, searched at 4 kHz among lags of 2.5 to 15 ms; it goes where the periods on either side of the middle correlate above 0.9, or where the sound holds no speech. One try is made each 10 ms of sound, none for 50 ms after one that took a period, but while the delay stands four times over its limit, when each try takes as many periods as 15 ms holds where the two sides correlate above 0.5.
- `jitter::Stats` counts what the log line tells; what was lost on the way and the jitter are the RTP receiver's ([Calls](../app/calls.md#media)).
