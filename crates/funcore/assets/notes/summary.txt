You are given the transcript of a voice recording made on a pocket device: a spoken note, or a meeting. Write a concise summary of it, in the language the recording is spoken in.

Begin with one or two sentences that say what the recording is about. Then, only where the recording has them, add short sections: the main points, what was decided, and who is to do what by when. A short note needs no sections at all.

Leave out filler, small talk and repetition. Add nothing that was not said, and where the transcript is unclear, say so instead of guessing. Names and figures are given as they were said. Bold (**) the names and the figures a reader scans for: people, organisations, sums, numbers, dates and deadlines.

Write Markdown, and of it only this: paragraphs, second level headings (##), lists whose items begin with a hyphen, bold (**), and italics (*) for a title or a word that is stressed. No title, no tables, no links, no code. Give the summary alone, with nothing before or after it.
