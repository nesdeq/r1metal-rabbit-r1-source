Turn this photograph into an original piece of old school pixel art, the way a game artist of the 16-bit era would have drawn this very scene for an adventure game.

It is a drawing, not a filtered photograph, and it is a drawing of this scene and of no other. Keep what the photograph shows: every main thing, where it stands, what it is and what it is made of, the place, the time of day and the light. Night stays night and a dark room stays dark; metal stays metal, plastic stays plastic, glass stays glass, and nothing turns into wood that is not wood. Add nothing the photograph does not show: no plants, flowers, furniture, decoration, animals, people, windows, sun or sky. Within that, redraw freely: simplify, stylise and exaggerate. Reduce every thing to its bold, readable shape. Leave out fine texture, grain and small clutter. Give the main subject charm and character, as a sprite in a SNES or Amiga game has it: clean silhouette, rounded friendly forms, slightly bigger eyes and features. Draw the surroundings that are there as tidy game scenery, with a few big shapes and repeating tile-like patterns.

Rendering: hand-placed pixels on a coarse square grid, as if drawn at roughly 128x128 logical pixels and displayed large. Crisp square pixels of one uniform size. Clean clusters of flat colour, hard edges, dark outlines around the main shapes, a few deliberate highlights. Ordered checkerboard dithering only where a gradient is needed, and sparingly. No photographic detail, no noise, no blur, no smooth gradients, no soft anti-aliased edges, no 3D gloss.

Palette: 16 to 24 colours in all, warm, cozy and vibrant. That is a matter of colour alone, never of what the picture shows. Start from the colours the scene has, so that every thing keeps a colour one knows it by, then make them clear and saturated and lean them to the warm side: cream and amber in the lights, deep warm brown or ink blue in the darks instead of grey and black. Keep the scene as bright or as dark as it is: a night scene stays dark and takes its warmth from the lights that are lit in it. Several distinct colour families side by side where the scene has them, never one hue all over. Shift hue between light and shadow instead of only darkening.

Never render text, letters, numerals, UI elements, borders, frames, logos or watermarks. Fill the whole square.
